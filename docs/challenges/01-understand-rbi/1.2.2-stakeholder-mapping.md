## Filled prompt

```text
I am working on this innovation challenge: "How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process? Working goal: How can we give mass-affluent customers useful, affordable financial guidance that helps them achieve their goals and strengthens their relationship with the bank? Company: Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), network banks in CEE (Serbia, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania...). Our team is in Budapest 13.–16.10.2026 and will do street interviews there. Market and customer group are NOT decided yet."

Help me create a comprehensive stakeholder map:

1. PRIMARY STAKEHOLDERS (Directly Affected)
   - Who are the primary users, customers, or beneficiaries affected by this challenge?
   - What segments or groups exist within them?
   - What does each group care about most?

2. INTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS
   - Which departments or teams will be involved or affected?
   - Suggest typical departments based on my challenge type (e.g., for operations: Operations, IT, Finance, Quality; for customer-facing: Customer Service, Sales, Marketing, Product)
   - What are their typical concerns and priorities?
   - Who needs to approve or support this initiative?
   - Who might resist this change and why?

3. EXTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS
   - Which external parties are affected or have influence?
   - Examples: Vendors/partners, Customers, Regulators, Industry bodies, Service providers
   - What influence or constraints do they create?

4. HIDDEN/OFTEN OVERLOOKED STAKEHOLDERS
   - Who might be indirectly affected but often forgotten?
   - Internal: Training teams, Change management, Communications, Business continuity
   - External: End users who aren't direct customers, Community groups, Adjacent teams
   - What concerns might they have?

For each stakeholder group, analyze:
- Their PRIMARY INTERESTS in this initiative
- Their CONCERNS or potential objections
- Their INFLUENCE level (High/Medium/Low)
- Their INTEREST level in this project (High/Medium/Low)
- ENGAGEMENT APPROACH (how to involve them)
- SUCCESS CRITERIA (what would make them happy)

Create a stakeholder matrix:
- High Influence + High Interest = Manage Closely
- High Influence + Low Interest = Keep Satisfied
- Low Influence + High Interest = Keep Informed
- Low Influence + Low Interest = Monitor

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Detailed stakeholder table with analysis, plus a prioritization matrix showing how to engage each group.
```

## Output

### 1. Primary stakeholders (directly affected)

The customers. Within the mass-affluent group there are several sub-groups that care about different things. These match the customer-group options in [1.2.3](1.2.3-scope-definition.md).

| Sub-group | Who they are | What they care about most |
|---|---|---|
| **P1. Savers without a plan** | Good income, savings mostly in deposits, no investment plan | Safety, understanding the options, not making a mistake, knowing how much cash to keep |
| **P2. Investors without follow-up** | Invested once (often a fund), no regular review | "Am I on track?", someone to call when markets fall, clear costs |
| **P3. Unrecognised mass affluent** | Similar income/savings but not Premium | Being noticed, getting relevant offers instead of generic ones |
| **P4. Premium customers investing elsewhere** | Salary at the bank, investments at a fintech, broker or state securities | Better returns or lower fees, easy apps, trust in the provider |
| **P5. Young / emerging affluent** | 25–40, growing income, digital-first | Doing things in the app, quick answers, learning, goals like a first flat |
| **P6. Older customers near retirement** | 50+, larger savings, lower risk appetite | Keeping capital safe, income in retirement, a known person to talk to |

### 2. Internal stakeholders

**Typical departments for a customer-facing challenge like ours:** Premium / affluent segment management, retail branch network, investment and wealth products, digital channels, contact centre, compliance and legal, risk, data and analytics, marketing, HR and training, finance.

- **Who needs to approve or support:** the segment owner (Premium / affluent head) for any change to the service promise; compliance for any wording that could look like advice; the digital product owner for app changes. In Innovation School, the RBHU CEO session on 15.10.2026 and the coaches are where support matters first.
- **Who might resist, and why:**
  - *Branch and Premium bankers*, if a digital tool looks like it replaces them or adds new work.
  - *Compliance*, if guidance seems to cross into unregulated advice.
  - *Investment product team*, if goal-based guidance often ends with "keep cash" and lowers fund sales.
  - *IT / digital*, if the idea needs integrations they cannot deliver soon.

### 3. External stakeholders

| Party | Influence or constraint |
|---|---|
| **EU regulators (ESMA) and national supervisors** (Hungary: MNB; Czech Republic: CNB; Serbia: NBS / Securities Commission) | Set suitability, appropriateness, product-governance and consumer rules. Serbia is outside the EU. **National supervisor roles require verification** |
| **Data protection authorities (GDPR)** | Limit how transaction data can be used for targeting and personalisation |
| **Fund managers and partners** (for example Raiffeisen's asset-management companies, platform vendors) | Provide products and tools; their product range limits what a bank can recommend |
| **Fintechs and competitors** (robo-advisers, brokers, neobanks, other banks' premium offers) | Set customer expectations on ease and price; destination for hidden defection |
| **State retail securities** (for example Hungarian retail government bonds) | Strong local alternative for savers. **Local relevance requires verification** |
| **Consumer groups, financial-education organisations** | Shape public trust; possible partners for education content |

### 4. Hidden / often overlooked stakeholders

| Group | Why forgotten | Likely concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner, spouse, family** | Advice is given to one person, but decisions are often shared | Being left out; plans that ignore the household |
| **Customers who have already left** (moved money elsewhere) | They are no longer visible in the data | Why stay or come back? Best source of honest reasons |
| **Contact centre agents** | Seen as service, not advice | Customers ask investment questions they are not allowed or trained to answer |
| **Training / HR** | Change is assumed to "just happen" | Bankers need new skills (explaining, coaching, using new tools) |
| **Communications / complaints team** | Only involved after problems | Misunderstood fees or losses become complaints |
| **Internal audit / risk** | Comes in late | Unclear accountability for automated recommendations |
| **Back-office operations** | Paperwork happens behind the scenes | New flows must still produce correct contracts and records |
| **Other RBI network banks** | Our work looks like a local project | Re-use: could they adopt it? Czech robo team might share lessons |
| **Our own team's line managers** | Not part of the challenge | Time spent; learning transfer by 15.12.2026 |

## What this means for our challenge

- **H1 personalisation:** customers and Premium bankers are in "manage closely". We need both sides of the same advice conversation, so plan at least one banker conversation alongside street interviews.
- **H2 knowledge and confidence:** the partner/family and contact centre are often forgotten. Customers may learn and decide with them. Ask "Who do you talk to before deciding?"
- **H3 hybrid channels:** the digital/app team has high influence but medium interest. Show them a small, concrete handover improvement rather than a large new app.
- **H4 advisor data and tools:** bankers may resist if new tools add work. Frame any idea as "less admin, more time to explain", and measure preparation time.
- **Compliance early:** it can stop an idea late. A quick check of the guidance vs. advice line before prototype testing protects the pitch.
