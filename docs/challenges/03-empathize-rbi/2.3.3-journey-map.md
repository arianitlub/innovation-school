# 2.3.3 Current-State Journey Map — mass-affluent investment guidance

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt **2.3.3**, run 14.10.2026 with skill `innovation-empathize`. **Updated 14.10.2026** with two street interviews (C1, C2) and the UX research summary. Inputs: [2.3.1](2.3.1-interview-pattern-analysis.md), [2.3.2](2.3.2-personas.md).

> **Scope:** a cross-market current-state map. Bank-side steps come from 2 experts (Hungary, Serbia). Customer thoughts and emotions come from **2 street interviews** and the UX study. It is a first "as-is", to validate with more customers. Main persona: **Self-Directed Comparer (C1)**; Cautious Branch Saver (C2) shown where different.

---

## 1. Journey stages

1. **Trigger** — "I have savings; should I do something?"
2. **Search for information** — whom do I ask?
3. **Contact with the bank** — offer or conversation
4. **Profile and suitability** — MiFID questionnaire
5. **Decide and invest**
6. **Get help when unsure**
7. **Follow-up / review**
8. **Exit or stay** *(new stage, from C1)*

## 4. Journey variants

- **C1, Self-Directed Comparer:** digital path, leaves the bank over cost and push.
- **C2, Cautious Branch Saver:** stops at stage 3. Rejects phone and online offers; would only continue in the branch or on their own initiative.
- **Common point:** both break at **stage 3 (bank contact)**.

## 5. Current vs future state

This map is the **as-is**. The future state belongs to Ideate. Direction: bank contact **started by the customer**, **personal and clear**, **cheaper**, with **help on demand**.

## 8. Insights

- The journey breaks at **first contact** and at **the exit**, not in the transaction.
- **Control over contact** runs through every stage.
- The customer's **real adviser today is family or ChatGPT**.

## 9. Cross-channel breaks

- Adviser availability forced C1 to switch to digital: context and relationship were lost.
- Phone and ads do not lead to the branch, where C2 would trust the bank.
- Not yet known: whether the app passes context to RaiConnect or a banker.

## 10. Narrative (Self-Directed Comparer, from C1)

A few years ago, C1's nephew told them ETFs were worth exploring. C1 opened an investment account at their bank and started putting money aside. They had enough information, but it wasn't clear, and the conversation felt like a product push: it covered the investment questionnaire, not their plans for the near future. Seeing an adviser meant fitting their life around the adviser's diary.

Then C1 noticed the account cost "a lot more" than they expected. This year they cancelled it. The bank asked why; C1 said it was too expensive, and that was the end of it. No other option was offered.

Today C1 invests long-term and regularly online, in options their nephew sent and that they checked against where they live. When new money comes in, they upload their fund list to ChatGPT and ask what to focus on. They say they would share their life situation with a bank that used it to make guidance personal and cheaper. Right now, the bank isn't in that conversation.

*(A second street interviewee, C2, never reached the decision stage: they reject phone and online offers and would trust only a branch conversation they started themselves.)*

## Journey-map takeaway

> **First contact on the bank's terms → product push and unclear, costly offers → customer decides elsewhere (family, ChatGPT, cheaper provider) → bank loses the relationship at exit**
