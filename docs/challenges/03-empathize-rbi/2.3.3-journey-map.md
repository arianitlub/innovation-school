# 2.3.3 Current-State Journey Map — mass-affluent investment guidance

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt **2.3.3**, run 14.10.2026 with skill `innovation-empathize` after [2.3.1 pattern analysis](2.3.1-interview-pattern-analysis.md) and [2.3.2 provisional archetypes](2.3.2-personas.md).

> **Scope and limitation:** this is a **cross-market current-state synthesis** from two expert interviews, not a directly observed end-to-end customer journey. Hungary and Serbia have different service models. Customer thoughts/emotions are marked unknown unless the interviews support them. The journey should be validated with customers before it is treated as the definitive “as-is”.

---

## 1. Journey stages

1. **Recognised / becomes eligible**
2. **Chooses or enters a service path**
3. **Learns and explores options**
4. **Completes profile / suitability steps**
5. **Decides and invests**
6. **Gets help when needed**
7. **Receives follow-up / reviews progress**

## 3. Two current-state paths visible in the evidence

### Path A — Self-service / non-advisory mass-affluent path (clearest in Hungary)

**Eligibility/savings → digital information → MiFID/profile → choose available investment → execute digitally → follow-up/support unclear**

What works:
- fast digital execution;
- securities-account opening and profiling can be digital;
- education content exists.

Main uncertainty/gap:
- what happens when the customer needs **personal guidance rather than product information**, but is not in full Premium advisory service?

### Path B — Premium / advised path (Hungary + Serbia)

**Eligibility/relationship → banker contact → goals/risk/horizon discussion → advisory tool/video → recommendation/decision → periodic relationship support**

What works:
- human expertise;
- more personal context;
- remote/video interaction;
- established advisory tooling.

Main implication:
- this path demonstrates that the bank already has advisory capability. The innovation opportunity may be how to make an **appropriate level of guidance accessible without reproducing the entire Premium model**.

---

## 4. Moments of truth

### Moment of truth 1 — “Do I understand enough to act?”

The transaction can be easy while the decision remains difficult. Hungary evidence specifically points to fear of loss and lack of knowledge.

**Priority: High**

### Moment of truth 2 — “I have a personal question; who can help me?”

This is the core gap emerging between self-service and full advisory support.

**Priority: High**

### Moment of truth 3 — “Do I want a full Premium relationship to get this help?”

Some eligible customers do not use Premium/Private service. Why is still unknown.

**Priority: High — evidence gap**

### Moment of truth 4 — “What happens after I invest?”

Premium follow-up is visible in Hungary; the non-Premium path is unclear.

**Priority: Medium — evidence gap**

## 6. Cross-channel questions still open

The interviews do **not** establish whether:

- an adviser sees the customer's completed digital profile/journey when the customer asks for help;
- the customer has to repeat goals/questions when switching channel;
- non-Premium customers can get an appropriate one-off human conversation;
- follow-up context survives across app, video, phone and branch.

These are important tests because the strongest concept direction is a **bridge** between existing self-service and existing human expertise.

---

## 7. Improvement opportunities — not selected solutions

### Near-term / small experiments

- Make the next available support option clearer at moments of uncertainty.
- Test whether a short goal/risk summary can travel from digital self-service to a human expert.
- Test education embedded beside a decision rather than only in a separate learning area.
- Test permission-based “Would you like help?” prompts instead of unsolicited outreach.
- Test a lightweight review/check-in for customers without dedicated relationship managers.

### Longer-term questions

- Whether a new service layer is needed between Mass and full Premium advisory.
- Whether shared adviser pools, digital guidance or AI-assisted explanation can scale that layer while respecting regulatory boundaries.
- How to connect existing tools rather than duplicate them.

## 8. Evidence-based narrative

A mass-affluent customer may already be visible to the bank through salary, deposits or eligibility rules. If they opt into a higher service tier, there is evidence that a stronger advisory journey can exist: a manager, video interaction, profiling, an advisory tool and personalised discussion. If they remain outside that service model, they can still have strong digital execution capability, including investment information, suitability steps and a fast transaction.

The emerging problem sits **between those two paths**. A digitally capable customer may not need a full relationship manager, but at some point they may face a question that generic information does not resolve: “Is this suitable for my goal?”, “How much risk am I taking?”, or “What should I compare?” Hungary evidence suggests that many customers value self-direction but still want a person available when needed. At the same time, they may not welcome repeated unsolicited contact.

This means the current-state problem is less about the absence of products or technology and more about **access to the right depth of guidance at the right moment**. The bank already has segmentation, digital execution, education, video and advisory capabilities. The next design step should explore how these capabilities could serve customers who fall between pure self-service and full Premium advice, without assuming that every customer wants the same level of human involvement.

This narrative is a synthesis of expert evidence, not a directly observed customer story.

---

## 9. Journey-map takeaway

The biggest design opportunity is at the transition:

> **Self-service information → uncertainty/question → appropriate guidance → confident next step**

That transition becomes the main input to the HMW question in prompt 2.3.4.
