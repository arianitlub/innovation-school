# 2.3.1 Interview Pattern Analysis — two expert interviews

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt **2.3.1**, run 14.10.2026 with skill `innovation-empathize` using the evidence in [2.3.0-expert-interview-evidence.md](2.3.0-expert-interview-evidence.md).

> **Data-quality warning:** this synthesis uses two real internal expert interviews (Hungary and Serbia), supplied as AI-generated meeting summaries. They are not verbatim transcripts and they are not customer interviews. Counts below are therefore **expert-signal counts only**. With `n=2`, percentages are descriptive, not statistically meaningful.

---

## 1. Pattern clustering — needs and pain points

### Theme A — A service gap between self-service and full advisory support

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.**

- **E-HU-1:** mass-affluent customers can buy investment products but are served on a non-advisory basis; personalised advice is mainly Premium/Private.
- **E-RS-1:** personalised advice exists for affluent/Premium clients through a manager, video meeting and advisory tool, but some eligible clients choose not to use Premium/Private service.

**Explicit need:** access to an appropriate level of guidance, not necessarily a full private-banking relationship.  
**Implicit need:** a path for customers who have more money/savings than ordinary retail customers but are not using a dedicated-adviser model.  
**Latent opportunity:** bridge the space between basic self-service information and high-touch advisory service.

### Theme B — Customers need confidence and understandable investment information

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.**

- Hungary: fear of losing money and insufficient knowledge were named as engagement barriers; education initiatives already exist.
- Serbia: advisers spend part of the advisory conversation explaining investment concepts and expected outcomes using brochures and tool-supported information.

**Need:** understandable guidance around risk, horizon, goals and investment choices.  
**Important correction:** the problem is not simply “there is no education.” Education already exists; the question is whether it reaches the right customer at the right moment and helps them act confidently.

### Theme C — Customers want autonomy plus human help when it matters

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.**

- Hungary: expert-reported customer research says many customers want to invest independently in the app but be able to reach a person for questions; repeated unsolicited calls/emails are not preferred.
- Serbia: current advised journey already combines video conversation, a human adviser and a shared advisory tool.

**Need:** control and convenience without losing access to expertise.  
**Potential friction:** the service model can become either too self-service or too relationship-manager-heavy.

### Theme D — The bank already has much of the underlying capability

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.**

- Hungary already has a 4–6 minute digital investment journey, MiFID profiling, digital documentation, RaiConnect and education initiatives.
- Serbia already has an adviser-facing advisory tool, video meetings, customer profiling and goal/risk discussions.

**Learning:** a new standalone advisory tool is not the obvious answer. The stronger opportunity may be **access, orchestration and handover across existing capabilities**.

### Theme E — Segmentation and outreach exist, but engagement is not automatic

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.**

- Both markets use financial conditions/signals to identify customers for higher service tiers.
- Serbia proactively contacts prospects; Hungary has a structured route from Mass to Premium/Private.
- Yet some eligible customers do not use Premium, and Hungary research suggests customers may dislike unsolicited outreach.

**Need:** engagement that feels timely and relevant rather than simply more outbound sales contact.

### Theme F — Market alternatives can be more attractive than bank investment products

**Evidence: 1/2 experts.**

- Hungary: government bonds were described as a strong competitor because of perceived safety and favourable fee/tax treatment.

This is a meaningful market factor, but it is **not yet a cross-market pattern**.

## 3. Emotional intensity

There is not enough direct customer-language data for reliable emotional scoring.

One clear signal from Hungary is **fear of losing money**, which points to anxiety around investment decisions. Beyond that, emotional intensity should be left **unknown** until customer interviews provide direct language and stories.

---

## 4. Segmentation patterns

The strongest segmentation is behavioural/service-based rather than demographic:

1. **Self-directed mass-affluent customer outside full advisory service** — can use digital investment capability but may lack personalised guidance.
2. **Guidance-seeking/new-to-investing saver** — needs confidence, explanation and a safe next step.
3. **Advised Premium client** — opts into/qualifies for a higher-touch relationship and can access personalised advice.
4. **Eligible-but-not-engaged customer** — meets financial criteria but does not choose Premium/Private or does not respond to proactive outreach.

These are **provisional archetypes**, not validated customer personas.

---

## 5. Behavioural patterns

- Customers can enter the journey either **proactively through the bank** or **by initiating it themselves**.
- Digital execution can already be very fast; therefore, the main friction may not be transaction speed.
- Customers appear to value **independence for simple actions** and **expert support for uncertainty/important decisions**.
- Advice in higher tiers is goal/risk/horizon based; lower-tier customers may only have product access and information.
- Existing education and advisory tools mean the design challenge is more about **when, for whom and how support is available** than inventing a completely new capability.

| **H4 — Advisers lack enough data/tools to personalise efficiently** | Both markets already have substantial tools/capabilities; neither interview identified missing tools as the central blocker | **CONTRADICTED AS WRITTEN** | Replace with: “Even where advisory tools exist, service coverage, eligibility, capacity or handover design may limit access to personalised guidance.” This new hypothesis still needs testing |

### Hypothesis-check takeaway

The largest shift is from **“we need a better advisory tool”** to **“we may need a better service bridge between self-service and full Premium advisory.”**

---

## 7. Unexpected insights / surprises

1. **The digital investment transaction itself can already be extremely short** in Hungary. A long process is therefore not automatically the main pain point.
2. **Personalised advisory capability already exists.** The opportunity is not a blank-sheet robo-adviser.
3. **The segmentation engine is not the obvious problem.** Both banks already identify financially attractive customers.
4. **More outreach may be the wrong answer.** Hungary's reported customer research suggests many people want control and human help on demand, not repeated calls/emails.
5. **H1 needs segmentation.** “Mass affluent” and “Premium” overlap, and experiences differ sharply depending on whether the customer is in an advised service model.

---

## 8. Root-cause view

### Why might a mass-affluent customer remain under-supported?

Because personalised advice is linked to a higher service tier → not every eligible or relevant customer uses that tier → self-service can give product access but not the same level of guidance → customers who need more confidence may have no obvious middle path.

### Why might customers hesitate even when investing is technically easy?

Because speed of execution does not remove uncertainty about risk, suitability, goals and alternatives → information/education exists, but the customer may still want reassurance at the decision moment.

## 9. Confidence and data quality

**Strong expert signals (2/2):** existing segmentation, existing advisory capability, education importance, hybrid/digital-human pattern.  
**Moderate/market-specific signal:** government-bond competition, follow-up cadence.  
**Not established:** customer emotions beyond fear of loss, real drop-offs, why customers decline Premium, actual handover quality, customer willingness to use new guidance, business impact.

Because there are only two experts and no direct customer interviews in this synthesis, **none of the original customer pass marks can be claimed as achieved**.

---

## 10. Implications for solution design

### Prioritise

- A clear bridge between **self-service investing** and **full adviser-led Premium service**.
- Education/explanation embedded at the moment of decision, not only as generic content.
- Easy access to human expertise when uncertainty becomes high.
- Reuse of existing profiling, digital execution, video and adviser capabilities.
- A service model that respects customer autonomy and avoids unnecessary outbound contact.

### Do not assume

- that customers need another standalone advisory tool;
- that all mass-affluent customers have the same needs;
- that education alone solves the problem;
- that the transaction journey is the main friction;
- that increasing sales/investment is the customer problem.

### Next evidence to collect

1. Why do eligible customers choose not to use Premium/advisory service?
2. At which exact moments do self-directed customers want help?
3. What do non-Premium mass-affluent customers do when they are unsure?
4. Does app-to-human handover preserve context?
5. What happens after investment for customers without a dedicated relationship manager?

These findings feed the provisional personas, current-state journey and HMW question in prompts 2.3.2–2.3.4.
