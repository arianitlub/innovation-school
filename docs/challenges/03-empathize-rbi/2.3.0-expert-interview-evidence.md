## E-HU-1 — Hungary expert

### Customer segmentation and Premium movement

- Raiffeisen Hungary has been working on a project to move eligible customers from Mass into Premium or Private service models.
- Customers can move after meeting eligibility conditions and choosing/opening the relevant account; movement is not simply automatic.
- The expert reported approximate Premium thresholds around **EUR 1,100 monthly income** for entry-level Premium and **EUR 1,900 monthly income** for a higher Premium service level. The higher service level includes a dedicated relationship manager. An approximate asset threshold of **EUR 27,000** was also mentioned. These values require verification.

### Advice access

- The expert said **mass-affluent customers are currently served on a non-advisory basis** in Hungary.
- They can purchase most investment products themselves, but personalised investment advice is available mainly in **Premium and Private** service models.
- No general digital-advisory solution for the mass-affluent/non-Premium path was reported as currently available.

### Digital investment journey

- A recurring-investment journey is available through the **My Life mobile application**.
- A customer without a securities account can reportedly open one, complete the MiFID questionnaire, choose from three investment options and complete documentation digitally.
- The expert described the end-to-end journey as taking roughly **4–6 minutes**.
- A previous limitation around moving money between the current account and securities account had recently been removed, improving the digital flow.

### Human and video support

- Premium customers with dedicated service can receive relationship-manager contact and investment advice.
- The expert reported that relationship managers are expected to contact these customers roughly **two or three times per year**.
- **RaiConnect** video meetings support remote discussion, documentation and digital signing. The expert reported that video meetings account for a meaningful share of Hungarian investment transaction volume.
- The **RAIA** virtual assistant is currently basic, but the expert saw a possible future role for immediate investment information and connection to a human relationship manager when needed.

### Education and confidence

- The expert said customer engagement is limited by **fear of losing money and insufficient investment knowledge**.
- A **Learn to Invest** education module was expected to launch in Hungary, and the bank also has an investment podcast.
- This suggests that education is already an active initiative; our challenge should not assume that education content does not exist.

### Competition and engagement

- Hungarian government bonds were described as a strong alternative because customers see them as safe and because of their fee/tax position relative to bank investments.
- The expert referred to customer research indicating that many customers prefer to **make investment decisions independently in the mobile app while still being able to reach a person when questions arise**.
- The same research reportedly showed that these customers generally do **not** want repeated unsolicited calls or emails.

## E-RS-1 — Serbia expert

### Segmentation and acquisition

- The expert said customers earning more than approximately **RSD 150,000 per month** generally had a manager, while some eligible customers still chose not to use Premium or Private banking services. The threshold is interview-reported and requires verification.
- The bank uses signals such as **salary and deposit balances** to identify prospective Premium customers and contact them.

### Personalised advisory process

- Affluent/advised customers can be served through proactive or customer-initiated contact, commonly followed by a **video meeting**.
- Advisers use an **advisory tool** during the meeting and share the screen with the customer.
- The process covers the customer's wishes, future plans, risk tolerance, investment horizon and whether the priority is protecting or growing money.
- The adviser then discusses an appropriate low-, medium- or high-risk fund direction.
- An active client profile is required before the advisory session.

### Premium value proposition

- Premium clients were described as receiving a dedicated bank contact, reducing the need for branch visits, together with additional service benefits.
- Some benefit details in the AI-generated meeting notes appear ambiguous and should not be repeated as verified facts without checking the source.

### Education

- Advisers use brochures and explanation during the meeting to help customers understand investment concepts and expected outcomes.
- The advisory tool can show historical fund performance, while recommendations are framed around customer objectives such as saving, seeking returns or protecting money from inflation.

### Country-specific fund/tax points

- The expert described Serbian tax incentives and holding-period rules for certain alternative funds.
- These statements are useful context for Serbia but are **not used as cross-market evidence for the main problem framing** unless separately verified.

- Whether customers themselves describe the problem as lack of personalisation, lack of knowledge, lack of trust, poor timing, or something else.

- Which exact moments require a human adviser versus self-service.
- Whether context is preserved when a customer moves from app to human support.
- Conversion, satisfaction, abandonment, AUM or retention outcomes.

These gaps are carried into the synthesis outputs rather than filled with assumptions.

## UX — internal investment-education research (secondary customer evidence)

Summary of an internal presentation, not raw transcripts. **Not confirmed as mass-affluent participants.**

- **Serbian study:** 8 qualitative interviews (2025) with regular savers who were **not yet investing**, low-to-medium investing confidence, medium-to-high digital literacy.
- **Cross-country synthesis:** Serbia, Romania, Albania, Hungary; sample size not stated.
- **Reported barriers:** lack of financial education, perceived complexity, concerns about risk and security, unclear explanation of processes and funds.
- **Reported needs:** clear risk, return and withdrawal information; an overview of available funds; concise, interactive content; explanations inside the content where they are needed.
- **Themes:** control, relevant learning, clear explanations, practical planning, **access to human support**.
- **Quotes reported in the presentation (not verified against audio):** "I want to compare options, not just be told." (Hungary) · "I still prefer to talk to a person." (Hungary) · "It felt more like it knew me." (Hungary) · "I want to learn only what I need." (Serbia) · "Give me the full picture, don't just tease me." (Romania)
- Behaviour versus intention: willingness to invest was reported, not observed.
