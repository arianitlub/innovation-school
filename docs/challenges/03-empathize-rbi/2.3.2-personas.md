# 2.3.2 Provisional Personas / Behavioral Archetypes

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt **2.3.2**, run 14.10.2026 with skill `innovation-empathize` after [2.3.1 pattern analysis](2.3.1-interview-pattern-analysis.md).

> **Important limitation:** prompt 2.3.2 asks for research-based customer personas. We currently have two real **expert** interviews, not direct customer interviews. Creating ages, occupations, family situations, quotes or detailed “day in the life” stories would invent evidence. Therefore this output creates **provisional behavioural archetypes** only. They are useful for ideation, but they must not be presented as validated customer personas.

---

## Archetype 1 — The Independent Mass-Affluent Explorer

### Evidence basis

- Hungary expert-reported customer research: many customers prefer making investment decisions independently in the app while retaining access to a person for questions.
- Hungary: non-Premium mass-affluent customers can access investment products digitally but do not receive full personal advisory service.
- Serbia: some customers who meet higher-value criteria still choose not to use Premium/Private banking.

### Behaviour

- Comfortable doing routine or simple financial actions digitally.
- Does not necessarily want an ongoing dedicated-banker relationship.
- Wants control over timing and dislikes unnecessary calls or emails.
- May need help only when a decision becomes uncertain, high-value or difficult to understand.

### Goals

> “I want to manage my money independently, but I want reliable help available when I am unsure.”

### Likely pain points to validate

- Self-service information may not answer a personal question.
- The route from app/self-service into appropriate human guidance may be unclear.
- They may have to choose between “do it alone” and “enter a full Premium/adviser relationship.”

### Needs

- Clear information at the decision point.
- Confidence that expert help is reachable when needed.
- A low-friction transition between self-service and human support.
- No pressure to accept a full relationship-management model.

### Unknowns

- Why they do not use Premium even when eligible.
- Which decisions trigger the desire for human help.
- Whether they would trust advice initiated from the app.
- Whether fees, product choice, competitors or trust matter more than service access.

## Archetype 2 — The Guidance-Seeking Saver / New Investor

### Evidence basis

- Hungary expert: fear of losing money and insufficient knowledge limit engagement.
- Serbia expert: advisers spend time explaining investment concepts, objectives and expected outcomes.
- Hungary already has education initiatives, showing that understanding is a recognised need.

### Behaviour

- Has savings or financial capacity but may hesitate to invest.
- Needs to understand risk, time horizon, goals and what happens to their money.
- May consume education, but information alone may not be enough to act.
- Can benefit from a guided path that translates concepts into a decision relevant to their situation.

### Goals

> “I want to understand my options well enough to make a decision without feeling that I am gambling with my savings.”

### Likely pain points to validate

- Fear of loss.
- Difficulty translating generic information into “what does this mean for me?”
- Uncertainty about which product/risk level matches the goal.
- Possible preference for familiar alternatives perceived as safer or simpler.

### Needs

- Plain-language explanation.
- Goal- and horizon-based guidance.
- A trusted way to ask questions.
- A next step that feels proportionate, not overwhelming.

### Unknowns

- Whether knowledge is the primary blocker or merely one of several.
- Which educational format actually changes behaviour.
- Whether bank-provided guidance is trusted as advice or perceived as sales.

## Archetype 3 — The Advised Premium Client

### Evidence basis

- Hungary: higher Premium service can include a dedicated relationship manager, regular contact and video advice.
- Serbia: advised affluent clients can use a video meeting and an advisory tool covering goals, risk tolerance, horizon and protect-vs-grow preferences.

### Behaviour

- Has opted into or qualifies for a higher-touch service model.
- Is willing to discuss personal goals and risk with an adviser.
- Can receive investment recommendations through an established advisory process.
- May expect relationship continuity and periodic follow-up.

### Goals

> “I want a knowledgeable person who understands my situation and can help me make important financial decisions.”

### What already works

- Personal contact.
- Goal/risk/horizon conversation.
- Remote/video support.
- Adviser-supported tools and documentation.

### Needs / improvement questions

- Consistency across channels.
- Relevant follow-up over time.
- Efficient preparation and handover.
- Confidence that the adviser has enough context.

### Unknowns

- Actual satisfaction with personalisation.
- Whether follow-up is frequent/useful enough.
- Whether Premium clients still invest externally and why.

## Archetype 4 — The Eligible but Unengaged Customer

### Evidence basis

- Serbia expert: some customers who meet the profile still choose not to use Premium/Private banking.
- Hungary: customers can qualify for movement to higher service tiers, but customer choice/signature is still required.
- Hungary research signal: many customers do not want unsolicited calls or emails.

### Behaviour

- Financial profile may make them attractive for a higher service tier.
- Does not automatically convert into a Premium/advisory relationship.
- May ignore or decline outreach, prefer another provider, or simply not see enough value.

### Goals

Unknown from direct customer evidence. Possible goals should **not** be invented.

### Key questions to validate

- Why do they decline or ignore Premium?
- Do they already invest elsewhere?
- Is the barrier price, trust, convenience, perceived relevance, product offering or dislike of proactive sales?
- Would a lighter/on-demand form of guidance be more attractive?

## Design implications

### Primary archetypes for the challenge

For ideation, the most useful focus is probably **Archetype 1 + Archetype 2**: mass-affluent customers who are not using the full Premium advisory model and need either selective help or more confidence.

Archetype 3 is important as a **benchmark**: it shows what stronger advisory support already looks like. The challenge is not to rebuild Premium for everyone.

Archetype 4 is strategically important because it may explain why segmentation and proactive campaigns do not automatically create engagement.

### Design principle

Do not force every customer into one channel. A useful service should allow different levels of self-direction and support while keeping the customer goal, suitability and trust central.

---

## Validation checklist before calling these “personas”

We still need direct customer evidence on:

- real goals and triggers;
- channel preferences;
- recent investment/savings behaviour;
- reasons for refusing/ignoring Premium;
- emotional language and trust;
- workarounds and competitors;
- exact moments where help is wanted.

Until then, use the term **provisional archetypes**, not validated personas.
