## Output

### 1. Current state

**Common model in CEE: a Premium tier decided by income or savings, plus a personal adviser.**

- **Hungary, Raiffeisen Bank Zrt.:** Premium Banking needs HUF 400,000 monthly income and gives video advice (RaiConnect) plus investment specialists in branches. Premium Banking Plus needs HUF 700,000 income or HUF 10 million savings and gives a dedicated adviser by video or in person [1]. *(The page also says HUF 400,000 for Plus in one place; requires verification.)*
- **Hungary, OTP Bank:** Premium is for HUF 700,000 net monthly income or HUF 10 million savings. It offers a personal adviser, booking with your own adviser, a phone line, wider investment choice and pricing benefits [2]. "OTP Pénzügyi Tervező" is a planning tool used *in the branch* with an adviser. It looks at goals and risk appetite, and includes tax benefits and state subsidies [3].
- **Elsewhere in RBI:** Serbia advertises a personal banker, video contact and financial planning. Tatra banka (Slovakia) offers a shared team or a named banker. Raiffeisenbank Czech Republic offers automated, limited-scope advice through Raiffeisen investice [4][5][6].
- **Austria, Erste/Sparkassen:** George Invest pitches app investing "for everyone", starting small with regular plans [7]. Its earlier Invest Manager (2019) was built with the Swiss robo-adviser True Wealth: €5,000 minimum, 1.2% all-in fee [8]. *Whether it still exists and on what terms requires verification.*

**Global pattern: a free digital plan, an automated portfolio, then paid human help at higher levels.**

- **JPMorgan Chase (US):** Wealth Plan is a free "digital money coach" in the Chase app, with goal tracking and a full picture of the customer's money. It gives no investment recommendations and lets the customer book an adviser from the app [9].
- **Vanguard (US):** Personal Advisor Select costs 0.30% a year, has a US$500,000 minimum and gives a dedicated CFP® planner [10].
- **Schwab (US):** its comparison page lists automated portfolios with no advisory fee [11].
- **HSBC UK Premier** and **Moneyfarm** have clearly defined levels of human support [12][13].

**Metrics.** Public sources did not show which KPIs banks track. Common practice in the industry *(our assessment, this requires verification)* includes:

- advice coverage (share of eligible customers advised)
- assets under management per customer and net new money
- share of wallet
- how many started journeys end in an investment
- time advisers spend with clients
- NPS (Net Promoter Score, a customer-recommendation score), complaints, retention or churn

### 2. Trends 2024–2026

- **Regulators are pushing "help below full advice".** The UK FCA's *targeted support* regime started on 06.04.2026. Firms can give ready-made suggestions to groups of customers with similar situations. The FCA estimates **about 23 million UK consumers are underserved** by advice and guidance [14]. In the EU, the Retail Investment Strategy was provisionally agreed on 18.12.2025. It adds "value for money" cost tests and clearer disclosure of inducements (commissions). It will likely apply about 24 months after it is published [15].
- **AI helps advisers rather than replacing them.** Morgan Stanley's Debrief tool (June 2024) takes notes in client meetings (with consent), drafts follow-up emails and saves notes to the CRM. One adviser quoted saves about 30 minutes per meeting. **98% of adviser teams** use its earlier AI Assistant [16]. Capgemini's 2026 report says firms that give relationship managers "just-in-time client insights" see better satisfaction, share of wallet and retention [17].
- **Customers expect personal service and are ready to switch.** Capgemini: only **17%** of HNWIs (high-net-worth individuals, the very wealthy) feel their advice experience is seamless and personalised. **97%** of firms still group clients mainly by wealth band [17]. EY 2025: **29%** globally say they are likely to change their main wealth provider within three years. This is a stated intention, not actual churn [18].
- **Hungary is a market in motion.** Households' investment funds (**HUF 16,400 billion**) overtook retail government bonds (**HUF 13,700 billion**) by the end of 2025. This followed the repricing of the inflation-linked PMÁP bond, whose yield fell from 18–19% to about 4–5%. Accounts holding funds rose by more than 100,000 to nearly 1 million. Bond money moved into funds "particularly" among wealthier savers [19].

### 3. Key challenges

- **Wealth is concentrated and service depends on thresholds.** In Hungary, the top 10% of households hold **71%** of financial wealth [19]. Customers just below a Premium threshold may get little personal help. *How large this group is at RBI requires verification.*
- **Lazy money and low trust.** Nearly half of Hungarian term deposits sit in banks paying under 1% interest, mostly balances under HUF 10 million. Savers who could hold government bonds but don't say risk and low interest rates put them off [19]. This points to gaps in knowledge and confidence.
- **Adviser capacity and cost.** Personal advice is expensive. Vanguard needs US$500,000 for a dedicated planner [10]. Our internal interview (Serbian context, not verified) reports limited branch time and a lot of paperwork.
- **Rules.** MiFID II requires a suitability assessment (checking that advice fits the customer) and a suitability report. These add steps to the process. The EU Retail Investment Strategy will add cost scrutiny [15].
- **Barriers:** client data split across app and branch, tools that only work in the branch (OTP's planner), and pricing models that may not cover small balances. *Size of these barriers at RBI requires verification.*

**Other industries:**

- **Intuit TurboTax:** customers choose "Do it myself", "Help me do it" or "Do it for me". The middle option adds on-demand experts and an expert final review [21].
- **Kry/Livi (digital health):** digital-first care that "guides patients to the right level of care". The company reports 14 million appointments [22].

### 5. Cross-industry insights

- **Tiered help on the same journey (TurboTax):** let customers start alone and call an expert at the moment they get stuck, instead of choosing upfront between "app" and "branch".
- **Triage to the right level (Kry):** a short digital check sends simple cases to self-service and complex ones to an adviser. This protects adviser time.
- **Free planning layer (Chase Wealth Plan):** a no-cost goal tracker builds the habit and the data. The regulated recommendation stays with the adviser.
- **AI for preparation and follow-up (Morgan Stanley):** less paperwork means more advice time.
- **Group-based suggestions (FCA targeted support):** a model for affordable guidance that is more specific than general education but cheaper than full one-to-one advice. *Whether EU/CEE rules allow this requires verification.*

## What this means for our challenge

- **H1 (advice not personalised enough):** Capgemini's 17% "seamless and personalised" and 97% "segment by wealth band" figures support H1 globally. They come from very wealthy clients, so street interviews in Budapest must check whether mass-affluent Hungarians feel the same.
- **H2 (lack of knowledge/confidence):** the MNB data supports H2 for Hungary. Many savers keep deposits earning under 1%, and potential bond investors say risk puts them off. Ask: "What stops you investing?"
- **H3 (preference for hybrid digital + human):** Raiffeisen HU's RaiConnect video advice, Chase Wealth Plan and the TurboTax tiers all mix digital and human. Test *when* in the journey people want a human, not only *whether* they want one.
- **H4 (advisers lack data/tools/time):** Morgan Stanley's AI note-taking and OTP's branch-only planner suggest preparation and paperwork are where time goes. Ask the expert how much adviser time goes to administration.
- **Threshold gap (a possible new angle):** Raiffeisen HU and OTP use similar Premium thresholds (HUF 700,000 income or HUF 10 million savings). Customers just below them may be a good segment to look at, but this requires verification with internal data.
