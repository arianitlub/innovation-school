# 1.2.3 Scope Definition & Boundary Clarification — mass-affluent wealth advice

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt 1.2.3, run 13.10.2026 with skill innovation-understand. AI-assisted draft for the team to discuss; not a team decision. Not RBI-verified.

**Important:** this file does **not** decide our scope. The booklet says defining the problem is the team's job. Where a recommendation is given, it is labelled **Suggestion** for the team to accept, change or reject.

---

## Filled prompt

```text
I need help defining the scope and boundaries for my innovation project.

My challenge is: "How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process? Working goal: How can we give mass-affluent customers useful, affordable financial guidance that helps them achieve their goals and strengthens their relationship with the bank? Company: Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), network banks in CEE (Serbia, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania...). Our team is in Budapest 13.–16.10.2026 and will do street interviews there. Market and customer group are NOT decided yet."

Help me clarify:

1. IN-SCOPE ANALYSIS
   Based on my challenge, what SHOULD be included:

   Areas to examine:
   - Which processes, touchpoints, or activities are directly relevant?
   - Which user groups, customer segments, or stakeholders should be included?
   - Which stages, phases, or time periods should be covered?

   Aspects to investigate:
   - Process or workflow issues
   - Quality or performance problems
   - Resource or capacity constraints
   - Technology or tool limitations
   - Communication or coordination gaps
   - Policy or regulatory factors

2. OUT-OF-SCOPE CLARIFICATION
   What should likely be EXCLUDED to keep project focused:

   Suggest typical exclusions based on my challenge type, such as:
   - Adjacent processes or departments not directly affected?
   - Infrastructure changes that don't directly impact the challenge?
   - Long-term strategic initiatives beyond this project?
   - Issues that are symptoms rather than root causes?

   For each potential exclusion, explain:
   - Why it might be out of scope
   - What's the risk of excluding it?
   - When might it need to be reconsidered?

3. GRAY AREAS / BOUNDARY DECISIONS
   What areas are ambiguous and need team decision:
   - Where does this challenge area connect with adjacent systems or processes?
   - Should we include supporting activities that indirectly impact the challenge?
   - Should we include upstream/downstream processes?
   - Do we include third-party or external dependencies?

4. PHASING RECOMMENDATIONS
   If scope is too large, suggest phasing:
   - Phase 1 (Quick wins - 3 months)
   - Phase 2 (Medium complexity - 6 months)
   - Phase 3 (Long-term strategic - 12+ months)

5. SCOPE RISKS
   - What happens if scope is too narrow? (Missing root causes)
   - What happens if scope is too broad? (Analysis paralysis, lack of focus)
   - How to maintain right balance?

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Clear IN/OUT/MAYBE sections with reasoning, plus phasing recommendation if needed. Include a simple scope statement we can use to communicate the project boundaries.
```

## Output

### 1. IN scope (proposal)

#### Areas to examine

**Processes and touchpoints.** The customer journey from "I have money and should do something with it" to "first review of my goal". In our [02-understand.md](../02-understand.md) sketch these are six stages: trigger, looking for information, going to the app or branch, questionnaire, decision and signing (including funding), and after the action. Touchpoints: mobile app and investment app, branch, Premium banker (in person, phone or video), contact centre, chatbot, campaigns (email, push), statements.

**Customer groups.** Mass-affluent customers in **one** market. This includes people who are Premium and people who are not yet Premium but have similar income or savings. It also includes customers who already invest elsewhere, because they show why the bank loses business (see "hidden defection" in [1.2.1](1.2.1-terminology.md)).

**Other people.** Premium bankers and branch advisers (they deliver advice), the digital/app team (owns the journey), and compliance (sets the limits). Full list in [1.2.2](1.2.2-stakeholder-mapping.md).

**Time period.** Today's experience (last 12 months of the customer's story). Ideas should be testable within the programme: prototype by 16.10.2026, follow-up by 15.12.2026.

#### Aspects to investigate

| Aspect | What to look at in our challenge | Linked hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Process or workflow | Steps where customers stop: questionnaire, signing, funding; repeated questions across channels | H3 |
| Quality or performance | Does advice fit the customer's own goals? Is it advice or product presentation? | H1 |
| Resource or capacity | Banker time per client, number of clients per banker, ~1.5 h per advice session (expert estimate, unverified) | H4 |
| Technology or tools | What the app and the robo can do; what data advisers see before a meeting | H3, H4 |
| Communication or coordination | Handover between app and banker; follow-up after investing; explaining risk and fees clearly | H2, H3 |
| Policy or regulatory | MiFID II suitability and appropriateness; the line between guidance and advice; GDPR for data use | All |

### 2. OUT of scope (proposal)

| Exclusion | Why out of scope | Risk of excluding it | Reconsider when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Private banking clients and services** | Different customers, service model and economics; it is our upper boundary | Low. We might miss ideas that could be simplified downwards | If interviews show "upper mass affluent" customers want private-banking-like help |
| **Small-amount mass-retail investing** (for example €20–50/month starters with no savings) | Different target group and economics; our challenge says mass affluent | Medium. Emerging affluent customers overlap with this group | If the team picks "first-time investors" as the customer group |
| **Designing new investment products** (new funds, new structured products) | Owned by product teams and asset managers; our gap is advice and journey | Medium. "Wrong or expensive products" could be a real cause | If customers say the products themselves are the problem |
| **Core banking or platform replacement** | Far too large for Innovation School; does not change the customer experience directly | Low | Only if a concept cannot work at all without it (then note it as a dependency) |
| **Changing regulation (MiFID II, local law)** | Not in our control | Medium. Ideas may hit legal limits late | Always: check each idea with compliance early, but do not try to change the rules |
| **Pricing and fee strategy across the bank** | Bank-wide decision with many dependencies | Medium. Fees affect trust and value | If "hidden fees" is a top pain in interviews; then include fee *explanation*, not fee *setting* |
| **Lending, mortgages and insurance as products** | Separate product journeys | Medium. Goal-based planning touches debt and protection | Include them as *topics* in a goal check, not as sales journeys |
| **Bank-wide marketing campaigns and brand** | Symptom area (generic campaigns) rather than root cause | Low | If the trigger stage ("bank contacts me at the wrong moment") becomes our focus |
| **All RBI markets at once** | Cannot interview or test across many countries in three days | High if we generalise from one market | When a tested idea is proposed for roll-out to other network banks |

### 3. GRAY AREAS — decisions the team must make

#### A. Which market? (main open decision)

| Option | For | Against | What we would need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hungary** | We are in Budapest 13.–16.10.2026: street interviews will naturally be with Hungarian customers. Real evidence, same market. RBHU CEO session on 15.10.2026 (see [guide.md](../../training/guide.md)) | We have no desk research on Raiffeisen Bank Hungary's Premium, advice or digital offer yet. Local products (retail government securities, TBSZ accounts) shape behaviour and are new to us. **This requires verification** | One quick public-page check of the Hungarian offer; one Hungarian banker or expert conversation |
| **Serbia** | Our only expert interview (Veljko) is from Serbia; public Premium pages advertise a personal banker and planning | No street interviews possible this week; Serbia is outside the EU so MiFID II does not apply directly; transcript has many gaps | Label Budapest interviews "general customer signal, other market" |
| **Czech Republic** | Automated advice already exists (Raiffeisen investice, disclosure 03.11.2025), so we could build on a real capability | No expert contact yet; no street interviews possible; we know the offer only from public documents | A Czech expert contact; ask what robo users do and where they request a human |

**Suggestion (not a decision):** choose **Hungary** for customer interviews, because we can collect real evidence this week. Use Serbia (expert interview) and the Czech Republic (existing robo) as *comparison inputs*, clearly labelled. If the team picks Serbia or the Czech Republic instead, label all Budapest street interviews as "other-market customer signal".

#### B. Which customer group?

| Option | Who | Why interesting | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1. Savers without a plan** | Good income, savings mainly in deposits, no investment plan | Largest possible value of guidance; tests H2 (knowledge and confidence) | Keeping cash can be the right answer; do not frame them as "lost revenue" |
| **B2. Investors without follow-up** | Already invested once, no regular review | Tests H1 (personalisation) and post-sale value; retention risk | Harder to find on the street |
| **B3. Unrecognised mass affluent** | Similar income/savings but not in Premium | Bank may not see them at all; hidden defection | Needs a clear screening question |
| **B4. Premium customers who invest elsewhere** | Salary at the bank, investments at a fintech, broker or state securities | Directly shows why the bank loses business | Sensitive topic; ask without amounts |
| **B5. Young Premium / emerging affluent** | 25–40, growing income, digital-first | Tests H3 (hybrid); future relationship value | Overlaps with mass retail |

**Suggestion (not a decision):** start with **B1 savers without a plan**, and keep B4 as a second group if we meet them. B1 is easy to screen on the street ("Do you have savings beyond everyday needs?") and fits our working goal. B4 helps the business case.

#### C. Other boundary questions

| Question | Options | Suggestion |
|---|---|---|
| Include non-investment outcomes (cash reserve, paying off debt)? | Yes / No | **Yes.** Our goal is the customer's goals, not only selling investments |
| Include upstream "trigger" stage (how the bank finds and contacts the customer)? | Yes / Only observe | **Observe only.** It is important, but it involves data use and marketing, which are hard to prototype this week |
| Include downstream "after investing" (reviews, market-drop calls)? | Yes / No | **Yes.** H1 and the expert both point to weak follow-up |
| Include third parties (fintechs, state securities, partner fund managers)? | As competitors only / As partners | **As competitors only** for now: ask customers why they use them |
| Include the adviser's own workflow (preparation, paperwork)? | Yes / No | **Yes**, because H4 is one of our three priority hypotheses |
| Include the family / household? | Yes / No | **Ask about it in interviews**, but do not design for it yet |
| Use AI or real customer data in the prototype? | Yes / Mock only | **Mock only.** Fictional customers and labelled mock recommendations |

### 4. PHASING (only if the idea later becomes a real project)

The Innovation School itself covers prototype and test (13.–16.10.2026) and the pitch. These phases describe what could follow if the idea is taken forward. Timings are illustrative.

| Phase | Time | Possible content | Main dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1: Quick wins** | ~3 months | Improve what already exists: a one-page, plain-language explanation of a recommendation (goal, risk, costs, alternatives); a "talk to a banker" button that passes the customer's answers on; a short pre-meeting checklist for advisers | Compliance review of wording; app team capacity |
| **Phase 2: Medium complexity** | ~6 months | A connected digital and human journey: short education, guided goal check, robo or banker proposal, simple signing and funding, a first goal review after a few months | Integration of app, CRM and advice system; adviser training |
| **Phase 3: Long-term strategic** | 12+ months | Household view, event-based contact (with permission), adviser workspace with AI-prepared summaries under human control, roll-out to other network banks | Data permissions (GDPR), AI governance (EU AI Act), business case per market |

### 5. SCOPE RISKS

**Too narrow.** If we only fix one screen (for example, the questionnaire), we may fix a symptom. The real cause could be that no one explains the proposal, or that nobody follows up. We would also miss customers who never reach the questionnaire.

**Too broad.** "All mass-affluent customers, in all RBI markets, across all products" cannot be interviewed or tested in three days. We would end with a generic slide ("build a hybrid app") that is not backed by evidence. Analysis paralysis is a real risk with so much desk research already collected.

**How to keep the balance.**
- One market, one main customer group.
- Look at the full journey in interviews, but choose **one stage** to prototype.
- Each scope item must link to at least one hypothesis (H1–H4). If it does not, park it.
- Re-check scope after the first interviews (end of 14.10.2026), and record any change in [team-log.md](../team-log.md).
- Keep a "parking lot" list for good ideas that are out of scope.

### Scope statement (fill in the blanks together)

> We focus on **[customer group, e.g. savers with no investment plan]** in **[market, e.g. Hungary]**, from the moment they think about what to do with their savings until **[end point, e.g. their first goal review]**.
> We look at how **personal**, **understandable** and **well connected (digital and human)** the help is today, and what limits advisers in giving it.
> We will prototype one improvement for the stage **[journey stage]**.
> Private banking, new investment products, core-system replacement and changes to regulation are out of scope.
> Success for this week means: **[e.g. 10 interviews, hypotheses H1/H3/H4 checked, one tested prototype]**.

## What this means for our challenge

- **Market choice drives evidence quality.** Interviews will be in Budapest, so picking Hungary keeps customer evidence and market the same. Whatever we pick, label other-market inputs clearly.
- **H1 personalisation and H2 knowledge** fit group B1 (savers without a plan) best. Screening on the street is easy, and "keep cash" stays a valid outcome.
- **H3 hybrid channels** sits on the boundary between app and banker. Keep the handover in scope, but prototype only one stage.
- **H4 advisor data and tools** needs the adviser workflow in scope. Plan at least one adviser or expert conversation in Hungary.
