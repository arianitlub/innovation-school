# 3.2.2 SCAMPER — mass-affluent wealth advice

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt 3.2.2, run 14.10.2026 with skill innovation-ideate. AI-generated ideas as input for the team; not decisions. Evidence base: 2 experts, 2 street interviews, UX study (small n).

> **How to read this:** the booklet's example (document uploads in account opening) is replaced with today's investment-guidance journey for a mass-affluent customer in Hungary, taken from the [journey map](../03-empathize-rbi/2.3.3-journey-map.md) and the [Problem One-Pager](../03-empathize-rbi/2.4-problem-one-pager.md). The bank-side steps were reported by 2 internal experts; the customer side rests on 2 street interviews (C1, C2) and the UX study. The "4–6 minutes", the three options and the Premium thresholds (about EUR 1,100–1,900 monthly income or about EUR 27,000 in assets) were reported in interviews. This requires verification. Example companies are described from their own websites, not from proven results.

## Filled prompt

```text
I need to apply the SCAMPER creative technique to generate innovative ideas for giving mass-affluent customers outside full Premium advice clear, affordable guidance that starts from their own situation and is there when they choose to ask for it, so they can make confident investment decisions with their bank.

Current situation/process: "Outside Premium, a mass-affluent customer in Hungary invests via My Life (MiFID questionnaire, 3 options, 4–6 min) with no personal advice; personal advice only after qualifying for Premium; bank contact is often outbound (phone/ads) and product-led; when a customer cancels an investment account for cost, the bank asks why but offers nothing; customers compare options with family or ChatGPT."

Apply SCAMPER systematically:

**S = SUBSTITUTE**
- What can we replace or substitute?
  * Technology: What if we substituted [X] with [Y]?
  * Process: What if we substituted manual steps with automation?
  * Channel: What if we substituted outbound phone offers with [something else]?
  * Data source: What if we substituted product-first questions with [alternative data, e.g. the customer's life plans]?

Questions to explore:
- What if customers could start from their goals and plans instead of a product list?
- What if we used existing bank signals (income, deposits) to invite customers instead of calling them?
- What if we substituted waiting for an adviser slot with on-demand video or written answers?
- What if AI explained options instead of customers asking ChatGPT, while a banker still recommends?

Generate 5-7 substitute ideas with descriptions

**C = COMBINE**
- What can we merge, combine, or integrate?
  * Features: Combine [X feature] with [Y feature]
  * Data: Combine information from multiple sources
  * Channels: Integrate mobile with video and branch
  * Services: Bundle services together

Questions to explore:
- What if we combined the MiFID questionnaire with life-plan questions to streamline the process?
- What if one action (e.g. closing an account) also provided additional value?
- What if we combined our solution with complementary tools (Learn to Invest, RaiConnect, the advisory tool)?
- What if we integrated the people customers already trust (family) into the process?

Generate 5-7 combination ideas

**A = ADAPT**
- What can we adapt from other contexts or industries?
  * From e-commerce: What lessons from Amazon/shopping apps?
  * From social media: What from Facebook/Instagram?
  * From gaming: What engagement mechanics?
  * From healthcare: What from patient portals and triage?
  * From travel: What from Uber/Airbnb?

Questions to explore:
- How does Netflix personalize content - how could we adapt this?
- How does Amazon make processes frictionless - adapt for our context?
- How do various apps make onboarding engaging - adapt for our process?
- How do delivery apps provide status updates - adapt for our processes?

Generate 5-7 adapted ideas from other industries

**M = MODIFY, MAGNIFY, or MINIFY**
- What can we make bigger, smaller, faster, slower, more, less?

MAGNIFY:
- What if an expert answer took 1 day instead of waiting for an adviser's schedule?
- What if we offered help in the evening and at weekends instead of business hours?
- What if we expanded personal guidance significantly beyond Premium?
- What if the life profile took 2 minutes instead of a long meeting?

MINIFY:
- What if we reduced the information shown per option to the 3 things that matter?
- What if a guidance session were 15 minutes instead of a full advisory meeting?
- What if we simplified cost to one number in HUF?
- What if we focused on 3 core customer questions instead of the full product range?

MODIFY:
- What if we changed the order of steps?
- What if we changed when/where guidance happens?
- What if we changed the interface paradigm?

Generate 5-7 modify/magnify/minify ideas

**P = PUT TO OTHER USES**
- How else could we use this process, technology, or asset?
  * Repurpose existing features for new uses
  * Use customer data for additional value
  * Leverage technology for different problems

Questions to explore:
- What if the technology/process also helped users with related tasks?
- What if one component also generated additional value?
- What if analytics also enabled proactive support?
- What if one tool could serve multiple purposes?

Generate 3-5 repurposing ideas

**E = ELIMINATE**
- What can we remove completely?
  * Steps in the process
  * Required information
  * Unnecessary features
  * Points of friction

Questions to explore:
- What if we eliminated cold calls? (customer-initiated contact)
- What if we eliminated repeated questions? (tell it once)
- What if we eliminated jargon? (plain language)
- What if we eliminated waiting? (on-demand help)
- What if we eliminated steps that cause friction?
- What if we eliminated the Premium threshold as the only gate to personal help?

Generate 5-7 elimination ideas

**R = REVERSE or REARRANGE**
- What if we reversed or rearranged the process?
  * Reverse roles: What if the customer invited the bank?
  * Reverse sequence: What if we started with life plans instead of products?
  * Reverse assumptions: What if we did opposite of standard practice?

Questions to explore:
- What if the bank prepared guidance before customers even asked, but only showed it when they chose to?
- What if customers set requirements and options competed for them?
- What if we gave guidance before asking for any commitment? (trust first)
- What if the exit were the start of a conversation instead of the end?
- What if we handled worries BEFORE customers reported them?

Generate 5-7 reverse/rearrange ideas

SUMMARY & ANALYSIS:
1. Which SCAMPER dimension generated most interesting ideas?
2. Which ideas feel most feasible?
3. Which ideas are most radical/innovative?
4. Which ideas best address customer pain points?
5. Which ideas should we explore further?

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Ideas organized by SCAMPER dimension, each with:
- Idea name
- Description (2-4 sentences)
- How it addresses customer needs
- Innovation level (incremental/moderate/radical)
- Rough feasibility (easy/medium/hard)

Plus summary analysis and top 10 recommendations across all dimensions.
```

## Output

### Today's journey in one line

**Bank calls or advertises → customer (maybe) opens My Life → MiFID questionnaire → 3 options → invests alone in 4–6 minutes → no personal help unless Premium → no review → cancels for cost → bank asks why, offers nothing.** Meanwhile the real decision happens with family or ChatGPT.

Innovation level and feasibility below are **our rough judgement**, not measured. Feasibility assumes reuse of My Life, RaiConnect, the advisory tool, Learn to Invest and RAIA; whether these can be extended requires verification.

### S = Substitute

**S1 — Life Questions Instead of Product First**
Replace the product-first start of My Life with three life questions: what is the money for, when will you need it, what is planned in the next 1–3 years. The MiFID questionnaire still follows; it is not replaced.
- Customer need: guidance from their own situation (RH1; C1 would share life context).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**S2 — Invitation Instead of Cold Call**
Replace outbound phone offers with one consent-based invitation, by app notification or by letter, depending on the customer's channel. The invitation offers guidance, not a product.
- Customer need: control of the contact; C2 rejects phone offers (RH3).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy as a process; sales impact unknown

**S3 — Bank AI Explainer Instead of ChatGPT**
Replace the customer's outside ChatGPT session with an in-app assistant that explains the bank's options in plain language, using approved content. It is labelled as AI and does not recommend; a banker gives any MiFID recommendation.
- Customer need: clear information, bank present at the decision (RH2, RH5).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: hard (MiFID boundary, AI Act transparency from 02.08.2026, hallucination risk)

**S4 — On-Demand Video Instead of Adviser Schedule**
Replace "fit your life around the adviser's diary" with RaiConnect video sessions the customer books at their own time, including evenings, opened to customers outside Premium.
- Customer need: help when they ask (RH3, RH4; C1 quote about adviser availability).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium (capacity and cost require verification)

**S5 — Plain Comparison Instead of Product Sheets**
Replace separate product documents with one side-by-side view: deposit, government bond, fund, for the customer's amount and time, with yearly cost in HUF and "what can go wrong".
- Customer need: clear, comparable options (RH2; C1 "enough information… it wasn't clear").
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium

**S6 — Tablet Plan Instead of Paper Forms in Branch**
In the branch, replace product leaflets with a tablet showing the same life profile and comparer as the app, and print a one-page personal plan to take home.
- Customer need: trusted branch channel for older customers (C2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium

**S7 — Written Answers Instead of Meetings for Simple Questions**
Replace a full meeting with a written or short-video answer from a specialist for simple questions ("is a 3-year deposit or a bond better for this?"), within a stated time.
- Customer need: help on demand without a meeting (RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

### C = Combine

**C1 — MiFID + Life Profile in One Flow**
Combine the MiFID questions and the life questions into one short, plain-language flow, saved once and shared across app, video and branch.
- Customer need: one personal starting point (RH1).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium (compliance sign-off)

**C2 — Learning + Deciding**
Combine Learn to Invest lessons with the decision step: a 1-minute lesson appears right where the customer hesitates (risk levels, fund costs, withdrawal).
- Customer need: clarity at the decision point (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy

**C3 — App + Video + Branch with One Context**
Combine the three channels so the customer's profile, options viewed and open question travel with them. The banker sees them before the session and uses the advisory tool to recommend. This is the core of the team's Money Guide idea.
- Customer need: help in the trusted channel without starting again (RH3, RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium (integration of My Life with RaiConnect/CRM requires verification)

**C4 — Customer + Trusted Family Member**
Combine the customer's session with an optional family member (as C1's nephew), invited by video or to the branch, or given a shared read-only summary with consent.
- Customer need: decisions happen with family (RH5).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy

**C5 — Deposit Maturity + Guidance**
Combine a term deposit maturing with a short, optional guidance offer: "your deposit ends on 30.11.2026; here are your options side by side; want to talk to someone?". Only if the customer has agreed to such notices.
- Customer need: help at a natural moment, not a sales call (RH1, RH3).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**C6 — Account Closing + Real Alternative**
Combine the cancellation step with an honest cost check and one real alternative (a cheaper option at the bank, if any), plus a "talk to someone first" button.
- Customer need: a real option at the exit moment (RH2; C1 was offered nothing).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**C7 — Yearly Summary + Check-in Invitation**
Combine a yearly personal money summary (saved, costs paid, goal progress) with the invitation to an optional 10-minute check-in.
- Customer need: follow-up outside Premium (journey stage 7).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

### A = Adapt

**A1 — Health Triage (NHS 111)**
Adapt digital triage: a few questions decide whether the customer needs content, a written answer, a video call today or a branch meeting. Human time goes where it matters most.
- Customer need: a person when unsure, without overloading advisers (RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**A2 — Three Levels of Help (TurboTax)**
Adapt "do it yourself / on-demand expert / full service" to investing, with an expert check before committing at the middle level.
- Customer need: control and help at the same time (RH3).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**A3 — Personal Recap (Spotify Wrapped)**
Adapt a yearly recap to money: what you saved, what you paid, how your goals moved, in a friendly and shareable format.
- Customer need: understanding and follow-up (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium

**A4 — Learning Streaks (Duolingo)**
Adapt short, daily-sized lessons linked to the customer's goal, with a simple progress path. No pressure to buy at the end.
- Customer need: confidence to decide (RH2; UX study).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**A5 — Status Tracking (delivery apps)**
Adapt "your order is on its way" to help requests: "your question is with Anna from RaiConnect, answer by 18:00 tomorrow".
- Customer need: help they can rely on (RH3, RH4).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium

**A6 — Fair Cancellation (good subscription services)**
Adapt the better subscription exit flows: easy to leave, but first one honest comparison and one real offer, with the reason recorded for the product team. No tricks that make leaving hard.
- Customer need: a real alternative at the exit (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy

**A7 — Async Coaching (Future fitness app)**
Adapt the coach model: one human expert supports many customers asynchronously through the app (messages, short videos), with the app collecting progress in between.
- Customer need: personal help at lower cost (RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

### M = Modify, magnify or minify

**M1 — Magnify Access: Evenings and Weekends**
Offer RaiConnect video slots in evenings and on Saturdays so customers do not adjust their life to the adviser.
- Customer need: help on the customer's terms (RH3; C1).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium (staffing)

**M2 — Magnify Speed: Answer Within One Working Day**
Promise an expert answer to any written question within one working day, instead of waiting for a meeting slot.
- Customer need: immediate support when lost (C1).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**M3 — Magnify Reach: Guidance Below Premium**
Extend personal guidance (not full Premium service) to customers outside Premium, through a shared video team rather than named advisers.
- Customer need: personal guidance outside Premium (RH1, RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium–hard (capacity and business case unknown)

**M4 — Minify Information: Three Things per Option**
Show only three things first for each option: what can go wrong, when you get the money back, what it costs in HUF a year. More detail is one tap away.
- Customer need: clarity (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy

**M5 — Minify the Profile: Two Minutes**
Keep the life profile to about two minutes and five questions, with a loss comfort slider ("how would you feel if HUF 2 million became HUF 1.8 million for a while?").
- Customer need: easy to share context (RH1).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**M6 — Modify the Timing: Guidance Before the First Offer**
Change the order: the customer gets a short guidance step (profile + comparison) before any product is shown or offered.
- Customer need: not a product push (RH1).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy–medium

**M7 — Minify the Service: A 15-Minute Guidance Call**
Offer a short, low-cost or free 15-minute call for one question, instead of the full advisory meeting tied to Premium. A paid one-off longer session could follow; price to be tested.
- Customer need: affordable help (RH2, RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

### P = Put to other uses

**P1 — Premium Signals Used for an Invitation, Not a Call**
The bank already uses salary and deposit signals to find Premium prospects (RS expert). Use the same signals to send one opt-in guidance invitation instead of a sales call.
- Customer need: control of contact (RH3).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy–medium (GDPR and consent check)

**P2 — The Advisory Tool as a Shared Screen**
Use the advisory tool that bankers already use in video sessions (RS) as a screen the customer sees and keeps afterwards, so the recommendation is explained, not only given.
- Customer need: clarity and trust (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium (depends on the tool's features; requires verification)

**P3 — RaiConnect for Guidance, Not Only Premium**
Use the RaiConnect video channel (built for Premium) for short guidance sessions for customers outside Premium, on request.
- Customer need: a person when unsure (RH4).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**P4 — RAIA as Front Door to Humans**
Use the RAIA virtual assistant mainly to understand the question and connect to the right human quickly, before adding any advice-like AI. A later RAIA-to-human connection was reported as planned (HU).
- Customer need: help when they ask, without long searches (RH3, RH4).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**P5 — Exit Reasons as Product Feedback**
Use the reason customers give when they close an investment account (today asked but not used) as structured feedback for pricing and product teams.
- Customer need: lower cost over time (RH2).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy

### E = Eliminate

**E1 — Eliminate Cold Calls**
Stop outbound investment sales calls for this segment. Contact starts with the customer, or with one opt-in invitation.
- Customer need: trust and control (RH3; C2 "if it is by telephone, then no").
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy as a rule; needs management agreement

**E2 — Eliminate Telling Your Story Twice**
Profile, question and options viewed are passed to whoever helps next, in any channel.
- Customer need: help without starting again (RH4).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: medium

**E3 — Eliminate the Waiting for a Slot for Simple Questions**
Simple questions go to written answers or the AI explainer; only real decisions need a booked session.
- Customer need: immediate support (C1).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**E4 — Eliminate Jargon**
Rewrite the MiFID questionnaire and the three options in plain language, with "why we ask this" for each question.
- Customer need: clarity (RH2; UX study).
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium (compliance sign-off)

**E5 — Eliminate Hidden Cost**
Show all costs as one yearly number in HUF before the customer commits, and again once a year.
- Customer need: transparent cost (RH2; C1 "it costs too much money").
- Innovation: incremental · Feasibility: easy–medium

**E6 — Eliminate the Threshold as the Only Gate**
Personal help is no longer only for customers who reach Premium. Customers below it can get a short call, a written answer or a paid one-off session.
- Customer need: affordable personal guidance (RH1, RH4).
- Innovation: radical (for this bank's model) · Feasibility: hard (business model and capacity)

### R = Reverse or rearrange

**R1 — The Customer Invites the Bank**
Reverse the contact: the bank never starts a sales contact; the customer opens a "guidance" door in the app or branch when they want, and sets how the bank may contact them.
- Customer need: control of contact (RH3).
- Innovation: radical · Feasibility: medium

**R2 — Life First, Product Last**
Rearrange the journey: life plans → clear comparison → help if wanted → MiFID suitability and recommendation → product. Products come at the end.
- Customer need: guidance from their situation (RH1).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

**R3 — Exit Becomes the Start of a Conversation**
When a customer closes an investment account, the bank treats it as the start of a guidance conversation ("what would make this right for you?"), with a real offer and no pressure.
- Customer need: a real alternative at the exit (RH2; weak evidence, single case).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy–medium

**R4 — The Customer Brings Their ChatGPT Answer**
Reverse the competition with ChatGPT: the customer can paste or show what ChatGPT or family suggested, and the bank explains it honestly, including where it may be right.
- Customer need: bank present where the decision is made (RH5).
- Innovation: radical · Feasibility: medium–hard (MiFID boundary; requires compliance check)

**R5 — Guidance Before Commitment**
Give the comparison and a first conversation before asking the customer to open a securities account or sign anything (trust first).
- Customer need: trust and clarity (RH2, RH3).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: easy–medium

**R6 — The Bank Shows Options It Earns Less On**
Reverse the usual sales logic: the comparison also shows deposits and government bonds, and says when they may suit better.
- Customer need: trusted, neutral-feeling comparison like ChatGPT (RH5).
- Innovation: radical · Feasibility: medium (business acceptance unknown)

**R7 — Worries Handled Before Customers Call**
When markets fall, customers who agreed to it get a short, plain explanation and an offer to talk, before they panic or sell.
- Customer need: confidence and help on their terms (RH2, RH3).
- Innovation: moderate · Feasibility: medium

### Summary analysis

**1. Which SCAMPER dimension generated the most interesting ideas?**
**Reverse/Rearrange** and **Eliminate**. They attack the root problems the interviews found: contact on the bank's terms (R1, E1), product before life (R2) and the unused exit (R3). Substitute is close behind because it maps each pain to an existing RBI asset (S4 RaiConnect, S3 RAIA/AI).

**2. Which ideas feel most feasible?**
Ideas that change content or process, not systems: C2 Learning + Deciding, M4 Three Things per Option, E4 Eliminate Jargon, A6 Fair Cancellation, C6 Account Closing + Real Alternative, P5 Exit Reasons as Feedback, C4 Family Member, C5 Deposit Maturity + Guidance. Feasibility still depends on compliance sign-off and release cycles, which are not known.

**3. Which ideas are most radical?**
E6 Eliminate the Threshold as the Only Gate, R1 The Customer Invites the Bank, R4 The Customer Brings Their ChatGPT Answer and R6 The Bank Shows Options It Earns Less On. Each changes how the bank earns or sells, so each needs a business case.

**4. Which ideas best address the customer pain points?**

| Pain point | Strongest ideas |
|---|---|
| Product-led, not life-led | S1, C1, R2, M6 |
| Contact on the bank's terms | E1, S2, R1, S4, M1 |
| Information unclear | S5, M4, E4, C2 |
| Cost and unused exit moment | E5, C6, R3, A6 |
| Decisions made elsewhere | S3, C4, R4, R6 |

**5. Which ideas should we explore further?**
The ideas that (a) reuse existing systems, (b) touch one of the three moments of truth (first contact, decision, exit) and (c) can be tested on paper or by role-play in a few days. See the top 10 below.

**Evidence warning:** cost as the exit driver, the unused exit moment and ChatGPT as adviser each rest on **one** customer (C1). Ideas built mainly on them (C6, R3, R4) should be tested with more customers before investment.
