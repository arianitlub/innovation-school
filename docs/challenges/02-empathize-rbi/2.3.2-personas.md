# 2.3.2 Provisional Personas — mass-affluent investment guidance

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt **2.3.2**, run 14.10.2026 with skill `innovation-empathize`. **Updated 14.10.2026** after two street interviews (C1, C2) and the UX research summary. Input: [2.3.1](2.3.1-interview-pattern-analysis.md).

> **Limitation:** each customer persona rests on **one** real street interview plus expert and UX signals. Names are labels, not real people. Ages, jobs and family details are **not** invented; where the prompt asks for them we write "not known". Quotes are from transcripts (lightly edited). Treat these as **provisional personas** until more customers are interviewed.

---

## Filled prompt

```text
Based on my customer interviews, I need to create 2-4 personas that represent distinct user types with different needs and behaviors regarding mass-affluent investment guidance and wealth-management advice (customers outside full Premium advice).

Here are my key findings from interviews:

COMMON CHARACTERISTICS OBSERVED:
No demographics recorded (no ages, jobs or amounts asked). C1: English-speaking, digitally comfortable, invested about 2 years in ETFs, had and cancelled a bank investment account. C2: Hungarian-speaking long-time saver (decades), investing not described, distrusts online offers. UX study: 8 Serbian regular savers not yet investing, low-to-medium investing confidence, medium-to-high digital literacy. See 2.3.0.

COMMON BEHAVIORS & USAGE PATTERNS:
C1: prefers digital ("I can see everything and do everything"), wants immediate support; had to fit adviser's schedule. C2: trusts branch and self-initiated contact; rejects phone, internet and ad offers. Experts: Mass customers invest via My Life (4–6 min, no advice); Premium via relationship manager / RaiConnect video / advisory tool.

DISTINCT PAIN POINTS BY GROUP:
C1: product push, not personalised, information not clear, too expensive, bank offered nothing when they left. C2: offers by telephone. UX: complexity, risk/withdrawal unclear, text-heavy content.

DISTINCT NEEDS BY GROUP:
C1: personal guidance based on life situation and near-term plans, cheaper, on-demand help; willing to share context. C2: face-to-face trust, self-initiated contact. UX: simple explanations, learn only what's needed, human access.

NOTABLE QUOTES:
"It was not too personalised… I felt it was more of a product push." (C1) · "I had enough information… It wasn't clear." (C1) · "If it was more personalised for me and if it would be cheaper." (C1) · "I had to adjust my life around the availability of the adviser." (C1) · "I think it's really bad, but I use ChatGPT for that." (C1) · "If it is by telephone, then no." (C2) · "If an offer is made in a bank branch, there is more trust." (C2) · UX (reported): "I want to compare options, not just be told."

Help me create research-based personas:

1. IDENTIFY DISTINCT PERSONA TYPES
   - How many personas should I create? (typically 3-4 for this scope)
   - What are the KEY DIFFERENTIATING FACTORS?
     * Not just demographics, but behaviors, needs, goals, pain points
   - What makes each persona meaningfully different from others?
   - Ensure personas cover main user groups but avoid over-segmentation

2. PERSONA STRUCTURE
   For each persona, create a comprehensive profile:

   A. PERSONA SNAPSHOT
   - Name (realistic, memorable)
   - Age
   - Occupation
   - Location (urban/suburban/rural if relevant)
   - One-sentence description

   B. DEMOGRAPHIC & BACKGROUND
   - Industry relationship (products used, account tenure)
   - Life situation (family status, financial goals)
   - Digital proficiency level (tech-savvy, comfortable, struggling)

   C. DIGITAL SERVICE USAGE BEHAVIOR
   - Primary channel (system, website, in-person)
   - Frequency of use (daily, weekly, monthly, rarely)
   - Main use cases (checking balance, paying bills, transfers, support, etc.)
   - Device preferences (smartphone, tablet, desktop)
   - When they use our digital services/channels (on-the-go, at home, at work)

   D. GOALS & MOTIVATIONS
   - What are they trying to accomplish with our digital services/channels?
   - What would make their experience better?
   - What outcomes matter most to them?
   - Frame as: "I want to [goal] so that [benefit]"

   E. PAIN POINTS & FRUSTRATIONS
   - Top 3-5 specific frustrations (prioritized)
   - What causes them stress or anxiety?
   - What makes them consider leaving?
   - Include emotional aspects (fear, confusion, annoyance)

   F. NEEDS & PRIORITIES
   - What do they need most from our digital services/channels?
   - What trade-offs matter to them? (speed vs. security, simplicity vs. features, etc.)
   - What would delight them?

   G. BEHAVIORS & WORKAROUNDS
   - How do they currently cope with problems?
   - What alternatives do they use?
   - When do they abandon digital and call/visit branch?

   H. REPRESENTATIVE QUOTE
   - Select one powerful quote from interviews that captures this persona
   - Should be authentic, in customer's voice

   I. A DAY IN THEIR LIFE (Scenario)
   - Brief narrative showing how this persona interacts with our digital services/channels
   - Include a specific situation that highlights their needs and pain points
   - Example: "Maria opens the system on her commute to check if her paycheck arrived..."

   J. TECHNOLOGY ATTITUDE
   - How do they feel about technology in general?
   - What other apps/services do they use and love?
   - What sets their expectations?

   K. COMPETITIVE PERSPECTIVE
   - Have they used other companies' digital services?
   - What do they compare us to?
   - What would make them switch?

3. PERSONA COMPARISON MATRIX
   Create a table comparing personas across key dimensions:
   - Digital proficiency
   - Usage frequency
   - Primary pain points
   - Primary needs
   - Channel preference
   - Emotional drivers (what matters most emotionally)

4. DESIGN IMPLICATIONS PER PERSONA
   - What does each persona need from our solution?
   - How might solutions differ for each persona?
   - Are there conflicts between personas' needs?
   - Who should we prioritize and why?

5. VALIDATION & QUALITY CHECK
   - Do these personas cover most participants from interviews?
   - Are they distinct enough to be useful?
   - Are they based on real research patterns (not stereotypes)?
   - Do they avoid edge cases or extreme outliers?
   - Are they actionable (can designers/developers use them)?

OUTPUT FORMAT:
2-4 complete persona profiles (detailed descriptions) plus comparison matrix.
Each persona should feel like a real person, grounded in actual research data.
Include specific examples and quotes from interviews to bring personas to life.
Maximum 500 words per persona.
```

## Persona 1 — "The Self-Directed Comparer" (based on C1)

### A. Snapshot
- **Label:** Self-Directed Comparer
- **Age / job / location:** not known (English-speaking; market not stated)
- **One line:** Invests long-term on their own, with family and ChatGPT as advisers, after leaving the bank's investment account because of cost and product push.

### B. Background
- Saves and has invested part of the savings for about two years (ETFs).
- Had a bank investment account; **cancelled it this year** because of cost.
- Digital proficiency: comfortable.

### C. Channel behaviour
- Prefers **digital**: wants to "see everything and do everything".
- Used an adviser before, but had to fit the adviser's schedule.

### D. Goals
> "I want to invest long-term and regularly, at low cost, in options that fit my life and plans."

### E. Pain points
1. Bank information was **not clear** and **not personal**: "more of a product push".
2. **Too expensive** to keep the investment account.
3. Had to **adjust to the adviser's availability**.
4. When leaving, the bank asked why but **offered nothing**.

### F. Needs and priorities
- Guidance that asks about **life situation and near-term plans**, not only the MiFID questions.
- Transparent, low cost.
- **Immediate support** when lost, on their schedule.
- Willing to share personal context for better guidance.

### G. Workarounds
- **Nephew** shortlists options.
- **ChatGPT** analyses and compares their funds and suggests where to put new money.
- Moved to an online provider.

### H. Quote
> "It was not too personalised… I felt it was more of a product push." — C1 *(transcript)*

### I. Scenario (from the interview, not invented)
After two years of investing, C1 wants to add money. They upload a list of their funds to ChatGPT and ask what to focus on. The bank, which once had their investment account, is not part of this decision.

### J. Technology attitude
Positive and pragmatic; feels slightly guilty using ChatGPT ("I think it's really bad") but finds it "very helpful".

### K. Competitive perspective
Already switched to a cheaper online option. Would come back for **personal + cheaper**.

## 4. Design implications per persona

- **Self-Directed Comparer:** personal, comparable, transparent options in the app; help on demand; a real offer at the exit moment.
- **Cautious Branch Saver:** a face-to-face or self-booked path; **never** cold calls.
- **New Learner:** clarity at the decision point; small, safe first step.
- **Shared need:** the **customer controls the contact**, and guidance starts from **their** situation.
- **Conflict:** digital vs branch. The solution must work in **more than one channel**.
- **Priority for Ideate:** Persona 1 (closest to mass affluent with investable money and a clear story), with Persona 2's channel needs as a constraint.
