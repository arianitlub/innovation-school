## Output

### Before any interview: principles and standard probes

**Rules (from the booklet and our team rules in 03-empathize.md §5)**
- Real people only. No AI-simulated answers. If colleagues answer as customers, label it "proxy evidence".
- Work in pairs: one asks, one writes. Write the person's exact words for quotes.
- Never ask for amounts, account numbers or names. Notes stay anonymous.
- Never describe or show solution ideas. If the person asks "what are you building?", say: "We are still learning. We don't have a solution yet. That's why your story helps."
- Open questions only: "Tell me about…", "Walk me through…", "How did you…". Avoid "Do you…?" and "Would you…?".
- About 80% listening. Ask "why" or "tell me more" 3–5 times per topic.
- Someone who never had an adviser should not be asked to rate "their adviser". Ask what help they actually got.
- If the person gets emotional (worry, anger, pride), slow down and probe there.

**Standard probes (use anywhere)**
- "Tell me more about that."
- "Why was that?"
- "How did that make you feel?"
- "What did you do next?"
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "What happened just before that?"
- **Silence:** after an answer, count to five in your head before speaking. People often add the most honest part then.
- **Mirroring:** repeat their last few words as a question. "…you just left it in the account?"

**Coding to do after each interview (for the results tracker in 03-empathize.md §6)**
- Advice received: **no advice / product information only / personal advice** (P1). Score H1 only among people with advice.
- In segment: yes / no. Saving pattern: deposits only (B1) / invests at own bank / invests elsewhere (B4).
- H1, H2, H3 (and H4 for the expert): **Y** supports, **N** contradicts, **?** unclear. Score from what people **describe**, not from what they say they would prefer.

### (a) Full guide — customer interview, about 60–65 minutes

Use for a longer interview with a mass-affluent customer. Timing: 3 + 6 + 8 + 23 (Part 4 without the adviser-only H4 block) + 10 + 7 + 5 + 3 = 65 minutes. To get under 60, shorten Part 3 and Part 6 to two questions each. The H4 deep dive is for advisers only and is in the expert version (b); a short adviser H4 block is also included here so the full guide covers all mission-critical hypotheses.

#### PART 1: Introduction (3 min)

**Say:**
> "Hello, thank you for your time. My name is ___, and this is my colleague ___. We are part of a learning programme at Raiffeisen Bank International. We are trying to understand how people make decisions about their savings, and what help they get or miss. We are not selling anything, and there are no right or wrong answers. Your own experience is exactly what we need."

> "My colleague will take notes. We will not write your name, and we will not ask about amounts or account details. Is it OK if we take notes? [If recording:] Is it OK if we record the audio, only for our team, and delete it after we write our notes?"

> "Please be completely honest. If something was confusing or annoying, that is the most useful thing you can tell us. Nothing you say will offend us, and it doesn't matter which bank you use."

**Rapport without bias:** start with their everyday life, not with banking. Do not mention Premium, robo-advice, apps or any idea we have.

**Listen for:** comfort level; whether they expect a sales pitch (if so, repeat that we sell nothing).

#### PART 2: Background and context (6 min)

1. "To start, tell me a little about yourself: what do you do, and what does a normal week look like for you?"
2. "Who else is part of your money decisions: a partner, family, anyone else?"
3. "Tell me about how you deal with your bank today. Which bank, and how do you usually get in touch: app, branch, phone, something else?" **[Team H3; Sabrina Q1 rephrased]**
4. "Apart from everyday spending, what do you have going on with money right now? For example savings, a loan, a goal you are saving for." *(Do not ask for amounts.)*
5. "When you think about investing, what comes to mind? How familiar does it feel to you?"

**Probes:** "When did that start?" / "Who taught you about money?"

**Listen for:** household role in decisions; channel habits; savings only in deposits vs investing; first signs of knowledge or confidence (H2). Note segment (B1/B4/other) and age range.

#### PART 3: Warm-up — general experience (8 min)

6. "Tell me about the last time you had some money left over and had to decide what to do with it. What happened?" **[Design Thinking prep Q1]**
7. "What were you trying to achieve with it, and when might you need the money?" **[Design Thinking prep Q2]**
8. "Walk me through what you did, from the first thought to what you finally did." **[Design Thinking prep Q3]**
9. "What made you start thinking about it at that moment?" *(trigger: life event, news, bank contact — U6)*

**Probes:** "What did you do first?" / "How long did that take?" / "How did you feel while deciding?"

**Getting frustrations to come out:** "Many people tell us parts of this are a bit annoying. What was the most annoying part for you?" (Normalises frustration without suggesting which part.)

**Listen for:** whether the bank appears in the story at all; who or what they turned to; where they stopped; emotions (worry, guilt, indifference).

#### PART 4: Deep dive by hypothesis (30 min)

##### H1 Personalisation (8 min) — with P1 screening first

Main questions:
10. "Tell me about the last time anyone gave you advice about your savings or investments. Who was it?" **[Team H1; Sabrina Q1, Q20 rephrased]**
    - If they say nobody: "When you needed help, what did you get instead?" → code **no advice** and move on to H2. Do not ask H1 questions 11–12.
11. "In that conversation, what did they ask you about your own life before they suggested anything? Your goals, family, other savings?" **[Sabrina Q4, Q13 rephrased]**
12. "How well did what they suggested fit what you were trying to do? Tell me why." **[Team H1]**

Follow-up probes:
- "Was it a recommendation for you, or a list of options to choose from? How could you tell?" *(separates personal advice from product information — P1)*
- "What did they not ask that they should have known?"
- "What happened after that conversation? When did you next hear from them?" **[Sabrina Q6, Q12 rephrased; P3]**
- "How did you feel walking out of that meeting?"
- "What would have made it more useful for you personally?" **[Team H1]**

Alternative explanations to explore: maybe the advice was personal but the product was wrong; maybe they did not want personal advice; maybe the problem was trust, not fit ("What made you trust it, or not?" **[Sabrina Q15 rephrased]**).

Listening checklist:
- [ ] No advice / product information / personal advice
- [ ] Did the adviser ask about goals, family, debts, other banks?
- [ ] One product or the whole situation?
- [ ] Any follow-up after the decision?
- [ ] Words like "selling", "they just wanted me to buy"

##### H2 Knowledge and confidence (7 min)

Main questions:
13. "What is difficult for you when you decide what to do with your savings?" **[Team H2]**
14. "Tell me about a time an offer or product was hard to understand. What was unclear: the risk, the fees, when you can get the money back, something else?" **[Team H2; Sabrina Q5, Q8 rephrased]**
15. "Think of a money decision you felt sure about. What helped you feel confident enough to decide?" **[Team H2]**

Follow-up probes:
- "What did you do when you didn't understand something?"
- "Who did you ask? Why them?"
- "What do you look at first when you see a savings or investment offer?" *(U3)*
- If relevant in Hungary: "Tell me about your experience with state securities. What did you do when the interest changed?" *(U4; context: MNB reports a shift from retail government bonds to funds after PMÁP yields fell)*

Alternative explanations: they understand well but don't trust the bank; rates are too low to bother; they have no money to spare; they simply have no time. Ask: "Apart from understanding it, what else held you back?"

Listening checklist:
- [ ] Knowledge named as the barrier, or something else (trust, rates, time, need cash soon)?
- [ ] Which words confuse them (risk class, fund, ETF, fees)?
- [ ] Where they get confidence: a person, a number, friends, a test?

##### H3 Digital vs human (8 min)

Main questions:
16. "Walk me through the last time you did something with your savings or investments. Where did you do each step: app, website, phone, branch, video?" **[Team H3; Sabrina Q11 rephrased]**
17. "Which money tasks did you do yourself in an app in the last month or so? Tell me how that went." **[Team H3]**
18. "Tell me about a moment when you wanted to talk to a real person about money. What was going on?" **[Team H3]**

Follow-up probes:
- "Why at that moment and not earlier?"
- "How did you reach a person? How long did it take?"
- "When you moved from the app to a person, or back, what did you have to explain again?" *(P6)*
- "If you had a question about your savings tomorrow, how would you get a person to help you?" *(P7)*
- "How did it feel doing that part alone?"

Alternative explanations: they want a person for everything (trust, habit); they want no person at all (speed, privacy); the channel matters less than who it is. Ask: "What matters more to you, how you talk to someone or who it is?"

Listening checklist:
- [ ] Actual channels used per step (not stated wishes)
- [ ] The moments they wanted a person: first time, big amount, market drop, life change, paperwork
- [ ] Repeating information between channels
- [ ] Whether they know how to reach human help

##### H4 Advisor data and tools (advisers only, 7 min) — skip with customers

Use only if the person is a bank adviser. The 30-minute expert version (b) has the full H4 block.
19. "Tell me about the last client you advised. What information did you need before the meeting, and where did you get it?" **[Team H4]**
20. "What information or tools were missing that day?" **[Team H4]**
21. "Walk me through your preparation. What took most of the time?" **[Team H4]**

Probes: "How long, roughly?" / "What did you do when the information wasn't there?" / "Tell me about a tool advisers stopped using." *(P10)*

Listening checklist:
- [ ] Data missing (goals, household, other banks, app history)
- [ ] Time per session; share spent on forms
- [ ] Tools used, ignored or worked around

#### PART 5: Pain points exploration (10 min)

22. "Looking back at everything you told me, where in the process did you feel most frustrated or confused?" **[Sabrina Q7 rephrased]**
23. "Tell me about a moment when you gave up, postponed, or decided to do it another way." **[Design Thinking prep Q5]**
24. "What did you do instead? How did you get around the problem?" *(workarounds)*
25. "Walk me through the steps from 'I want to do this' to the money actually being invested (or saved). Where did it take longer than you expected?" *(P5)*
26. "If you could remove one of those problems, which one would you remove first? Why that one?" *(prioritising pains)*

**Probes:** "How often has that happened?" / "What did it cost you: time, money, peace of mind?"

**Listen for:** where people stop (information, questionnaire, signing, funding, after investing); workarounds such as asking friends, ChatGPT, moving money to state securities or a broker; the one pain they care about most.

#### PART 6: Ideal future state (7 min)

Ask about outcomes and feelings, never about our ideas.
27. "Imagine a year from now your savings are in a place you feel really good about. What would be different from today?"
28. "Tell me about any service, in any area of life, where you felt really well looked after. What did they do?" *(cross-industry — doctors, travel, insurance, tax)*
29. "What would good help with your savings feel like? What would it not include?"
30. "What would make you trust advice about your money more?" **[Team street "Trust and value"; Sabrina Q15 rephrased]**

**Do not say:** "Would you use an app that…?", "What if the bank offered a video adviser…?". If they ask for our opinion, turn it back: "What would you want it to do?"

**Listen for:** values (safety, control, time, independence, being understood); expectations from other industries; what they would not want (pressure, jargon, being sold to).

#### PART 7: Comparisons and expectations (5 min)

31. "Where else have you looked for help with your savings: other banks, friends, the internet, ChatGPT, a broker? Tell me what happened." **[Team street "Trust and value"; Sabrina Q16 rephrased; U2]**
32. "Where do you keep savings or investments apart from your main bank? How did that start?" *(U5; no amounts)*
33. "What did they do differently from your bank?"
34. "What makes you stay with your current bank for this, or think about moving?" **[Sabrina Q14 rephrased]**

**Listen for:** competitor names (OTP, Erste, brokers, fintechs, state securities via MÁP); reasons: cost, simplicity, a person who helped, habit.

#### PART 8: Closing (3 min)

35. "If your bank could do one thing better for your savings, what would it be?" **[Team street close]**
36. "Is there anything I didn't ask that you think I should have asked?"
37. "Thank you very much, this really helps. Would it be OK if we contacted you once more later, for example to show you something early and hear your view? You can say no." *(Only collect contact details if they offer; store separately from notes.)*

**Note straight after:** best quote, advice coding, segment, H1/H2/H3 Y/N/?.

**Remember:** stories, not opinions. No amounts. No solution ideas. Count to five before the next question.

## What this means for our interviews

- **Start every customer interview by finding out whether advice happened at all.** Code it (none / product information / personal advice) so H1's 7/10 pass mark is counted fairly.
- **The street version is the team's draft, slightly tightened.** Two questions were reworded from "would you" or yes/no into stories ("Tell me about a moment when…", "What was unclear…"). Everything else is the team's wording. Get a native Hungarian speaker to translate it before going out, and do not machine-translate.
- **For the expert, protect the H4 block (section 4) and ask for names.** One expert cannot meet "most advisors". Ask for 1–2 Hungarian Premium or RaiConnect bankers we can speak to.
- **Score from what people describe, not what they say they prefer.** This matters most for H3. Use the results tracker in 03-empathize.md §6 straight after each interview.
- **Keep solution ideas out of every question.** That includes the expert's own ideas, education workshops (Sabrina Q17), robo or video advisers. If a person brings one up, note it as their idea.
