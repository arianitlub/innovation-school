## Challenge Question
> "How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process?"

This is the exact framing our team needs to investigate during the Innovation School 2026 Innovation Challenge track (research phase due 13.10, worked on throughout the Budapest on-site days via Design Thinking).

## Who Are "Mass Affluent" Clients

### In plain words
They are people with **more money than an average retail customer, but not enough for private banking.** Typically they have a good, stable salary, savings sitting in deposits, maybe a mortgage, and real goals: a flat, retirement, the kids' education. They are valuable to the bank, but there are many of them, so the bank **can't give each one a personal adviser for hours**. They often get "premium" perks (a card, a phone line, a nicer branch desk) instead of real financial planning.

"Mass affluent" is a **commercial label, not a legal/regulatory category.** Every bank names it differently: Premium, Select, Premier, Personal Banking. At Raiffeisen network banks it is usually **Premium**.

### Segment ladder (paper, section 1.1)
| Tier | Investable assets* | How they're served | What they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass retail | below ~€25k | Branch / app, no adviser | Products only (non-advisory) |
| **Emerging affluent** | €25k–€100k | Digital / self-directed, pooled remote support | Start investing, regular savings plans, **education** |
| **Core mass affluent** | €100k–€500k | Named or team adviser + digital | Goal planning, model portfolios, protection & lending |
| **Upper mass affluent** | €500k–€1m | Fewer clients per adviser, specialist access | More complex allocation, retirement, tax/estate referrals |
| Private banking *(out of scope)* | often €1m+ | Dedicated relationship manager + specialist team | Bespoke portfolios, credit, succession, structuring |

\*The paper calls these **"design bands", not verified eligibility criteria**. They vary by country, household income, total relationship balance and local economics. In CEE/SEE markets the real thresholds are much lower. The expert's Serbian example: Premium = salary ≥150,000 RSD (~€1,300/month), with no asset minimum. **For our case, "mass affluent" ≈ the upper part of mass retail plus the Premium segment.**

### How to recognise them: don't look at assets alone
The paper recommends a qualification model that combines:
- current financial assets and deposits
- **recurring investable surplus** (money left over every month)
- income stability and mortgage position
- pension and protection gaps
- liquidity events (expected inheritance, business sale)
- complexity and need for advice
- digital behaviour and willingness to engage
- **household** potential, not just the individual

This matches the expert: the bank already used "more than 200 left in the account for 3 months in a row" to find fund prospects.

### What they worry about (the questions current advice doesn't answer)
- *Am I saving enough?*
- *Can I retire when I want?*
- *How much cash/liquidity do I actually need?*
- *What happens if my income changes?*
- *Is my household overexposed to property, cash, or one employer?*

The paper's key point: a **compliant investment recommendation (MiFID suitability) is not the same as holistic advice.** You can sell someone a suitable fund and still leave all these questions unanswered.

### How banks serve this segment today (paper, section 1.2)
| Model | Examples | Strength | Typical weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium universal banking | **Raiffeisen network banks**, Erste, UniCredit, Intesa, Santander, BNP, Nordea | Broad relationship, local branches | "Premium" perks dominate over real financial planning |
| Adviser-led wealth | UBS, Julius Baer, Coutts, HSBC Private | Deep expertise, strong relationship | Too expensive to copy for mass affluent |
| Hybrid bank advice | ABN AMRO, ING, HSBC Premier, Barclays Premier | Digital servicing + adviser access | Journeys and data still split by channel |
| Digital managed portfolio (robo) | Nutmeg/J.P. Morgan, Scalable Capital, Moneyfarm, Vanguard | Easy onboarding, transparent, scalable | Can't handle complex household needs |
| Self-directed + escalation | Interactive Investor, Fidelity, Schwab, Merrill Edge | Customer control, help on demand | Product choice gets confused with advice |

**Takeaway:** RBI sits in row 1. The opportunity is to borrow the convenience of robo (row 4) and the human-at-key-moments of hybrid (row 3), without the cost of row 2.

## Key Findings From the Strategic Assessment (PDF)

### Executive Summary — 5 Conclusions
1. **"Mass affluent" is a commercial label, not a regulation** — local brands vary (Premium, Select, Premier...); working range ~€100k–€1m investable assets.
2. **Banks are hybrid at channel level, but not at continuity level** — mobile/web/branch/remote/specialist channels usually all exist, but data/conversations/portfolio views don't connect across them.
3. **Biggest customer gap: investment suitability ≠ holistic advice.** A compliant recommendation can still leave unanswered: Am I saving enough? Can I retire when I want? How much liquidity do I need? Am I overexposed in property/cash/one employer?
4. **Best operating model = "digitally prepared, human confirmed, continuously monitored."** Automation handles aggregation, prep, standard scenarios, docs, alerts. Advisers focus on goals, trade-offs, behavioral coaching, high-consequence decisions.
5. **A Raiffeisen-like universal bank has structural advantages** (broad retail relationship, account/lending signals, branch+remote distribution, insurance/investment capability, trust) — the opportunity is combining these around the customer's actual financial objectives instead of separate product journeys.

### Biggest Gaps Identified (paper, section 3), with the paper's proposed response
| Gap | What it means | Paper's response |
|---|---|---|
| **Fragmented financial picture** | Bank sees only its own accounts, not the whole household, so liquidity/retirement/concentration analysis is weak | Consented household balance sheet with data source, freshness and confidence shown. External data optional, and uncertainty visible (no false precision) |
| **Segment status ≠ advice** | A premium card or phone line is not wealth advice | Define a **minimum advisory promise**: annual wealth health check, documented goals, investment plan, proactive review triggers, access to a qualified adviser |
| **Product-led conversation** | Starts from a campaign/fund, so "goal discovery" just justifies the sale | Separate the problem from the solution: capture goal, horizon, liquidity need, capacity for loss **first**, then recommend |
| **Adviser capacity** | Holistic planning takes more time, so "just do deeper advice" won't scale | Centralise portfolio construction and complex cases, collect data digitally, auto-generate meeting prep, set contact frequency by **need/event, not wealth** |
| **Compliance feels like admin** | MiFID questionnaire feels repetitive and pointless to the customer | Explain *why* each question matters, reuse valid answers, flag contradictions, separate mandatory from optional, turn output into a customer-facing rationale |
| **Weak post-sale value** | Customer sees performance, not "am I on track?" | Report 4 things together: **goal progress, portfolio risk, contribution adequacy, recommended action**. Contact on meaningful deviation, not by sales calendar |

### Target End-to-End Journey (11 Stages, paper section 2)
Common thread across all current-state gaps: steps are *disconnected* (digital doesn't feed the adviser, the branch doesn't update the plan, monitoring is separate from the client's real objectives). The target is **one continuous process**, not a series of product interactions.

| # | Stage | Today | Customer pain | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify & qualify | Assets/income/products decide the segment | People with growing potential or urgent needs are missed; others get generic premium offers | Dynamic "advice need & potential" score with transparent suppression rules |
| 2 | Onboard & aggregate | KYC/AML/tax/investment data via several forms | Same questions again and again; no view of external assets/pensions | Prefilled digital fact-find; optional open-banking data; household balance sheet |
| 3 | Discover goals | Risk questionnaire, then straight to products | Goals stay vague ("growth", "retirement"), with no amount/date/priority | Guided goal discovery with trade-offs between competing goals; household participation |
| 4 | Build financial plan | Limited to the investment portfolio | Cash, debt, pension, insurance, contributions disconnected | "Financial plan lite": liquidity, emergency reserve, retirement trajectory, debt, protection |
| 5 | Assess suitability | MiFID questionnaire (knowledge/experience, capacity for loss, objectives, preferences) | Long, inconsistent answers, unclear why questions matter | Conversational but controlled workflow, prefilled data, contradiction checks, plain language |
| 6 | Construct recommendation | Adviser picks from approved products/model portfolios | Looks product-led, weakly linked to goals | Goal-to-portfolio engine: contribution plan, liquidity and risk scenarios |
| 7 | Explain & decide | Product docs, performance, adviser talk | Cost, risk, alternatives, downside hard to compare | **One-page decision summary**: goal, recommendation, total cost, risks, alternatives, "what would change my plan" |
| 8 | Execute | Separate signatures, funding, product processes | **Drop-off between "yes" and a funded portfolio** | Straight-through execution, e-signature, funding tracker, adviser alert on exceptions |
| 9 | Monitor & review | Statements + periodic reviews | Performance vs. market, not vs. own goals | Goal-progress dashboard, drift alerts, review-trigger rules |
| 10 | Life-event engagement | Campaigns or adviser memory | Advice comes after the event, or as a generic sales message | Permission-based signals: retirement, mortgage change, cash build-up, inheritance, family changes |
| 11 | Retain & deepen | Cross-sell, tier upgrades | Household gets several uncoordinated bank contacts | Persistent household plan + interaction record across branch, remote and digital |

**Where the expert says RBI hurts most for mass customers:** stages 3 → 8. Discovery, plan and suitability are skipped (non-advisory), and execution isn't end-to-end digital.

### Practices: ready now vs. needs more control (paper, section 4)
- **Established, scalable now (no autonomous AI needed):** centrally governed model portfolios · digital fact-find + e-signature with adviser exception handling · remote video/phone advice with branch escalation · customer goal dashboards · approved next-best-conversation prompts · central specialist desks (pensions, protection, investment, succession) · CRM-integrated notes and follow-ups.
- **Emerging, needs tighter controls:** GenAI summaries and meeting prep · AI *drafting* (not approving) suitability rationales · conversational financial-planning assistants · predictive life-event detection from transactions · continuous suitability monitoring · AI-generated portfolio explanations. These should be treated as **decision support**, with human review, traceable inputs, and a clear line between *education*, *guidance* and *regulated personalised advice* (GDPR, MiFID II and the EU AI Act, assessed per use case).

### Priority Opportunity Set (ranked, with complexity)
| # | Concept | Customer Benefit | Bank Benefit | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mass-Affluent Wealth Health Check | Clear view of cash/investments/debt/protection/retirement | Repeatable advice entry point beyond products | Medium |
| 2 | One Advisory Workspace | No repeated story across channels | Lower prep time, consistent follow-up | Medium–High |
| 3 | Goal-Progress & Action Dashboard | "Am I on track?" + next action | Recurring engagement, credible value | Medium |
| 4 | Event-Driven Next-Best Conversation | Timely, relevant contact | Better adviser focus, conversion | Medium |
| 5 | Hybrid Service Tiers | Choice of digital vs human | Cost aligned to complexity/willingness to pay | Medium |
| 6 | Adviser Copilot w/ Compliance Guardrails | Faster, more consistent meetings | Less admin, better documentation | High |
| 7 | Household/Open-Finance View | Advice reflects full financial life | Better planning, relationship depth | High |
| 8 | Family & Intergenerational Service | Helps with inheritance/family decisions | Asset retention across generations | High |

### Three Concepts Most Likely to Differentiate
- **A. Wealth Health Check** — 30–45 min hybrid process → household net-worth view, liquidity/emergency-fund check, retirement trajectory, concentration/risk observations, protection/debt indicators, 3 agreed actions. Valuable even with no product sold.
- **B. Goal-Based "Financial Control Centre"** — one digital view: goals & priority, probability range of outcomes, contributions vs. required, portfolio alignment/risk, next review, adviser commitments.
- **C. Life-Event Advice Engine** — governed: detect material change → assess relevance/permitted use → present adviser with evidence/questions → adviser accepts/postpones/rejects → document outcome. Converts signals into conversations, not automatic sales.

### Recommended Roadmap (from the paper)
1. **Near term**: define minimum advisory promise, standardized wealth-health conversation, plain-language suitability journey, one-page recommendation summary, structured goals/review record, pilot transparent event triggers.
2. **Build phase**: adviser workspace + customer dashboard, centrally governed planning/model portfolios, remote specialist pools, digital pre/post-meeting prep, measure goal coverage/review completion (not just sales).
3. **Transformational**: permission-based external asset/pension data, controlled adviser copilots, event/deviation-based engagement (vs. scheduled), family/intergenerational planning, paid-advice tiers only once value is proven.

### Guardrails Called Out (relevant if our solution touches AI/data)
Suitability-by-design, no hidden autonomy, purpose limitation, data minimization, human accountability, explainability, conflict management (no quiet product-margin optimization over suitability), outcome monitoring (customers actually on track — not just paperwork complete).

### Bottom Line (paper's own framing)
> Don't imitate private banking at lower cost. Create a distinct scaled-advice model: a continuously updated household financial plan, digitally prepared and monitored, with human advice at the moments that matter.

## Expert Interview — Veljko's Meeting (internal investment-advice expert)
Source: `Innovation_School_English_Transcript-Veljko.txt`. It's an auto-transcript translated to English. There are many `[unclear]` gaps, and the speaker labels ("Veljko", "Speaker 3") are not reliable. Context is a **Serbian network bank**: amounts in dinars, and local regulation is lighter than EU MiFID. Treat numbers as approximate.

This is our **internal expert input** (the team task required ≥1 expert before Budapest). It provides reported internal observations, mainly in a Serbian-market context. It does not verify the current state across all RBI network banks.

### Current state at the bank
- **Non-advisory vs. advisory.** In non-advisory, the bank presents products ("here are 15 funds, you choose"). Advisory means questionnaires (knowledge/experience, risk profile) followed by a recommendation.
- **Mass customers get non-advisory in practice.** Branches have no time ("like a production line"), so advisory exists mostly in premium (only some parts) and private banking.
- **The economics don't work manually.** A mass customer investing €200–500 earns the bank very little, while full advice takes ~1.5h of staff time, so **the process has to be automated**.
- **Segments (Serbian example):** mass (no personal banker) → premium (personal banker, e.g. salary ≥150,000 RSD; thresholds differ per country, e.g. higher in CZ) → private banking (own advisers). Premium is split again: "premium investment" customers (almost private-banking level) vs. high-income customers with no deposits. Today some mass customers have premium potential, because premium lacks the capacity to take them.
- **Robo-advice was recently piloted** in an investment app (name unclear in the transcript). It gives a personalised recommendation, monthly contributions (e.g. €200/month auto-allocated), and automatic rebalancing when the portfolio drifts from the profile.
- **Romania** colleagues stopped using an existing advisory tool because it was too complex. They prefer the simpler robo-advisory tool that mass and affluent customers can also use. **"Give me something simple."**

### Biggest gaps (expert's view)
1. **No end-to-end digital process.** This is the biggest bottleneck. The customer should open the app, get profiled, receive a concrete personalised proposal (e.g. "40% alternatives / 30% bonds / 30% …"), accept or tweak it, and have everything (subscriptions, standing orders) opened in the background. The expert's verdict: "If we built that tomorrow, customers would say 'where do I sign?'"
2. **Education comes first.** Customers need to learn before they invest, e.g. an in-app quiz ("you are this type of investor"). The education component is "almost defined, needs implementing". The intended flow is **educate → profile → recommend → execute**.
3. **Unguided processes stall.** "If the process is not guided, the customer will always get stuck somewhere."

### What the solution should look like (expert's vision)
- **Hybrid / assisted digital.** This is more realistic than pure self-service (it also dominates in SK/CZ for complex products), e.g. a video call while the customer clicks through.
- **Tiered service, inherited upward.** A basic digital service is open to everyone, including private-banking customers who want to self-serve. Higher tiers add a banker who assists, runs campaigns, and invites the customer to meetings. Lower tiers are always available to the segments above them, never the other way around.
- **A guided chatbot + "call a banker" handover.** An in-app chatbot already exists and is being worked on. Extending it to investment products is "a small extension". The chatbot must be **excellent** (team member: bank bots are usually worse than ChatGPT-type tools). It should be **context-aware**, seeing the same screen as the customer. ~10 users going through the process would produce ~30 questions covering ~95% of all questions.
- **Short profiling was suggested by the expert.** About 4 questions were proposed as a starting point: *What are you saving for? For how long? What do you know/have you done? What do you do if the market drops (sell all / do nothing / buy more)?* EU standardises the questionnaire content (MiFID), so **the challenge is delivery (UX), not content**.
- **The investment horizon is the #1 input.** Get it right and most of the problem is solved. There are two typical mistakes: a risky fund for money needed in 1 year (e.g. an apartment), or a cash fund for a 5-year horizon (return given up for nothing). After horizon come **subjective risk tolerance** and **knowledge/experience** (beginners → diversified managed fund first).
- **Behavioural data can enrich profiling:** employment stability (bank employee vs. freelancer), age (horizon), other income, overdraft/saving-drawdown patterns (liquidity risk → don't invest in equities). An earlier targeting rule for fund prospects: **>200 (unit unclear) left in the account for 3 consecutive months**.

### Employee side
- **Mass segment:** the employee helps fill in data, explains, is available when the customer gets stuck, and **calls when markets fall** to explain and prevent panic selling.
- **The biggest help is speeding up the whole process.** Less paperwork (copying, signing, filing contracts), and the banker's time goes to *explaining why the proposal fits the customer*. That explanation is the added value.
- **AI over customer data** (licences coming soon): head-office analysts could ask "which customers are most likely to use our funds / become private banking?" The expert doubts branch staff would run such queries themselves, so it's more useful for central roles.
