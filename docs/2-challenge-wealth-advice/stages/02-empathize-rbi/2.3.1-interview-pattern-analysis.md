## 1. Pattern clustering — needs and pain points

### Theme A — Personal guidance sits in Premium; outside it, help feels like a product push

**Evidence: 2/2 experts, 1/2 customers (C1), UX supportive.**

- E-HU-1: mass-affluent customers are served **non-advisory**; personal advice mainly in Premium/Private.
- E-RS-1: personal advice works well for advised clients; some eligible clients don't use Premium.
- **C1:** got "enough information" but it was "not too personalised… more of a product push"; asked only the [MiFID?] questions, not about life plans *(transcript)*.
- UX: "It felt more like it knew me" was valued (Hungary).

**Explicit need:** guidance that starts from *my* situation and plans. **Implicit:** a bank that asks about life context, not only the compliance questionnaire. **Latent:** customers are **willing to share** that context (C1) — the bank isn't using it.

### Theme B — Information is there, but it isn't clear or relevant

**Evidence: 2/2 experts, 1/2 customers, UX strong.**

- C1: "I had enough information… It wasn't clear" *(transcript)*.
- UX: text-heavy content, unclear risk/return/withdrawal, missing fund overview, glossary hard to find.
- Experts: education already exists (Learn to Invest, podcast, brochures).

**Need:** less information, more *clarity* at the decision point. **Correction:** the problem is not "no education", it is **unclear and not personal**.

### Theme C — Customers want control, with help on their terms

**Evidence: 2/2 experts, 2/2 customers (but in different ways), UX supportive.**

- C1: prefers digital — "I can see everything and do everything. If I get lost, there is immediate support"; dropped human advice because of having to fit the adviser's schedule *(transcript)*.
- C2: trusts the **branch** or **contacting the bank themselves**; not internet, not ads.
- Both say, in effect: **the customer decides when and how contact happens.**
- E-HU-1 customer research: self-service + person on request; no repeated calls/emails.

**Need:** customer-initiated, on-demand help. The *channel* differs (app vs branch); the *control* is common.

### Theme D — Outbound phone and unsolicited offers destroy trust (NEW, strong)

**Evidence: C2 explicit; E-HU-1 research; interviewer notes that all street contacts so far said "not by telephone" (not counted).**

**Need:** permission-based, customer-initiated contact. **Implication:** "more outreach" to the segment is likely to backfire.

### Theme E — Cost decides whether customers stay (NEW)

**Evidence: C1 explicit; E-HU-1 (government bonds win on fee/tax).**

- C1 cancelled the bank investment account because "it costs a lot more"; wanted it "cheaper" *(transcript)*.
- When C1 left, the bank asked why — and **offered nothing** *(transcript)*.

**Need:** transparent, affordable options and a real conversation at the exit moment. This matches our working goal word "**affordable**".

### Theme F — Customers learn from family, other people and ChatGPT, not the bank (NEW)

**Evidence: 2/2 customers.**

- C1: nephew → ETFs; nephew shortlisted options; **ChatGPT** to compare funds and decide where new money goes ("very helpful") *(transcript)*.
- C2: does not research; relies on information from other people.

**Latent need:** a trusted, neutral-feeling "comparer". **Risk for the bank:** it isn't in the conversation when the decision is made (hidden defection). UX: "I want to compare options, not just be told."

### Theme G — Safety first, long-term

**Evidence: experts (fear of loss, bonds seen as safe), C1 (not risky, long-term recurring), UX (risk and security concerns).**

### Theme H — The bank already has the capability

**Evidence: 2/2 experts.** 4–6 min digital journey, MiFID profiling, RaiConnect video, advisory tool, education. Gap = **access, clarity, cost and timing**, not technology.

## 5. Behavioural patterns

- **Customer-initiated contact** is preferred (C1 digital, C2 self-initiated / branch).
- **Workarounds:** family advice, ChatGPT comparison, moving to a cheaper provider (C1).
- **Exit trigger:** cost (C1). **Trust trigger:** face-to-face, self-initiated (C2).
- Fast digital execution exists, but **the decision itself is made outside the bank** (C1).

### Takeaway

The problem moved from "*we need a better advisory tool*" to "**customers outside Premium get product-led, unclear, costly offers on the bank's terms, so they decide elsewhere (family, ChatGPT, cheaper providers)**".

## 7. Unexpected insights and surprises

1. **ChatGPT is already the customer's adviser** (C1) — for comparing funds and deciding where new money goes.
2. **The bank asked why the customer left but offered nothing** (C1): a lost retention moment.
3. **Cost, not knowledge, made C1 leave.**
4. **Phone outreach is rejected** (C2; reportedly all street contacts).
5. **Not everyone is digital:** C2 trusts the branch, not the internet.
6. **Customers are willing to share life context** to get personal guidance (C1).
7. A trust answer outside our control: "If Hungary used the euro" (C2) — currency trust; out of scope.

---

## 8. Root-cause view (5 Whys)

**Why did C1 stop investing with the bank?** It cost too much and felt like a product push. → **Why a product push?** The conversation covered product and MiFID questions, not life plans. → **Why?** Personal advice is tied to Premium; outside it the service is non-advisory (E-HU-1). → **Why does it matter?** The customer finds clearer, cheaper help elsewhere (nephew, ChatGPT, other provider).

**Why do customers distrust bank offers?** They come by phone or ads, on the bank's terms (C2). → **Why does that hurt?** Trust grows when the customer starts the contact or meets someone in person.

---

## 9. Competitive and comparative insights

- **Cheaper providers / ETFs** win on cost (C1).
- **ChatGPT** wins on availability and neutral-feeling comparison (C1).
- **Family** wins on trust (C1, C2).
- **Government bonds** win on safety and fee/tax (E-HU-1).
- The bank wins on **face-to-face trust** when the customer comes to it (C2).

## 11. Confidence and data quality

- **Strong (several source types agree):** control over contact, safety first, information unclear, capability exists.
- **Moderate:** product-led advice outside Premium (experts + C1), no outbound phone (C2 + HU research), learning outside the bank (C1 + C2).
- **Weak / single case:** cost as exit driver, exit moment unused, ChatGPT as adviser.
- **Missing:** a counted set of ≥10 in-segment customers; why eligible customers reject Premium; adviser-side capacity data; any numbers.
- **Contradiction to keep:** C1 wants digital, C2 wants branch. Don't force one channel.

## 12. Implications for solution design

### Prioritise
- Guidance that starts from the customer's **life situation and plans**, not the product.
- **Clear, comparable** options with transparent **cost**.
- **Customer-initiated** help, in the customer's preferred channel (app, video, branch).
- Using the **exit/doubt moment** to help instead of losing the customer.
- Building on existing tools (My Life, RaiConnect, advisory tool, Learn to Invest).

### Avoid
- More outbound calls or ad-driven offers.
- More generic education content.
- A new advisory tool built from scratch.
- Digital-only designs (C2).
- "Sell more" as the success measure.
