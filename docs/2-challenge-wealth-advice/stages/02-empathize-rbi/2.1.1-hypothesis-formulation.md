- **[External survey]**: a consultancy or industry survey, usually global and often about very wealthy clients, not CEE mass-affluent customers.

## Filled prompt

```text
I am working on: "How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process? Working goal: How can we give mass-affluent customers useful, affordable financial guidance that helps them achieve their goals and strengthens their relationship with the bank? Company: Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), CEE network banks. Interviews this week (13.–16.10.2026) in Budapest: 1 internal investment-advice expert (30 min) and ~10 street interviews with passers-by (5–7 min each)."

Based on initial data analysis, I need to formulate hypotheses to test through interviews with users/customers/stakeholders.

What I know from data:
(We have NO internal RBI data. These are desk-research and interview findings, each labelled by evidence strength.)
- [Public source] Raiffeisen Bank Hungary Premium Banking starts at HUF 400,000 monthly income and offers video advice (RaiConnect) plus investment specialists in branches. Premium Banking Plus needs HUF 700,000 income or HUF 10 million savings and gives a dedicated adviser (the page also shows HUF 400,000 for Plus in one place; this requires verification).
- [Public source] OTP Bank Hungary Premium uses the same thresholds (HUF 700,000 net monthly income or HUF 10 million savings) and offers a personal adviser plus a planning tool (OTP Pénzügyi Tervező) used in the branch with the adviser.
- [Public source] Erste, MBH and UniCredit Hungary sell similar Premium tiers with higher thresholds (HUF 8–50 million savings or HUF 800,000–1,000,000 income). No Hungarian bank publishes uptake, satisfaction or retention results for its advice.
- [Public source] Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB), Savings Report 2026: household investment funds (HUF 16,400 billion) overtook retail government bonds (HUF 13,700 billion) by end of 2025, after the inflation-linked PMÁP bond yield fell from about 18–19% to about 4–5%. Nearly half of term deposits sit in banks paying under 1%. Savers who could hold government bonds but don't say risk and low interest rates put them off.
- [Single reported interview] Mass customers get non-advisory service in practice ("here are 15 funds, you choose"); branches have no time ("like a production line"). Full advice takes about 1.5 hours of staff time, which does not pay for a customer investing EUR 200–500. Serbian context, numbers approximate.
- [Single reported interview] "If the process is not guided, the customer will always get stuck somewhere." Education should come first: educate → profile → recommend → execute. Romanian colleagues stopped using a complex advisory tool: "Give me something simple."
- [Single reported interview] Robo-advice was recently piloted in an investment app (market and name unclear in the transcript). Hybrid / assisted digital (for example a video call while the customer clicks through) is seen as more realistic than pure self-service.
- [External survey] Capgemini 2026: only 17% of high-net-worth clients feel their advice is seamless and personalised; 97% of firms still group clients mainly by wealth band. EY 2025: 29% of wealth clients globally say they are likely to change main provider within three years (stated intention, not actual churn).
- [Generic paper] Mass-affluent customers often get "premium" perks rather than goal planning; repeated story across channels and weak follow-up are named gaps. Not RBI-verified.

What I'm uncertain about:
- WHETHER mass-affluent customers in Hungary received any advice at all, or only product information and an appropriateness test (team H1 depends on this)
- WHY advice feels or does not feel personal: missing goals conversation, one product at a time, no follow-up, or no advice at all (team H1)
- Whether lack of knowledge and confidence, or something else (low trust, low rates, no time), stops savers from deciding (team H2)
- At which moments customers want a human and which steps they are happy to do in an app, based on what they actually did (team H3)
- What really limits advisers: missing data, missing tools, preparation time, paperwork, or number of clients (team H4; we have only one expert interview, Serbian context)
- Where customers look for advice first (bank, friends, internet, ChatGPT) and whether they trust the bank's advice
- Why some customers keep their salary at the bank but invest elsewhere (state securities, brokers, fintechs)
- How needs differ between "savers without a plan" and "Premium customers who invest elsewhere" (market and customer group are not yet decided; suggestion is Hungary, group B1 first, B4 second)

Help me formulate testable hypotheses:

1. PROBLEM HYPOTHESES
   Convert my uncertainties into specific, testable hypotheses about problems:
   - Format: "We believe that [specific user group] experiences [specific problem] when [specific situation] because [hypothesized reason]"
   - Example: "We believe that new employees struggle to complete onboarding paperwork because the instructions are unclear and they don't know which forms are required for their role"

   Generate 10-15 problem hypotheses covering relevant areas such as:
   - Process or workflow issues
   - Tool or system usability problems
   - Information gaps or communication failures
   - Resource or support limitations
   - Training or knowledge gaps

2. USER/CUSTOMER BEHAVIOR HYPOTHESES
   What assumptions am I making about what users want, value, or do?
   - Format: "We believe that [specific user group] prefers/values/does [specific behavior] because [hypothesized reason]"
   - Example: "We believe that experienced users skip the standard process and use workarounds because the official method is too slow for their needs"

   Generate hypotheses about:
   - Method or approach preferences
   - Priorities (what matters most to them)
   - When and how they currently handle this area
   - What drives their satisfaction or frustration
   - What influences their success or compliance

3. SEGMENTATION HYPOTHESES
   How might needs differ across user groups?
   Suggest relevant segments based on my challenge, such as:
   - By experience level (new vs. experienced)
   - By role or responsibility
   - By frequency of interaction (daily vs. occasional)
   - By location or context
   - By goals or priorities

4. MISSION-CRITICAL IDENTIFICATION
   - Which hypotheses, if wrong, would completely change our solution direction?
   - What makes each one mission-critical?
   - Mark top 5 mission-critical hypotheses

5. TESTABILITY ASSESSMENT
   For each hypothesis, suggest:
   - How could we test it in interviews? (specific questions to ask)
   - What evidence would validate it?
   - What evidence would falsify it?
   - How could we observe this in customer behavior?

6. HYPOTHESIS PRIORITIZATION
   Help me prioritize which hypotheses to test first based on:
   - Impact if true (would it significantly change our solution?)
   - Uncertainty level (how confident are we already?)
   - Feasibility to test (can we learn this from interviews?)

OUTPUT FORMAT:
List of 15-20 hypotheses organized by category, with mission-critical ones marked, and testing approach for each. Include a prioritized list of top 10 to focus on.
```

## What this means for our interviews

- **Ask "did you get advice at all?" before anything about personalisation.** Code each person as no advice / product information / personal advice, then score H1 only among those who got advice. Keep the team's 7/10 pass mark.
- **Score H1 and H3 from stories, not from direct questions.** Do not ask "Was your advice personalised?" or "Do you prefer hybrid?". Ask what happened last time and judge from what they describe.
- **H4 rests on one expert this week.** Label it "single interview" and ask the expert to name 1–2 Hungarian Premium or RaiConnect bankers we could speak to, so "most advisors" can be tested later.
- **Use the street screening to mark segments** (deposits only / invests at own bank / invests elsewhere; Premium yes/no if offered). This lets us compare B1 and B4 without asking about amounts.
- **Keep the evidence labels when we write results.** Desk research and the transcript suggest where to look; only the real interviews can validate or invalidate H1–H4.
