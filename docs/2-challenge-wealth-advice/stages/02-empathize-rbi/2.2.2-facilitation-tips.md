## Output

**Two very different interviews.** A **street interview** is 5–7 minutes with a stranger who did not plan to talk to us: we need warmth, speed and only the core questions. The **expert interview** is 30 minutes with a colleague who knows the topic: we need structure, follow-up questions and a clear line between facts and opinions. Each section below gives tips for both where they differ.

All example phrases are in English. **A native Hungarian speaker should translate them** and adapt the tone; polite forms (for example formal "Ön" vs informal "te") matter. Which form to use with strangers of different ages is a judgement for the native speaker.

### 1. Building rapport quickly

**Street (first 30–60 seconds, not 2 minutes)**
- Smile, stand at a comfortable distance, slightly to the side rather than blocking their way.
- Say who you are in one sentence and that it is short and anonymous (script in [2.1.3](2.1.3-participant-recruitment.md)).
- Ask a very easy first question, such as the screening question, so they can answer quickly and feel safe.
- If they sit (café, bench), ask "May I sit down?" or crouch to eye level instead of standing over them.
- Only one person talks; the note-taker introduces themselves briefly and stays a little back.

**Conversation starters**
- "Thank you for stopping. There are no right or wrong answers: we just want to hear your experience."
- "Let's start simple: how do you usually deal with your bank, app, branch, phone?"

**Expert (first 2 minutes)**
- Thank them for their time, repeat the purpose and the 30-minute limit, confirm anonymity and the recording choice.
- "We're not evaluating anyone. We want to understand how advice really works for this group, including the messy parts."
- Start with their role: "What does a normal week look like for you?"

**Empathy and active listening:** nod, short encouragers ("I see", "mm-hmm"), repeat a key word they used, and react to feelings ("That sounds frustrating").

**Body language**
- In person: open posture, no crossed arms, phone and clipboard low, eye contact without staring. Note-taker writes calmly, not frantically.
- Teams: camera on, look at the camera when asking, mute others, no typing noise from the lead.
- Phone: not planned. If used: slightly slower pace, more verbal encouragers ("Yes, go on"), because they cannot see you nod.

### 2. Asking questions effectively

**Open questions that bring stories:** start with "Tell me about the last time…", "Walk me through…", "What happened when…", "How did you decide…".

**Avoid leading questions:** do not put our hypothesis into the question, and do not suggest the answer.

| Topic | Bad (leading / closed) | Good (open, story) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 personalisation | "Was the advice too generic?" | "Tell me about the last time you got advice about your money. How well did it fit your own situation?" |
| H2 knowledge | "Is investing too complicated for you?" | "What is difficult for you when you decide what to do with your savings?" |
| H3 hybrid | "Would you prefer an app for simple things and a person for big decisions?" | "Which money tasks do you happily do yourself in an app? At which moment would you want a real person?" |
| Trust | "Don't you trust banks?" | "Where do you look first for money advice? Why there?" |
| H4 (expert) | "You don't have enough tools, right?" | "What takes most of your preparation time before an advice meeting?" |
| Solution | "Would you use a goal-planning app from your bank?" | Do not ask. No solution ideas in this step |

**Silence:** after a question or an answer, wait 3–5 seconds. People often add the most honest part after a pause. On the street keep silences short (2–3 seconds) so it does not feel awkward.

**Follow a thread or stay on script?**
- Street: follow a thread only if it is clearly about advice, confidence, channels or trust, and only for one or two follow-ups. Then return to the next hypothesis block.
- Expert: follow surprising threads (e.g. "there's a group nobody covers") for up to 2–3 minutes, then return. Mark skipped questions to ask at the end.

### 3. Probing deeper — the "5 whys"

On the street we usually have time for **1–2 probes**, not 5. With the expert, 3–4 levels are realistic.

**Vary the "why"** so it does not sound like an interrogation:
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What do you mean by '[their word, e.g. complicated]'?"
- "Can you give me an example from the last time?"
- "What did you do next?"
- "How did that feel?"
- "What made that important for you?"
- "What would have had to be different?"

**Example chain (street, H2)**
> They: "I just keep it in my account."
> Us: "What made you decide to keep it there?"
> They: "I don't really understand funds."
> Us: "What part is unclear for you?"
> They: "If I need the money, can I get it? And what are the fees?"
> (Stop: we have the H2 story and the specific gap, access and fees.)

**Keep probing when:** they show emotion, use a vague word ("complicated", "they always…"), or describe an action (gave up, moved money).
**Move on when:** they repeat themselves, show discomfort, look at their watch, or the topic moves towards amounts or private details.

### 4. Handling difficult situations

| Situation | How to detect | What to do | Phrases |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Short answers** | "Yes", "no", "fine" | Ask for a concrete moment instead of an opinion; offer options as a start | "Think of the last time you had money left at the end of the month. What did you do with it?" / "Was it more the app, a person, or a friend?" |
| Still short | After 2 tries | Accept it. Ask the close question and thank them. Note "low depth" | "Last question: if your bank could do one thing better for your savings, what would it be?" |
| **Off-topic** (e.g. general politics, inflation, a long story about a loan) | Not about advice, confidence, channels or trust | Check if it connects (inflation may explain why they keep cash = relevant). If not, redirect kindly | "That's interesting, and let me bring us back to how you get advice on your savings…" |
| **Venting** (angry about bank fees or a banker) | Repeated complaints, rising voice | Acknowledge once, then turn it into a story and a need | "That sounds really frustrating. What would a good experience have looked like?" / "What did you do after that?" |
| **Saying what we want to hear** | Very positive, agrees with everything, looks at our badge, says "you probably want…" | Reassure, ask for counter-examples | "There's no right answer, honestly: the negative stories help us most." / "Was there a time it didn't work well?" |
| **Doesn't remember** | "I don't know", long pause | Anchor in an event: payday, bonus, a big purchase, a market drop, a bank call | "Do you remember when you last opened a savings or investment product? How did that start?" |
| Still doesn't know | After one anchor | Accept it; ask a nearby question; code "?" | "No problem. Who would you ask if you needed advice tomorrow?" |
| **Asks us for advice** ("Should I buy funds?") | Direct request | Do not advise; we are not allowed and it biases the interview | "I'm not allowed to advise here, sorry. But I'm curious: what would help you decide?" |
| **Asks which bank we are / attacks Raiffeisen** | Defensive or critical | Be honest, do not defend, note the feedback | "Yes, we work for Raiffeisen. That's exactly why your honest view helps." |
| **Companion joins in** | Partner or friend answers | Welcome it (family decides, see 1.2.2) but note who said what | "Do you usually decide these things together?" |
| **Expert goes too deep into one topic** | 10 min on one block | Park it and move on | "This is really useful. Can we come back to it at the end? I want to make sure we cover tools and time." |
| **Expert starts sharing client data** | Names, specific accounts | Stop kindly | "Please don't tell us anything that identifies a client; the general pattern is enough." |

### 5. Observing non-verbal cues

| Cue | Possible meaning | How to probe | How to note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hesitation, "hmm", looking away | Unsure, or uncomfortable (money) | "Take your time." / "It sounds like it's not simple. What makes it hard?" | `~` next to the answer |
| Lighting up, leaning in, faster speech | Strong preference or good experience | "You seem to like that. What is it about it?" | `!+` |
| Sighing, eye-rolling, shaking head | Frustration | "That looks like it annoyed you. What happened?" | `!-` |
| Frowning, asking us to repeat | Confusion with a term (e.g. "advice", "Premium") | Rephrase in everyday words; note the term | `?term` |
| Laughing it off, embarrassed smile | Embarrassment (e.g. not investing, not understanding) | Normalise: "Many people tell us the same." | `emb` |
| Crossed arms, short tone, looking at phone | Wants to leave / guarded | Skip to the close question | `guarded` + time |

Interpret carefully: a cue is a hint, not a fact. Write what you saw ("sighed when talking about fees"), not what you think it means.

### 6. Managing our own biases

We are bank employees testing our own hypotheses. That creates strong bias.

- **Do not defend or explain** the bank or any product, even if they are wrong about it. Say "Thank you, that's useful" and note it.
- **Do not correct misunderstandings** during the interview (e.g. they think fees are higher than they are). Their belief is the insight: write "believes fees are …".
- **No judgement**, positive or negative: avoid "Great!", "Really?", "That's not a good idea". Use neutral "I see".
- **No solutions:** do not mention robo-advice, goal-check apps or any idea we have.
- **Stay curious** in interview 9 even if you heard it 8 times. Ask "In your case, how did it happen?"
- **Watch for confirmation bias** when coding: the note-taker and lead code Y/N/? separately first for at least some interviews, then compare.
- **Do not "hunt" for H1–H3 agreement:** a "No" (N) is a valuable result.

### 7. Time management

**Street (5–7 min)**

| Minute | Part | Priority if short |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Approach, consent, screening | Must |
| 1–2.5 | H1 personalisation | Must (priority hypothesis) |
| 2.5–4 | H3 digital vs human | Must (priority hypothesis) |
| 4–5 | H2 knowledge and confidence | Should |
| 5–6 | Trust and value | Could |
| 6–7 | Close: "one thing your bank could do better" | Must (one line) |

- H1 and H3 are our priority customer hypotheses (⭐ in [../02-empathize.md](../02-empathize.md)), so ask them first. **This order is a suggestion**: 02-empathize.md lists H2 before H3; the team decides.
- **Running out of time:** ask one question per remaining block: "What is hard when deciding about savings?" (H2), then the close. Code skipped blocks "?".
- **Finishing early / person enjoys talking:** ask the "What would make you trust your bank's advice more?" question and probe the stage where they got stuck. Keep total under about 10 minutes unless they clearly want to continue.
- **Transitions:** "Thanks. A different angle now…", "Now a bit about apps and people…".
- The third team member (if any) gives a discreet time signal at minute 5.

**Expert (30 min):** follow the block timings in [../02-empathize.md](../02-empathize.md) section 3 (5 / 7 / 7 / 7 / 4). If short of time, protect **block 4 (H4)** and **block 2 (H1)**; block 1 facts (segment thresholds) can be asked by email or Teams later. If early, go back to parked topics and ask for data or documents we may see.

### 8. Active listening techniques

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Paraphrasing | "So what I'm hearing is: the banker showed you funds, but didn't ask about your plans?" |
| Mirroring (repeat last words as a question) | They: "…and then I just gave up." Us: "You gave up?" |
| Summarising (end of a block) | "So: app for payments, but for investing you'd want to talk to someone first. Is that right?" |
| Minimal encouragers | "Mm-hmm", "I see", "Go on", a nod |
| Labelling emotion | "That sounds like it worried you." |

Use paraphrasing to check, not to steer: if your summary adds a word they did not say ("not personalised"), you are leading.

### 9. Handling sensitive topics

**Money and financial struggles**
- Never ask amounts, income, balance, debts, or account details. If they offer amounts, do not write them down: write [amount].
- Use neutral words: "savings beyond everyday needs" rather than "wealth" or "rich". Many people in the target group do not feel affluent.
- Normalise: "Many people keep money in their account; we want to understand why." Keeping cash can be a sensible choice: never imply it is wrong.
- If someone mentions financial difficulty, show empathy, do not probe, and move on or close: "Thank you for sharing that. We'll skip that part."

**Embarrassment about knowledge or technology**
- "There are no wrong answers; many people find this confusing." Never test their knowledge ("Do you know what an ETF is?").
- If they say "I'm not good with apps", ask what they do instead, not why.

**Privacy concerns**
- Repeat: anonymous, no names, no amounts, no recording, they can stop. Show the note sheet if they want.
- If they still feel uneasy, end politely. Do not insist.

**Complaints about employees or service (ours or others')**
- Thank them, do not defend, do not ask for the employee's name or branch. Write the pattern, not the person.
- If they have a real complaint they want resolved, point them to the bank's official complaint channel. **This requires verification:** the correct RBHU complaint channel to mention (have it ready).

**Creating a safe space**
- Quiet spot, eye level, one talker, no badges in their face, calm tone.
- "You can skip any question."
- Expert: "We'll keep this anonymous as 'Expert 1'; feel free to be critical."

**Vulnerable people:** if someone seems confused, very elderly and unsure, distressed, or under pressure, keep it very short or end kindly. Do not include data if you doubt they understood the consent.

### 10. Closing strongly

**Street (30 seconds)**
1. One-line summary: "So the main thing for you is … Did I get that right?"
2. Close question: "If your bank could do one thing better for your savings, what would it be?"
3. "Is there anything I should have asked?" (only if time allows)
4. Thank you: "Thank you very much, this really helps us."
5. Next steps: none. We do not collect contact data, so do not promise to get back to them.
6. Note age band, in segment Y/N, current bank only if offered (per 02-empathize.md).

**Expert (4 minutes)**
1. Summarise 2–3 key points and check.
2. "What haven't we asked that we should have asked?"
3. "Who should we talk to next? Is there any journey or data we could see?"
4. Ask permission to follow up by Teams with clarifications, and offer a short summary of our findings after 04.11.2026.
5. Thank them specifically ("Your point about … was very useful").

### 11. Immediately after (within 15 minutes)

**Street (3–5 min between interviews)**
- Lead and note-taker step aside and complete the live sheet ([2.2.1](2.2.1-note-taking-template.md) T1): fill gaps, mark `!` quotes.
- Code H1–H3 Y/N/? together; in doubt, "?".
- Update the recruitment matrix (which groups are missing?).
- After each round of ~3–6, complete the detailed sheet T2 for each and type into the tracker.

**Expert (15 min)**
- Debrief with all note-takers: top 3 takeaways, facts vs opinions, numbers to verify, H4 code.
- Merge notes into T3; if recorded, note the timestamps of the key quotes, then delete the recording once notes are complete (as promised).
- Note quality concerns: interruptions, Teams problems, time cut.

### 12. Red flags and quality checks

| Red flag | What it means | How to flag |
|---|---|---|
| Guarded or seemed to say "safe" answers | Low reliability | Quality = low; note why |
| We asked a leading question and they agreed | Answer not reliable | Code that hypothesis "?" not "Y" |
| Turned out not in segment, or a bank insider | Should not count | In segment = N or "insider"; exclude from H1–H3 |
| Repeatedly interrupted (phone, companion, noise) | Partial data | Quality = medium/low; mark skipped blocks "?" |
| Language difficulties, translated on the fly | Meaning may be lost | Note "translated live"; quality medium |
| Answered about "people in general", not themselves | Opinion, not experience | Note "general view"; weaker evidence |
| Expert gives numbers without source | Unverified | "This requires verification" in the number table |
| Expert answers for customers (H1–H3) | Second-hand | Notes only, not Y/N for customer hypotheses |
| A colleague answered as a customer | Proxy | Type = Proxy, never counted |
| We mentioned a solution idea | Contaminated | Note it; consider excluding that part |

**Quality checklist (self-assessment after each interview, 1 minute)**

- [ ] Consent was clear and noted
- [ ] Screening done; in segment Y/N noted
- [ ] No names, amounts or account details written
- [ ] We asked for stories ("the last time…"), not opinions
- [ ] No leading or compound questions (or flagged where we did)
- [ ] We talked less than about 20% of the time
- [ ] We did not defend the bank, correct them, or mention solutions
- [ ] At least one exact quote captured
- [ ] Each hypothesis coded Y / N / ? with a reason; skipped = "?"
- [ ] Quality rated high / medium / low
- [ ] Recruitment matrix updated

### 13. Common interviewer mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Talking too much | Less of their story; we fill it with our view | 20% us, 80% them; short questions |
| Jumping to solutions | Biases answers; this is Empathize, not Ideate | Park ideas in a separate "ideas" list after the interview |
| Compound questions ("How do you get advice and do you trust it and would you use an app?") | They answer only the last part | One question at a time |
| Interrupting stories | We lose the key detail and the feeling | Wait for a pause; note the follow-up |
| Rushing because of time | Shallow answers, all "?" | Ask fewer questions well; use priorities in section 7 |
| Leading with the hypothesis ("We think advice is not personal enough…") | They agree to be polite | Ask about their last experience |
| Explaining what "mass affluent" or "Premium" means | Jargon, makes them feel tested | Use everyday words: "savings beyond everyday needs" |
| Approaching people in a group of three | Feels like pressure | One talks, one notes, third stays back |
| Asking about amounts "just roughly" | Breaks our rule and trust | Never |

### Do's and don'ts (one-page summary)

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Smile, introduce honestly as RBI employees, say "5 minutes, anonymous" | Pretend to be independent, or hide the bank |
| Accept "no" immediately | Follow or persuade |
| Screen first, note in segment Y/N | Interview first and decide the segment later from memory |
| Ask "Tell me about the last time…" | Ask "Would you…?" or "Do you like…?" |
| Use their words in follow-ups | Introduce our jargon (personalisation, hybrid, mass affluent) |
| Allow short silences | Fill every pause |
| Probe 1–2 times on the street, 3–4 times with the expert | Keep asking "why" until they feel uncomfortable |
| Note facts, opinions and our interpretation separately | Mix them |
| Thank them and leave on time | Overrun because "one more question" |
| Code "?" when unclear | Code "Y" because it fits our hypothesis |
| Point real complaints to the official channel | Promise fixes, offers or advice |
| Keep notes anonymous and in RBI-approved storage | Write names, amounts, accounts, or take photos |

| Problem | Quick fix |
|---|---|
| Nobody stops | Change place/time; shorter opener; go to cafés and parks, not busy streets |
| They speak only Hungarian and you don't | Hand over to the Hungarian speaker; if none, thank them politely |

| They get angry at the bank | Acknowledge, don't defend, ask "what would good look like?" |

| Expert gives numbers | Write them in the number table with "This requires verification" |

## What this means for our interviews

- **Street interviews are about warmth and speed:** honest one-line intro, screening, then H1 and H3 first (priority hypotheses), H2 if time, the close question always. Keep it to 5–7 minutes.
- **Never ask about amounts, never give advice, never mention solution ideas or defend the bank.** A "No" is a useful result.
- **Use story questions and neutral words** ("savings beyond everyday needs", not "wealth" or "mass affluent"), and have a native Hungarian speaker translate and lead where possible.
- **For the expert, protect the H4 and H1 blocks**, separate facts from opinions, and mark every number "This requires verification".
- **Run the 1-minute quality checklist after every interview** and code "?" whenever we led, skipped or were unsure.
