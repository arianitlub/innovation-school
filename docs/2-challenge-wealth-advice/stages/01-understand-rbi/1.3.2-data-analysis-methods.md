## Output

**Read this first.** This week our data is mostly qualitative: desk research, one transcript and about 11 planned interviews. Sections 1–6 describe how to analyse internal data **if** we get it later. Section 7 (qualitative analysis, including scoring against our pass marks) is what we will actually use in Budapest. All example numbers are **illustrative, not real data**.

### 1. Trend analysis over time

*Use when:* we receive monthly figures such as investment uptake, journey completion or complaints about advice.

**Steps**
1. Put one row per month, one column per metric (for example, new investors per month in the segment).
2. Plot a line chart. Add a 3-month moving average to smooth noise.
3. Compare the same month year on year to remove seasonality (bonus months, year-end, tax deadlines).
4. Mark known events on the chart: robo-pilot launch, app releases, market falls, campaigns.
5. Flag points outside the normal range as anomalies and ask the data owner what happened.

**Excel formulas**
- 3-month moving average: `=AVERAGE(B2:B4)` dragged down.
- Month-on-month change: `=(B3-B2)/B2`.
- Year-on-year change: `=(B14-B2)/B2`.
- Trend slope per month: `=SLOPE(B2:B13, A2:A13)` (A = month number 1–12).
- Normal range: mean `=AVERAGE(B2:B13)`, spread `=STDEV.S(B2:B13)`; flag with `=IF(ABS(B2-$M$1)>2*$M$2,"check","")`.

**Significant or normal variation?** With 12–24 monthly points, use a simple rule: a change bigger than two standard deviations, or 6+ months in a row on one side of the average, is worth investigating. For before/after comparisons (for example, before and after the robo pilot) compare the two averages and check the difference holds across several months, not one.

**Visualise:** line chart with moving average and event markers.
**Look for:** a drop after a process change; uptake rising only in campaign months (push, not need); market falls followed by selling.
**Pitfalls:** too few months; tracking changes that look like behaviour changes; reading one month as a trend.

### 2. Segmentation analysis

**Dimensions relevant to us**

| Dimension | Example groups | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Service tier | Mass, mass affluent (not Premium), Premium, Premium "investment" sub-group | The expert described two very different Premium sub-groups |
| Market | The chosen network bank (and, for comparison, a more digital market such as Czech Republic) | Offers and maturity differ |
| Age / life stage | Under 35, 35–54, 55+ | Horizon and channel preference differ |
| Channel behaviour | App-only, branch-only, mixed | Tests H3 |
| Investment status | Never invested, invested once, regular investor | Shows where support stops |
| Entry point | App, branch, campaign, banker invitation | Which entry converts |

**Steps**
1. Choose one outcome (for example, share who invested after starting the journey).
2. Build a pivot table: rows = segment, values = count and rate.
3. Compare each segment's rate with the overall rate. Also show segment size, because a bad rate in a tiny group matters less.
4. Most affected = big group × big gap.

**Excel formulas**
- Count per segment: `=COUNTIFS(Seg!A:A,"Premium",Seg!C:C,"Invested")`.
- Rate: `=COUNTIFS(...,"Invested")/COUNTIFS(Seg!A:A,"Premium")`.
- Gap to overall: `=E2-$E$10`.
- "Lost customers" in a segment: `=size*(overall_rate-segment_rate)` (only if positive).

**Visualise:** bar chart of rates by segment with segment size as labels; heatmap of segment × journey step.
**Look for:** mass-affluent customers who are not Premium and fall between offers (the expert said Premium "currently does not have the capacity for all those people").
**Pitfalls:** different segment definitions per market; groups too small to compare (under about 30 people); confusing customer counts with account counts.

### 3. Funnel / process flow analysis

**Map the flow** using the journey in [01-understand.md](../01-understand.md) section 3:
Trigger → Information → App or branch → Questionnaire (suitability) → Proposal → Decision and signing → Funding → Review / follow-up.

**Steps**
1. Get the number of customers reaching each step in one period (e.g. last 6 months).
2. Step conversion = this step ÷ previous step. Overall conversion = this step ÷ first step.
3. The biggest step loss is the first bottleneck candidate.
4. Add time between steps (median days). Long waits are a second type of bottleneck (the expert described "come back in a week" after a paper questionnaire).
5. Split the funnel by channel (app vs branch vs mixed) and by segment.

**Metrics that indicate problems:** step completion rate, median time between steps, abandonment at a specific question, number of help contacts, number of visits needed.

**Excel formulas**
- Step conversion: `=C3/C2`.
- Drop-off: `=1-C3/C2`.
- Lost at step: `=C2-C3`.
- Median days between steps: `=MEDIAN(D2:D500)` on a column of day differences (only if row-level aggregated data is allowed; otherwise ask the owner for the median).
- Highlight worst step: `=INDEX(A2:A8, MATCH(MAX(F2:F8), F2:F8, 0))`.

**Prioritise:** fix where (people lost) × (how close they were to acting) is largest. Losing people after "yes, I want this" (signing, funding) is usually most valuable to fix, because they already decided.

**Visualise:** funnel chart; bar chart of drop-off per step; split funnel app vs branch.
**Pitfalls:** people who pause and return later counted as lost; web and app counted twice; a step that is not tracked looks like a 0% drop.

### 4. Root cause analysis

**Symptom vs cause:** "45% stop at the questionnaire" is a symptom. Causes might be: questions not understood (H2), too long, not seen as relevant (H1), needs a human and none available (H3), or paper process with a return visit (H4).

**Steps**
1. Write the symptom at the head of a fishbone diagram.
2. Add cause branches: Customer (knowledge, trust, time), Process (steps, paperwork), Channel (app, branch, handover), People (advisor time, training), Tools / data (systems, missing info), Rules (MiFID, internal policy).
3. Ask "why?" five times on each likely branch, using interview quotes as evidence.
4. Turn the top 2–3 causes into testable statements and check them with data or interviews.

**Methods**
- **Cohort analysis:** group customers by month they started; compare how many invested within 30/90 days. Shows if a change helped.
- **Before / after:** compare completion before and after the robo-pilot launch (only causal if nothing else changed).
- **Correlation:** e.g., advisor appointments per week vs uptake per branch: `=CORREL(B2:B30, C2:C30)`.
- **Simple regression:** `=LINEST(C2:C30, B2:B30)` or the Excel Analysis ToolPak, only if we have 30+ data points.
- **Interview tagging:** count how many interviewees name each cause (section 7).

**Avoid confusing correlation with causation:** branches with more advisor time may also have richer customers. Ask "what else could explain this?", compare similar groups, and prefer small experiments (Prototype & Test stage) to prove cause.

**Visualise:** fishbone diagram; scatter plot with trend line; before/after bar chart.
**Pitfalls:** stopping at the first cause; taking the expert's view as the customer's view; blaming customers ("they don't understand") instead of the design.

### 5. Performance and quality analysis

**What "good advice" looks like for us:** the customer understands the choice, takes a suitable action (which may be "keep cash" or "repay debt"), and gets continuing support.

| Type | Indicator | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Leading | Share of customers who understood the proposal (post-meeting question) | H2 |
| Leading | Share who felt advice fits their goals (1–5) | H1 |
| Leading | Time to get a human when asked | H3 |
| Leading | Advisor preparation time per session | H4 |
| Lagging | Investment uptake after advice | Outcome |
| Lagging | Share with a review in the last 12 months | Continuing support |
| Lagging | Complaints about advice per 1,000 invested customers | Quality |
| Lagging | Retention and outflows to other providers | Relationship |

**Thresholds:** plot outcome against a driver in bands. Example question: "Above what preparation time does uptake stop improving?" Use `=AVERAGEIFS(Uptake, PrepTime, ">="&20, PrepTime, "<"&30)` per band and look for where the line flattens.

**Link operations to outcomes:** put branch-level operational metrics (session time, appointments per advisor) next to outcomes (uptake, complaints) and use scatter plots and `CORREL`.

**Visualise:** KPI cards with target lines; banded bar chart for thresholds.
**Pitfalls:** measuring only sales (uptake) and calling it quality; ignoring "no investment" as a valid good outcome.

### 6. Comparative and benchmark analysis

**External benchmarks (labelled, not RBI data)**
- FCA 2024, UK: 8.6% of adults received investment or pension advice in 12 months. A reference for "advice reach", UK only.
- EY 2025: 29% of wealth clients plan to change primary provider within 3 years. Intention, global mixed sample, not churn.

**Steps**
1. Put our figure (when we have one) next to the benchmark with source, year, country and definition.
2. Only compare if the definition is close; otherwise show it as context, not a gap.
3. For competitors, build a feature table from public pages: digital advice yes/no, human access, review frequency, fees shown, minimum amount.
4. App store reviews: collect 50–100 recent reviews per investment app; tag themes as in section 7.

**Internal comparisons:** chosen market vs a more digital market (Czech automated advice is public); Premium vs non-Premium mass affluent; before vs after the robo pilot; branch vs branch.

**Visualise:** gap bar chart (us vs benchmark); feature comparison table with ticks; quadrant plot (digital depth × human access) for competitors.
**Pitfalls:** comparing different definitions; using old benchmark numbers (Bain 2019) as current; treating public offers as proof of use.

### 7. Qualitative data analysis (main method this week)

**Sources:** expert transcript (Veljko), 1 new expert interview, about 10 street interviews, later complaints and app reviews.

**Steps**
1. **Prepare notes.** One row per interview in a shared sheet. Columns: ID, type (expert / customer), in segment (Y/N), age band, channel use, key quotes (exact words), notes.
2. **Code against hypotheses.** For each person and each hypothesis mark **Y** (supports), **N** (contradicts), **?** (unclear / not asked). Two team members code independently, then agree.
3. **Open coding.** Tag other themes that appear (e.g., trust, fees, time, family decides, ChatGPT as source). Group similar tags into 4–6 themes on a Miro board (affinity clustering).
4. **Count and quote.** For each theme: how many people, and the best 1–2 quotes.
5. **Separate evidence:** customer said / expert reported / our interpretation.

**Scoring interviews against our pass marks**

Only interviews marked "in segment = Y" count toward customer hypotheses. "?" counts as not supporting. If fewer than 10 in-segment interviews, scale the pass mark and say so.

| Hypothesis | Who counts | Pass mark | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 Personalisation | In-segment customers | 7/10 (70%) | Validated if Y ≥ 70% of in-segment interviews |
| H2 Knowledge / confidence | In-segment customers | 6/10 (60%) | Validated if Y ≥ 60% |
| H3 Hybrid | In-segment customers | 7/10 (70%) | Validated if Y ≥ 70% |
| H4 Advisor data / tools / time | Advisors / experts | "Most" | Validated if Y > 50% of advisors interviewed. With 1–2 experts, mark "signal only" |

**Excel formulas** (sheet `Tracker`: column C = in segment, D = H1, E = H2, F = H3, G = H4)
- In-segment count: `=COUNTIF(C2:C20,"Y")`
- H1 supports: `=COUNTIFS(C2:C20,"Y",D2:D20,"Y")`
- H1 share: `=COUNTIFS(C2:C20,"Y",D2:D20,"Y")/COUNTIF(C2:C20,"Y")`
- H1 result: `=IF(COUNTIF(C2:C20,"Y")<5,"too few interviews",IF(H1_share>=0.7,"Validated","Not validated"))`
- H2 uses `>=0.6`; H3 uses `>=0.7`.
- H4 (advisor rows, column B = type): `=IF(COUNTIFS(B2:B20,"Expert",G2:G20,"Y")>COUNTIF(B2:B20,"Expert")/2,"Supported","Not supported")`, then add "signal only" if fewer than 3 advisors.
- Contradictions worth reading: `=COUNTIFS(C2:C20,"Y",D2:D20,"N")`.
- Theme frequency: `=COUNTIF(H2:H20,"*trust*")` on a tags column.

**Sentiment:** for short street interviews, simple manual tagging (positive / neutral / negative about current advice) is enough. Text tools or Copilot can help cluster complaints later, but only on anonymised text, never customer data.

**Insight format:** "[Group] needs [need] because [reason]. Evidence: [count] of [n], quote." Example structure only.

**Visualise:** stacked bar per hypothesis (Y / N / ?) with the pass-mark line; affinity board; quote wall.
**Pitfalls:** leading questions; counting colleagues as customers (label "proxy evidence"); one loud quote driving the conclusion; 10 interviews treated as proof (they are a first signal); the transcript's speaker labels are unreliable, so do not attribute quotes to named people.

### 8. Prioritisation framework

Combine findings into a list of problems (pain points), then score each from 1 to 3.

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Impact on working goal | Low | Medium | High | 35% |
| People affected | Few | Some | Many | 25% |
| Effort to address | Hard | Medium | Easy | 20% |
| Risk if not addressed (customer harm, retention, compliance) | Low | Medium | High | 20% |
| **Evidence strength** (multiplier) | Assumption = 0.5 | Reported / single source = 0.75 | Several sources = 1.0 | — |

**Excel formula:** `=(B2*0.35+C2*0.25+D2*0.2+E2*0.2)*F2`, then sort descending: `=SORT(A2:G10, 7, -1)`.

The evidence multiplier stops well-scored but unproven problems from rising to the top. Take the top 2–3 into the "How might we" question in Empathize.

**Visualise:** impact × effort 2×2 matrix with bubble size = people affected; ranked bar chart.
**Pitfalls:** scoring alone (score as a team and discuss differences); weighting effort too high and only picking easy things; ignoring compliance risk.

### Worked example: interview scoring + funnel (illustrative, not real data)

**Part A — Hypothesis scoring after Budapest (illustrative, not real data)**

Imagine we ran 12 street interviews. 10 were in segment, 2 outside (excluded).

| Hypothesis | Y | N | ? | In-segment n | Share Y | Pass mark | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 Personalisation | 6 | 2 | 2 | 10 | 60% | 70% | Not validated (close) |
| H2 Knowledge | 8 | 1 | 1 | 10 | 80% | 60% | Validated |
| H3 Hybrid | 7 | 2 | 1 | 10 | 70% | 70% | Validated (just) |
| H4 Advisor tools | 2 of 2 experts | 0 | 0 | 2 | — | most | Supported, signal only |

**Reading it (illustrative):** knowledge and confidence look like a stronger theme than personalisation. H1 is not validated, but 2 "?" answers mean we may not have asked the question well; review the notes before rejecting it. H3 passes only by one person, so it is fragile. H4 has only two experts, so it is a signal, not a finding.

**Part B — Funnel, if the digital team shares figures (illustrative, not real data)**

| Step | Customers (6 months) | Step conversion | Drop-off | Lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opened investment section | 10,000 | — | — | — |
| Started questionnaire | 3,000 | 30% | 70% | 7,000 |
| Finished questionnaire | 1,650 | 55% | 45% | 1,350 |
| Viewed proposal | 1,500 | 91% | 9% | 150 |
| Accepted proposal | 900 | 60% | 40% | 600 |
| Funded investment | 630 | 70% | 30% | 270 |

Formulas: step conversion `=B3/B2`, drop-off `=1-B3/B2`, lost `=B2-B3`, overall `=B7/B2` (6.3% illustrative).

**Reading it (illustrative):** the biggest raw loss is at the start (people browsing). The most useful losses to study are the questionnaire (45% stop) and accepted-but-not-funded (30% stop after saying yes). Combine with Part A: if interviews also show knowledge problems (H2), the questionnaire step is a strong candidate for a "How might we". We would then check with Compliance what can change in the questionnaire and what MiFID requires.

## What this means for our challenge

- **H1 personalisation:** score it strictly (in-segment only, "?" not counted as support). If it fails narrowly, check whether our questions were clear before dropping it.
- **H2 knowledge / confidence:** pair interview scores with the questionnaire step in the funnel. Agreement between the two would be the strongest evidence we can get this week.
- **H3 hybrid channels:** split any funnel by app vs branch vs mixed; in interviews, record the exact moment people want a human.
- **H4 advisor data / tools / time:** with 1–2 experts this is a signal only. Report it as such and ask for a few more advisor conversations later.
- **Practical:** set up the `Tracker` sheet with the formulas above before the street interviews start, so results are counted the same way by everyone.
