## Filled prompt

```text
I need to understand emerging technologies and innovations that could help solve my challenge: "Giving mass-affluent bank customers useful, affordable, personalised financial guidance and wealth-management advice (before, during and after investing), while reducing advisor preparation and paperwork time and connecting digital self-service with human advisors. We are Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), a banking group in Central and Eastern Europe, regulated under EU MiFID II."

Analyze and report on:

1. RELEVANT TECHNOLOGY TRENDS
   - What emerging technologies are being applied to challenges like mine?
   - Which companies (in our industry and beyond) are leading in applying these technologies?
   - What are realistic capabilities vs. hype?
   - What are typical implementation challenges and costs?

2. AUTOMATION & EFFICIENCY INNOVATIONS
   - What processes or tasks related to my challenge are being successfully automated?
   - Which companies have implemented automation effectively in this area?
   - What are the trade-offs and limitations?

3. DATA & ANALYTICS APPLICATIONS
   - How are companies using data analytics to address similar challenges?
   - What types of insights or predictions are proving most valuable?
   - What data infrastructure is typically needed?

4. PROCESS INNOVATION & METHODOLOGY CHANGES
   - What new methodologies or process approaches are showing results?
   - Examples from our industry and other sectors
   - What organizational changes are required?

5. HUMAN-TECHNOLOGY COLLABORATION
   - Where is technology augmenting human work most effectively?
   - Where does human judgment or touch still matter critically?
   - Examples of balanced approaches

6. EMERGING TOOLS & PLATFORMS
   - What new tools, software, or platforms are addressing this type of challenge?
   - What adoption rates and satisfaction levels are they seeing?
   - Cost-benefit considerations
   - Technologies enabling this (in-app guidance, contextual tooltips, etc.)

7. FUTURE OUTLOOK (2-3 years)
   - What emerging developments could transform approaches to this challenge?
   - What should we be preparing for?
   - What's overhyped vs. genuinely impactful?

For each innovation area, provide:
- Current maturity level (experimental, emerging, mainstream)
- Examples from our industry (if any)
- Cross-industry examples to learn from
- Potential impact on solving the challenge
- Implementation complexity (low/medium/high)

CONSTRAINTS:
- Focus on innovations relevant to my specific challenge
- Distinguish between hype and proven solutions
- Flag information that needs verification

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Structured report by technology/innovation category with examples and feasibility assessment. Maximum 1500 words.
```

## Output

### Rules that shape every option (read first)

- **MiFID II / ESMA.** A personal investment recommendation needs a suitability assessment (knowledge and experience, financial situation including ability to bear losses, objectives including risk tolerance) and a suitability report [8][9]. ESMA's AI statement of 30.05.2024 says MiFID II applies fully when AI is used, the management body stays responsible for AI-made decisions, clients must be told when they talk to a chatbot, and staff use of public tools such as ChatGPT must also be controlled [7]. It names hallucination as a known risk [7].
- **EU AI Act.** Transparency rules (telling people they are talking to an AI) apply from 2 August 2026 [10][11]. Credit scoring is listed as high-risk; investment advice is not listed in Annex III [12]. High-risk rules for these areas were moved to 2 December 2027 by the political agreement on simplifying the AI Act [11]. *Whether any specific advice tool counts as high-risk requires verification with RBI Legal/Compliance.*
- **Robo-advice is not the same as generative AI.** Most robo-advisers use fixed, tested rules. ESMA's suitability guidelines already cover testing and supervision of these algorithms [9].

### 1. Relevant technology trends

| Trend | Realistic today | Hype |
|---|---|---|
| Robo / hybrid advice | Rules-based portfolios, goal questionnaires, rebalancing, human access by tier | "Robots replace advisers" |
| Generative AI for advisers | Search of internal research, meeting notes, draft emails | AI giving unsupervised personal recommendations |
| Customer AI assistants | FAQ, account questions, explaining approved content | A chatbot acting as a licensed adviser |
| Agentic AI (AI that completes tasks) | Early pilots for simple service tasks | Autonomous investing for retail clients |

**Leaders.** Morgan Stanley (adviser AI, see section 5) [1][2]; Vanguard, Betterment, Moneyfarm (hybrid tiers) [3][4][5]; DBS (personalised nudges, Gen AI assistants) [13][14]. In Europe: ING Smart Invest (Germany) [15], Finax (Slovakia and others) [16], and **Raiffeisenbank Czech Republic**, which already offers automated advice with employee involvement on request [17].

**Costs and challenges.** Data quality, core-banking/CRM integration, compliance sign-off, model testing, staff training. No public cost figures for a CEE bank were found (*requires verification*).

- **Maturity:** robo/hybrid mainstream; Gen AI for advisers emerging; agentic AI experimental.
- **Industry example:** Raiffeisen CZ automated advice [17]; Vanguard Digital Advisor (advisory fee about USD 15–16 per USD 10,000 per year) [3].
- **Cross-industry:** Khan Academy's Khanmigo AI tutor for guided learning [18].
- **Impact:** high, if it lowers the cost of serving each customer.
- **Complexity:** medium (robo) to high (Gen AI on client data).

### 2. Automation and efficiency

What is being automated successfully:

- **Meeting notes and follow-up.** Morgan Stanley's Debrief (launched 26.06.2024) takes notes with client consent, lists action items, drafts an email the adviser edits and saves a note to Salesforce [1]. One adviser quoted by the company says it saves "about half an hour per meeting" — an anecdote, not a measured average [1].
- **Pre-filling suitability data** from existing bank data, so the customer only confirms. *Specific bank examples require verification.*
- **Digital onboarding and e-signature.** EU Member States must offer an EU Digital Identity Wallet to every citizen by the end of 2026, which can create binding signatures [19]. *Actual availability per CEE market requires verification.*
- **Customer service chat.** Klarna (cross-industry) said in February 2024 its AI assistant handled two-thirds of service chats in the first month, with human agents still available [20]. *Later reports that Klarna brought back more human support require verification* — a useful warning not to over-automate.

**Trade-offs.** AI-drafted suitability reports still need human checking. Automation may make required information easier to give, not remove it [9].

- **Maturity:** emerging (Gen AI notes), mainstream (e-signature).
- **Industry example:** Morgan Stanley Debrief [1].
- **Cross-industry:** AI "ambient scribes" for doctors' notes in healthcare. *Specific results require verification.*
- **Impact:** high for adviser time (H4).
- **Complexity:** medium; needs consent, recording rules and data protection review.

### 3. Data and analytics

- **Next-best-action / nudges.** DBS reports sending 45 million personalised nudges a month to over five million customers to guide investment and planning decisions (2023 article) [13]. This is the company's own figure; the effect on customer outcomes is not public.
- **Life-event signals** (salary change, large inflow, new child) trigger a review offer. An inflow is not automatically money free to invest.
- **Open banking / account aggregation.** PSD2 already allows access to payment-account data with consent. The proposed Financial Data Access regulation (FiDA, proposed June 2023) would extend this to savings, investments and pensions [21]. *Its current legislative status requires verification.*

**Infrastructure needed:** a single customer view, consent management, a data platform with good quality (DBS cites internal platforms ADA and ALAN [13]), and links into the adviser CRM.

- **Maturity:** mainstream at leading banks; emerging in CEE (*requires verification*).
- **Industry example:** DBS [13].
- **Cross-industry:** recommendation engines in retail and streaming (*requires verification*).
- **Impact:** medium–high (H1 personalisation, H4 adviser preparation).
- **Complexity:** high (data quality, GDPR, consent).

### 4. Process innovation and methods

- **Goal-based planning** instead of product selling: the customer names a goal (house, child's education, retirement) and the plan tracks progress. Finax and Vanguard describe their journeys this way [16][4].
- **Tiered hybrid service:** digital by default, human when the customer needs it, paid upgrade for a dedicated adviser (Moneyfarm, Betterment Premium at 0.65% a year) [5][6].
- **Pooled adviser teams** reached by video or phone instead of one branch adviser. Vanguard says a professional calls back within two business days [4].
- **Organisational changes:** new roles (remote advisers, content owners, AI governance) and targets that reward suitable outcomes and reviews, not only sales.

- **Maturity:** mainstream in the US/UK; emerging in CEE.
- **Industry example:** Vanguard, Moneyfarm [4][5].
- **Cross-industry:** telemedicine — first contact digital, specialist by video.
- **Impact:** high (H3 hybrid).
- **Complexity:** medium (process and incentive change more than technology).

### 5. Human–technology collaboration

**Where tech helps most:** finding information fast, preparing meetings, writing summaries, and simple education. Morgan Stanley's AI Assistant answers adviser questions from the bank's own research with links to source documents [2]; the company reports 98% of adviser teams adopted it [1].

**Where humans still matter:** emotional moments (market falls, inheritance, divorce), complex family situations, trust, and accountability for the recommendation, which stays with the firm [7].

**Balanced example:** Morgan Stanley positions AI as freeing time while "the human touch remains fundamental" [1]. JPMorgan has reported an adviser AI "coach" tool. *This requires verification.*

- **Maturity:** emerging. **Impact:** high (H3, H4). **Complexity:** medium.

### 6. Emerging tools and platforms

- **Adviser copilots:** CRM-integrated Gen AI (Salesforce, Microsoft Copilot), specialist meeting-note vendors for advisers (for example Jump [22]). UBS has reported large Microsoft Copilot rollouts. *This requires verification.*
- **Customer assistants:** DBS digibot uses Gen AI in Singapore, Hong Kong and Taiwan, and plans agentic servicing tasks for retail customers in 2027; customers can still reach a human [14]. Erste's George and ING are reported to have AI assistants. *This requires verification.* Revolut offers a robo-advisor in the EU. *This requires verification.*
- **In-app guidance:** contextual tooltips, plain-language explanations of risk scores, "what does this mean for me" cards, short videos.
- **Adoption and satisfaction:** reliable independent numbers were not found. Company claims (98% at Morgan Stanley [1]; over 1 million users in DBS Hong Kong [14]) are self-reported.
- **Cost-benefit:** buying a platform is faster; building fits local rules and languages better. Low-cost robo fees (0.65–0.75% a year at ING Smart Invest, plus fund costs [15]) show the price customers compare against.

- **Maturity:** emerging. **Complexity:** low (education content) to high (AI on client data).

### 7. Future outlook (2–3 years)

**Likely to matter:**

- Gen AI meeting preparation and documentation become standard for advisers.
- AI Act transparency rules (from 2 August 2026) and possible high-risk duties (from 2 December 2027) [11].
- FiDA, if adopted, could let banks see a customer's wider financial picture with consent [21].
- EU Digital Identity Wallet making remote onboarding and signing easier [19].
- Agentic assistants for simple servicing [14].

**Prepare for:** clean data, consent design, AI governance, staff skills, and a clear line between general education and regulated personal recommendation.

**Overhyped:** autonomous AI advisers for retail investors; "hyper-personalisation" without good data; adoption figures without outcome data.

**Genuinely impactful:** saving adviser time, explaining proposals in plain language, and smooth hand-over between app and human.

## What this means for our challenge

- **H4 (advisers lack data, tools, time)** has the strongest proven technology match: meeting notes, summaries and preparation (Morgan Stanley [1][2]). A small prototype of "AI-prepared meeting brief + human-checked summary" fits our challenge and avoids the hardest regulatory questions. Ask advisers in interviews how many minutes they spend on preparation and paperwork.
- **H3 (preference for hybrid digital + human)** matches the market model: digital first, human on request, paid upgrade (Vanguard, Moneyfarm, Betterment [3]–[6]). Raiffeisen CZ already has a version of this [17], so our value may be a better hand-over, not a new robo-adviser.
- **H2 (lack of knowledge and confidence)** can be addressed with low-complexity education content and plain-language explanations. This is outside personal advice rules if it stays general, so it is cheap to test.
- **H1 (advice not personalised enough)** depends on data (nudges, life events, FiDA). It has high potential but is the most complex. Test the customer value first with fictional data before asking for real data access.
- Any customer-facing AI must disclose that it is AI and must not give unsupervised personal recommendations [7][10]. Check with RBI Compliance before going beyond prototypes.
