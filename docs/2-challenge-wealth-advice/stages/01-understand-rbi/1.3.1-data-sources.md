## Filled prompt

```text
I am starting a data analysis project for my innovation challenge:

"Challenge: How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process? Working goal: How can we give mass-affluent customers useful, affordable financial guidance that helps them achieve their goals and strengthens their relationship with the bank? Company: Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI), network banks in CEE."

Context:
- Problem area: service quality, customer retention / share of wallet, advisor efficiency
- What I'm trying to understand: where mass-affluent customers get stuck in the advice journey, how personalised the advice is, whether they want hybrid digital + human advice, and whether advisors lack data, tools or time

I need help identifying what data to collect and where to find it. I'm not sure yet what data exists or what would be most useful.

Help me systematically identify relevant data sources:

   - What would show impact and outcomes?

2. WHERE MIGHT THIS DATA EXIST?

   For each type of data suggested, help me identify:
   - Which internal systems might have it? (CRM, data warehouse, operational systems, etc.)

   - Is it structured data (databases, spreadsheets) or unstructured (documents, emails)?

3. WHAT WOULD BE MOST VALUABLE TO COLLECT FIRST?

   - Which data would give me the most insight into my challenge?
   - What's the "Quick Win" data? (easy to get, high value)
   - What's the "Critical" data? (hard to get but essential)

   Create a priority matrix: Impact (High/Med/Low) × Accessibility (Easy/Med/Hard)

4. WHAT TIME PERIOD AND GRANULARITY DO I NEED?

   Guidance on:

9. EARLY WARNING: COMMON PITFALLS

10. SUCCESS CRITERIA FOR DATA COLLECTION
```

### 3. What would be most valuable to collect first?

**Quick wins (easy, high value)**
- Advice offer per tier in the chosen market: public pages plus one call with segment management.
- Segment definition and rough size: usually already in a segment report.
- Our own expert and street interviews.

**Critical (hard but essential)**
- Drop-off per step in the digital investment journey.
- Robo-advice pilot figures: users, completion, requests for a human.
- Advisor time per session and paperwork share.

**Nice to have**
- Revenue and cost per advice session (needed for the business case, not this week).
- Flows to other investment providers (strong retention evidence but needs analytics work and privacy review).
- App store reviews of competitors.

**Priority matrix: Impact × Accessibility**

| Impact ↓ / Accessibility → | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Advice offer per tier (public + 1 call); segment definition; our interviews | Segment size + share that invests; complaints about advice | Journey drop-off per step; robo-pilot figures; advisor session time |
| **Medium** | Public competitor offers | NPS / survey comments by segment; advisory tool use | Channel-switching data; flows to other providers |
| **Low** | General market statistics | Revenue per invested customer | Full cost per advice session |

Get the top-left cell this week. Ask for the top-right cell now, because it takes longest to arrive.

### 4. What time period and granularity do I need?

- **How far back:** 12 months for journey, complaints and uptake data. This covers seasons (bonus months, year-end) and recent app changes. Robo-pilot: everything since launch (only a few months, per the expert). Retention and flows: 2–3 years if available, because hidden defection builds slowly.
- **Level of detail:** monthly totals by segment and step are enough for us. We do **not** need transaction-level or customer-level data, and should not ask for it.
- **Sample size:** for journey funnels, totals per step are enough. For complaints, read about 50–100 recent ones about investing to find themes. For interviews, about 10 street interviews give a first signal, not proof.
- **Trade-off:** older data shows trends but may describe a journey that no longer exists (for example, before the robo pilot). Prefer the last 6–12 months for "where do they get stuck", and longer periods only for retention.

### 5. What questions should I ask data owners?

**Availability and format**
1. Do you have this data for [market], and for which segment definition?
2. Is it in a report, a dashboard or an export? Can we see an existing report rather than a new extract?
3. Can it be split by segment (mass, mass affluent, Premium)?

**Quality and completeness**
4. How complete is it? Which steps, branches or channels are not tracked?
5. Are definitions the same across systems (for example, "investor", "active customer", "advice session")?
6. Were there changes in tracking or process during the period?

**Privacy and security**
7. Can we get it aggregated and anonymised for a training exercise?
8. Which GDPR or bank-secrecy limits apply? Who must approve?
9. May we put aggregated figures into an AI tool, or not at all?

**Refresh frequency**
10. How often is it updated, and what is the latest available month?

**Current use**
11. Who uses this data today, and what do they conclude from it?
12. What surprised you most in this data? What do you think is missing?

### 6. What data might be missing?

**Probably does not exist yet**
- Why a customer stopped (the system shows *that* they stopped, not *why*).
- Whether the advice felt personal (H1). This is rarely measured.
- Customer knowledge and confidence (H2).
- Real advisor time split (preparation, meeting, paperwork).
- Linked view of one customer across app, branch and call centre.

**What to set up to track later**
- A short question at the end of the digital journey or meeting: "Did this fit your goals?" (1–5).
- Exit question when someone stops the journey ("What stopped you?").
- A one-week time log for a few advisors (voluntary, anonymous).

**Proxies if direct data is missing**

| Missing | Possible proxy |
|---|---|
| Personalisation (H1) | Share of customers given the same model portfolio; complaints mentioning "not suitable" or "not explained" |
| Knowledge / confidence (H2) | Questionnaire drop-off at the knowledge section; number of help contacts before investing |
| Hybrid preference (H3) | Robo-pilot "talk to a person" requests; appointment bookings after app visits |
| Advisor time (H4) | Appointments per advisor per week; length of calendar slots |
| Hidden defection | Outgoing transfers to known brokers or fund platforms (aggregated) |

**External sources to supplement:** see section 8.

**Sequencing**

2. Send now, expect later: journey drop-off, robo-pilot figures, advisor time.
3. Only if the idea goes forward: revenue, costs, flows to other providers.

**Store and organise:** keep notes and aggregated figures in this repo under `docs/2-challenge-wealth-advice/`. Keep interview notes anonymous. Do not store customer-level data, exports with names, or credentials anywhere in the repo or in AI tools.

### 8. What external / competitive data is relevant?

All of these are public. Each needs checking before we quote it, and none is RBI data.

- **Industry reports:** EY 2025 global wealth research (29% of wealth clients plan to change primary provider within 3 years; intention, not churn). Bain on hidden defection (mechanism, older numbers). Large consultancies' wealth and retail-banking reports (needs verification per report).
- **Competitor information:** websites and price lists of other banks and fintech investment apps in the chosen market; annual reports; app store ratings and reviews of their investment apps.
- **RBI public pages:** Serbia Premium package and planning pages; Raiffeisenbank Czech Republic disclosure of automated advice via Raiffeisen investice (one-off, limited to selected instruments). Public, but they show offers, not usage.
- **Market research and surveys:** FCA Financial Lives 2024 (UK: 8.6% of adults received investment or pension advice in 12 months; UK only). Eurobarometer or OECD financial literacy surveys (needs verification for CEE figures).
- **Regulatory / government:** ESMA and national regulator reports on retail investment, MiFID II suitability guidance, the EU Retail Investment Strategy; national statistics on household savings and deposits.
- **Academic studies:** research on financial literacy and robo-advice adoption (search and verify; do not quote AI-suggested titles without finding them).

### 9. Early warning: common pitfalls

- **Data quality:** journey tracking often misses steps or counts the same person twice across web and app. Check totals against another source.
- **Privacy / GDPR:** only aggregated, anonymised data. Personal and financial data needs a purpose and approval. Never put customer-level data into an AI tool. Interview notes stay anonymous, with no amounts or account details.
- **Bank secrecy and MiFID:** suitability questionnaires are sensitive personal data. Ask Compliance before using even summaries.
- **Bias in historical data:** data shows customers who already came to the bank or app. People who never started (the silent group) are missing. Street interviews help here.
- **Survivorship bias in the pilot:** robo-pilot users may be early adopters and more digital than the segment.
- **Missing records:** branch advice time is often not recorded. Paper questionnaires (mentioned by the expert) may not be in any system.
- **Definitions vary:** "mass affluent", "Premium", "investor", "advice" and "active" can mean different things per market and system. Write down the definition next to every number.
- **Mixing evidence types:** do not present the expert's estimates (for example, about 1.5 hours per session), the strategy paper or external surveys as measured RBI facts.
- **Every transfer is not defection:** outflows can be spending or life events.

### 10. Success criteria for data collection

**Enough coverage when we have:**
- A written segment definition and rough size for the chosen market.
- A clear list of today's advice offer per tier.
- At least one behavioural figure (journey drop-off **or** robo-pilot completion), or a confirmed "not available".
- Expert interview done and at least 8–10 customer interviews with screening noted.

**Quality checks:**
- Each number has source, date, period and definition in the source log.
- Totals add up (funnel steps never increase).
- Each finding labelled: confirmed (public / internal), reported (interview), external, or assumption.

**When to stop and analyse:** when new interviews repeat themes already heard, or at the end of Budapest day 1 (13.10.2026) for the Understand readout. Missing internal data is a finding in itself; write it down as an open question and move on. The interviews matter more than waiting for data this week.

## What this means for our challenge

- **H1 personalisation:** no system is likely to measure "felt personal". Our interviews are the main source; complaints mentioning "not explained" or "not suitable" are a useful proxy if CX can share them.
- **H2 knowledge / confidence:** questionnaire drop-off and help contacts before investing are the best internal proxies. Ask the digital team if the knowledge section is tracked as its own step.
- **H3 hybrid channels:** the robo-advice pilot (reported by the expert, app name unclear) is the most valuable single data source. "Talk to a person" requests show when customers want a human.
- **H4 advisor data / tools / time:** session time is rarely recorded. Ask the expert for the branch network contact and consider a voluntary one-week time log later.
- **This week:** agree the market first, then send three requests (segment size + uptake, journey drop-off, robo-pilot figures). Do not wait for them before interviewing.
