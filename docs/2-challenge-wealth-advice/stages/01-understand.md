## 1. What this step is about

Before we think about solutions, we want to agree on **what the problem really is**, **who is involved**, and **what the journey looks like today**. The course warns against jumping to solutions too early. If we skip this step, we risk building something nice that solves the wrong problem.

At the end of this step we should have three things: a clear problem statement, a map of today's journey with its pain points, and a list of the people who are affected.

## 2. Draft problem statement

**Working goal selected by Arianit on 10.10.2026:** How can we give mass-affluent customers useful, affordable financial guidance that helps them achieve their goals and strengthens their relationship with the bank?

The goal does not select an app or assume every customer should invest.

> Some mass-affluent customers may struggle to make an informed financial decision and carry it through, even where investment products or advice are available. We need to identify the specific customers, market and journey stage affected, and learn whether the main difficulty is understanding, getting help, execution or continuing support. This is a research framing, not a validated finding.

**Review on 10.10.2026:** public research documents existing advice and human-support propositions. See [preparation research](../research/02-mass-affluent-customers-bank-services-and-retention.md). Avoid treating absence of an end-to-end journey as a group-wide fact.

**To discuss:** Is this the right focus? Is it too narrow (only investing) or too broad? Should we focus on one group, for example emerging affluent or young Premium customers?

## 3. Today's journey (as we understand it)

This is a first sketch based on reported interview experience, not an audited current journey. Public services differ by market and include automated advice. Check every stage against the selected bank and actual customer experiences.

**1. Trigger.** The customer has some savings and thinks "I should do something with this money". Or the bank sends a generic campaign. *Pain:* the bank rarely contacts the customer at the right moment for the right reason.

**2. Looking for information.** The customer searches online, asks friends, or asks ChatGPT. *Pain:* they don't understand funds, risk or fees, and the bank's own information is not very helpful.

**3. Going to the branch or the app.** The interview reports product presentation with limited staff time for some customers. Some network apps already offer recommendations. *Possible pain:* the customer cannot understand a proposal or get the help needed; validate this in the chosen market.

**4. Questionnaire.** A suitability assessment where investment advice is provided. EU requirements define information to collect; a long paper form is not the only way to deliver it. *Pain:* it feels like paperwork, the customer doesn't see why the questions matter, and it sometimes means coming back another day.

**5. Decision and signing.** Contracts, signatures, separate steps to fund the investment. *Pain:* drop-off between "yes, I want this" and money actually invested.

**6. After the action.** The customer may receive investment statements or other follow-up depending on the service. *Possible pain:* no clear answer to "am I on track for my goal?" or insufficient help when circumstances change. Check the actual review promise and customer experience.

## 4. Who is involved (stakeholders)

| Who | What they want | What frustrates them |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer** (mass affluent) | Make their money work, safely and simply | Doesn't understand products, no time for the branch, no guidance |
| **Branch employee** | Help customers quickly, reach sales targets | No time, paperwork, complex tools |
| **Premium banker** | Serve good customers well | Too many customers, not enough capacity |
| **Product / investment team** | More customers using funds | Low uptake in the mass segment |
| **Compliance** | Advice that follows MiFID/local rules | Processes that cut corners |
| **Digital / app team** | A journey that works end to end | Integration and guidance gaps to investigate |

## 5. Facts we still need

The course says "data brings facts, facts help to orientate". Useful numbers to look for:
- How many customers are in the mass / Premium segment, and how many of them invest?
- How much money do these customers keep in deposits?
- Where do customers drop off in the current digital investment journey?
- How long does an advisory session take in the branch today?
