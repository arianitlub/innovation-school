# 3.3.1 Multi-Criteria Idea Evaluation — mass-affluent wealth advice

RBI AI Prompt Library prompt 3.3.1, run 14.10.2026 with skill innovation-ideate. Scores are AI estimates on a small evidence base (2 experts, 2 street interviews, UX study); the team must re-score and decide.

> **How to read this:** the 12 shortlisted ideas (I-01…I-12) come from [3.2.1](3.2.1-solution-ideation.md) and [3.2.2 SCAMPER](3.2.2-scamper.md). Evidence codes: **C1** (Self-Directed Comparer, street interview), **C2** (Cautious Branch Saver, street interview), **UX** (internal UX study, secondary), **E-HU-1** / **E-RS-1** (internal experts, AI meeting summaries). "Guess" means no direct evidence. RBI capabilities (My Life, RaiConnect, advisory tool, Learn to Invest, RAIA) were reported by the experts and require verification. The Team idea (I-01 Money Guide) is scored like every other idea, with no bonus.

## Filled prompt

```text
I have generated 70 solution ideas for my challenge: "How might we give mass-affluent customers outside full Premium advice clear, affordable guidance that starts from their own situation and is there when they choose to ask for it, so they can make confident investment decisions with their bank?"

Now I need to evaluate and prioritize them.

My ideas include:
70 ideas in 10 categories (technology, process, human support, hybrid, communication, trust, cross-industry, unconventional, quick wins, strategic bets), see 3.2.1 summary table. They were combined with SCAMPER (3.2.2) into a 12-idea shortlist, which is scored in full:

I-01 Team idea — Money Guide: consent-based invitation, 2-minute life profile, AI that explains, one tap to a banker who recommends, fair exit offer; app for young, branch and printed plan for older customers
I-02 Life Profile before products: ask what the money is for and when, before showing options, reused in every channel
I-03 Clear comparer with yearly cost in HUF, including deposits and government bonds
I-04 Fair exit moment: honest cost comparison, one real alternative and a "talk to someone" button when closing
I-05 Tell-it-once hand-over: profile and question passed from app to RaiConnect or branch
I-06 Triage and three help paths: do it myself / ask a question / talk to an expert, routed by need
I-07 Ask an expert, answer by tomorrow: written or short-video answers from a licensed specialist
I-08 Guidance desk for non-Premium: RaiConnect video at the customer's chosen time, guidance-first
I-09 Branch guide with printed plan: same profile and comparer on a tablet, one-page plan to take home
I-10 Opt-in invitation instead of cold calls, with a "you call us" promise
I-11 Bring your family (or your ChatGPT answer): invite a trusted person, or bring outside advice for the bank to explain
I-12 Help ladder with a paid one-off guidance session below the Premium threshold

Context and guardrails:
- Evidence: 2 internal experts (HU, RS), 2 street interviews (C1, C2), 1 internal UX study (secondary). Small n.
- "Make customers invest more" is NOT a success criterion. Customer impact = a confident, suitable decision.
- "Retention impact" = keeping the investment relationship and share of wallet.
- MiFID II: AI may explain; a licensed banker gives the recommendation. EU AI Act: customers must know when they talk to an AI (from 02.08.2026). GDPR: consent for life-profile data.
- No cold calls; must work in app AND branch.

Create a comprehensive evaluation framework:

1. EVALUATION CRITERIA
   A. CUSTOMER IMPACT (1-10)  B. RETENTION IMPACT (1-10)  C. FEASIBILITY / IMPLEMENTATION EASE (1-10)
   D. INNOVATION / DIFFERENTIATION (1-10)  E. STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT (1-10)  F. RISK LEVEL (1-10, 10 = very low risk)
2. SCORING METHOD
   Weights: Customer Impact 30%, Feasibility 25%, Retention Impact 20%, Innovation 15%, Strategic Alignment 5%, Risk 5%
   Formula: (Impact × 0.30) + (Feasibility × 0.25) + (Retention × 0.20) + (Innovation × 0.15) + (Strategy × 0.05) + (Risk × 0.05)
3. EVALUATION PROCESS (matrix, score, calculate, identify top, reality check, re-score)
4. PRIORITIZATION MODELS: Impact vs Effort 2×2, ICE, RICE, MoSCoW
5. PORTFOLIO APPROACH: 40% quick wins (1-3 months), 40% medium-term (6-9 months), 20% long-term strategic (12+ months)
6. RISK ASSESSMENT PER IDEA (top-scoring ideas)
7. VALIDATION CHECKPOINTS
8. DECISION & COMMUNICATION

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Comprehensive prioritization framework including:
- Evaluation criteria definitions with scales
- Scoring template (Excel-compatible table)
- Multiple prioritization models
- Portfolio recommendations
- Top 10 ideas with scores and rationale
- Risk assessment for top ideas
- Implementation roadmap sketch
```

## Output

### 1. Evaluation criteria and scales

We keep the prompt's six criteria. Two are interpreted for our challenge.

| Code | Criterion | Scale | What it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CI** | Customer impact | 1–10 (10 = transformative) | Does the customer reach a **confident, suitable decision** with less pain? How many personas (C1, C2, UX) does it help? Does it hit a moment of truth (first contact, decision, exit)? |
| **RT** | Retention impact | 1–10 (10 = major retention driver) | Does the customer **keep the investment relationship** with the bank (share of wallet) instead of deciding with family, ChatGPT or a cheaper provider? Does it address C1's reasons for leaving (cost, product push, unclear)? |
| **FE** | Feasibility / ease | 1–10 (10 = very easy/quick) | Tech complexity, timeline, people needed, dependency on My Life / RaiConnect / advisory tool, MiFID and Compliance work |
| **IN** | Innovation / differentiation | 1–10 (10 = no one does this) | Compared with the benchmarks in Stage 1 (OTP planner, Finax, Moneyfarm, CZ automated advice, RaiConnect) |
| **ST** | Strategic alignment | 1–10 (10 = perfect fit) | Fits RBI's hybrid direction (self-service + human on request, E-HU-1), reuses group assets, works across markets |
| **RK** | Risk level | 1–10 (**10 = very low risk**) | Regulatory (MiFID, AI Act, GDPR), privacy, reputation, technology, customer adoption |

**Guardrail:** "make customers invest more" is **not** a success criterion. An idea that only raises sales volume does not score high on CI. Assets under management can be a downstream benefit, measured in RT, never the goal.

### 2. Scoring method and weights

**Main weighting (prompt example, set A):** CI 30% · FE 25% · RT 20% · IN 15% · ST 5% · RK 5%

`Total = CI×0.30 + FE×0.25 + RT×0.20 + IN×0.15 + ST×0.05 + RK×0.05`

**Sensitivity check (set B, "risk and fit count more"):** CI 25% · FE 20% · RT 15% · IN 10% · ST 15% · RK 15%

Set B tests whether the ranking holds if a bank committee gives regulatory risk and strategy more weight than the prompt's 5% each. An equal-weight check (all 16.7%) is also shown.

**Process (3A–F):** (A) the matrix below; (B) each team member scores alone first, then we discuss; (C) the formula; (D) top 5; (E) reality check: any score with "guess" as the reason gets discussed first; (F) re-score after more interviews.

### 8. Decision and communication

- **Who decides:** the team. Each member scores the 12 ideas alone (10 minutes), then we compare. Large gaps (≥3 points) are discussed first. Final choice by team vote; Arianit facilitates; coach as a sounding board.
- **Building consensus:** discuss the "guess" scores first; agree on the weights (A or B) before scoring, not after.
- **Communicating:** one slide with the top 5, the 2×2 and the chosen prototype; this file as backup.
- **Ideas not selected:** they stay in the 70-idea pool. We explain the reason in one line (for example "needs adviser capacity we cannot check yet") so they can come back.
