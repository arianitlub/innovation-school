# Mass-affluent wealth advice — research for Budapest

Research date: 10.10.2026. Prepared for Innovation School, 13.10.2026–16.10.2026.

This is a research reference, not a chosen team solution. Read [the preparation briefing](01-start-here-challenge-basics-and-school-preparation.md) first. The [source register](05-sources-and-further-reading.md) provides original material, dates and limits. Public product pages show advertised services; they do not establish customer satisfaction, adoption or profitability. Our internal interview adds reported operational experience, mainly in Serbia.

## 1. What exactly is our challenge?

> “How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process?”

We are investigating how people with meaningful savings or income get help making financial decisions. The challenge includes the experience before, during and after investing. It can include cash reserves, debt, family goals, retirement and protection; investment sales alone do not cover all those needs.

A useful starting question is: **Can the customer understand their choices, make a suitable decision, complete it, and know what to do afterwards?** Whether an app, banker or other service should provide that help is something to investigate.

## 2. Basics: customer segments and service tiers

**Mass affluent is a customer description. Premium is a service proposition.** A mass-affluent person can already be a Premium customer. Another can have the same wealth but use a standard account, hold most assets elsewhere, or prefer self-service. Private banking is a more intensive proposition for wealthier or more complex relationships. These labels are not interchangeable.

There is no single threshold we should import into the team case. EY's 2025 research groups mass-affluent investors at US$250,000–US$1m in assets. That is its survey definition, not an RBI eligibility rule. Its sample spans wealth segments and more than 30 geographies. [EY methodology](https://www.ey.com/en_au/wealth-management-research).

For a regional contrast, Tatra banka advertises Premium service mainly for customers with recurring account income above €2,500 or group-managed assets above €40,000, excluding supplementary pension saving. It offers a shared Premium telephone team, a named branch banker, or a named telephone banker. [Tatra Premium](https://www.tatrabanka.sk/sk/premium-banking/).

**Our inference:** a single global asset band would miss local differences. Agree on one market, its definition, and a behaviour or need: for example, employed Premium customers with regular surplus who have not yet made an investment plan. That is a candidate research group, not the team's agreed target.

### Vocabulary to be comfortable with

| Term | Plain meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Investable assets | Money and investments available to allocate; a home is generally treated separately in segmentation | High income does not mean the customer has a large investable balance |
| Wealth management | Helping organise, grow, protect and eventually transfer wealth | Wider than selecting a fund |
| Financial planning | Connecting present finances with future goals | Includes competing priorities and how much cash is needed |
| Investment advice | A personal recommendation about an investment | Carries responsibilities beyond explaining a product |
| Self-directed investing | The customer chooses investments | Trading access does not itself prove advice was provided |
| Portfolio management | Managing investments under an agreed mandate | Different from a one-off recommendation |
| Fund / ETF | A vehicle holding a collection of investments; an ETF trades on an exchange | Convenient diversification, but still carries risks and costs |
| Investment horizon | When the customer expects to need the money | A long-term investment can be wrong for a near-term goal |
| Risk tolerance | How much uncertainty or loss the person feels comfortable with | Emotional willingness to take risk |
| Capacity for loss | How much loss the person can financially absorb | Different from confidence or willingness |
| Liquidity | How readily money can be accessed | Essential for emergencies and upcoming spending |
| Rebalancing | Adjusting a portfolio towards its intended allocation | Maintaining allocation does not prove a life goal remains achievable |
| AUM | Assets under management | Investment balances managed by the provider; not all bank deposits |
| Share of wallet | The share of the customer's relevant business held with the bank | Can fall while the main account stays open |

For EU investment advice, MiFID II Article 25 requires information on knowledge/experience, financial situation including ability to bear losses, and investment objectives including risk tolerance. It also addresses a suitability statement explaining the recommendation. [ESMA Article 25](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mifid-ii/article-25-assessment-suitability-and). **Being Premium does not make someone a legally classified professional investor:** classification has separate criteria and procedures. [MiFID II Annex II](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mifid-ii/annex-ii).

## 3. What happens today? Separate three kinds of evidence

### What our internal interview reports

Veljko's available English transcript describes limited branch time, product presentation for many mass customers, paperwork, confusing tools and the need for education and assisted digital journeys. An example of roughly 1.5 hours for advice is an estimate from that discussion, not a measured group-wide average. Premium service itself varies, and the transcript mentions an existing robo-advice pilot whose identity is unclear.

Its useful operational question is: **What should the banker spend time on?** The interview argues for explaining why the proposal fits the customer, rather than copying and filing documents. Source: [local transcript](../team-research/Innovation_School_English_Transcript-Veljko.txt), particularly sections C, H–J and U–V.

### What public Raiffeisen material establishes

Serbia's Premium pages advertise a personal banker, video contact and financial planning. The package also includes investment, credit and insurance products, card choices and preferential pricing. Its planning page covers saving/investing, financing, protection and retirement. We should therefore investigate how well those promises reach customers, rather than claim Premium is only a better card. [Premium package](https://www.raiffeisenbank.rs/sr/premium/premium-paket.html), [planning scope](https://www.raiffeisenbank.rs/sr/premium/stednja-i-investiranje/planiranje-licnih-finansija.html).

**A significant finding:** Raiffeisenbank Czech Republic's disclosure, issued 03.11.2025, describes automated advice through Raiffeisen investice, with employee involvement requested through the platform. It states that the advice is non-independent and limited to selected instruments. Its scope is a one-off recommendation without considering or monitoring the entire customer portfolio; annual questionnaire updating is required. The advice service itself is described as free, which does not mean investments have no costs. [Official Czech disclosure, pages 1–2](https://www.rb.cz/attachments/mifid/informace-poskytovani-zavisleho-finporadenstvi.pdf).

This is a concrete public example; **we cannot identify it as the pilot in our transcript**, or infer its user numbers, customer satisfaction or full journey performance.

### What remains a hypothesis

We do not yet know the selected market's real drop-off rates, coverage of advice, service quality, customer reasons for holding cash, or assets moved to competitors. We also do not know whether customers most need first-investment support, ongoing goal planning, better explanations or human access. The earlier strategy paper is available only through our historical summary and is generic rather than verified RBI current-state evidence.

## 4. What does Premium add, and what can still be missing?

The correct comparison is **a standard service versus a Premium proposition versus a private-banking proposition**, while recognising that mass-affluent customers may sit in the first two.

| Service dimension | Standard/self-directed experience to investigate | Public Premium examples | What is still worth checking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access to people | General support or branch appointments; do not assume all advice is unavailable | Personal banker/video in Serbia; several care models in Slovakia | Is help reachable when a decision stalls? Is a substitute available? |
| Planning | Product information or calculators may exist | Serbia describes several planning topics; HSBC Premier advertises qualified advisers and family planning | Does the customer receive an actionable plan, and understand it? |
| Investment help | Product access can exist without a personal recommendation | Premium can add adviser access; automated advice can exist outside traditional adviser models | One-off product suitability, ongoing portfolio support, or whole-household planning? |
| Pricing and perks | Standard terms | Preferential pricing in Serbia; travel and account benefits in Czech EXKLUZIVNÍ | Are benefits meaningful to the customer, and transparent? |
| Continuity | Support can be transactional | A named banker can provide relationship continuity | Are goals and prior discussions carried across app, phone and branch? |
| After the sale | Performance statements can show balances | Depends on the contracted service | Who checks changing goals, contributions and life events? |
| Specialist depth | May require separate referrals | HSBC advertises access to specialists; private banking adds more intensive expertise | Which issues need qualified escalation rather than a general banker? |

This table is a comparison framework, not an audited feature list for every bank. Public evidence does not justify saying every standard customer lacks a service, or that every Premium customer receives it regularly.

HSBC UK Premier advertises family financial planning covering protection, education, retirement, wealth growth and transfer, with fees and eligibility conditions. It recommends from a selected provider range, so “personal advice” does not automatically mean unrestricted independent advice. [HSBC advice](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/premier/advice/).

Serbian private banking advertises goal-based investment advice and wider instrument access, including structured products and domestic/international trading. This illustrates greater service depth, not a reason to offer complex investments to everyone. [Raiffeisen Serbia private banking](https://www.raiffeisenbank.rs/en/private/usluga-privatnog-bankarstva.html).

Czech EXKLUZIVNÍ promotes travel, FX and savings benefits. Its fee-waiver conditions include an asset route or an income/card-use route. These account-package conditions should not be treated as proof of comprehensive ongoing wealth advice. [Current account page](https://www.rb.cz/osobni/ucty/bezne-ucty/exkluzivni-ucet).

**Our design implication:** service access, service scope and service delivery are three separate questions. A premium label alone answers none of them completely.

## 5. How other providers deliver it

| Provider / market | Documented approach | Lesson for our team | What the public source does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tatra banka / Slovakia | Shared Premium team or named banker, with phone and branch options | Human support can be organised in several ways | Cost savings or retention caused by each model |
| Raiffeisenbank / Czech Republic | Automated, limited-scope recommendation and requested employee involvement | Start by understanding and extending existing capabilities | That whole-household planning or monitoring is covered |
| Finax / European markets | Online questionnaire, suggested portfolio, identity/contract steps, funding, automated management and goal-oriented app features | Study the full journey from intention to funded investment | Superior returns, universal availability or every household need being addressed |
| ING Smart Invest / Germany | Digital portfolio management with Scalable Capital, ETF portfolios, monitoring/rebalancing and bank/app visibility | A bank can partner for investment capability | That it is a comprehensive financial-planning service |
| Moneyfarm / UK | Core automated advice/support; Premium consultant team; Private dedicated wealth manager | Make the human-support promise explicit at each service tier | That all human support has the same advisory scope |
| HSBC Premier / UK | Relationship services plus qualified advice and specialist referrals | Broader financial goals can be central to the proposition | How consistently every eligible customer receives these services |

Sources: [Finax journey](https://www.finax.eu/en/how-it-works), [ING services](https://www.ing.de/sparen-anlegen/smartinvest/konditionen/), [Moneyfarm tiers](https://www.moneyfarm.com/uk/our-service/), plus the bank pages above. These are examples for learning, not product recommendations.

Finax describes a sequence from questionnaire and portfolio proposal through online contracting and deposit to automated management. It also documents goal-related app features. Our inference is that funding and monitoring should appear in a journey map, rather than treating “recommendation shown” as the finish line.

ING lists a starting amount of €20 and a management fee tier of 0.75% annually below €50,000 or 0.65% from that amount, plus usual ETF costs. These are checked page terms, not RBI economics. Our inference: small entry amounts are operationally possible, but revenue and servicing cost still matter.

Moneyfarm's page lists Core at £500–£10,000, Premium at £10,000–£100,000, and Private above £100,000. It separates support, a consultant team and a dedicated wealth manager. The boundaries are provider terms; clarify eligibility when comparing an actual customer at a threshold.

## 6. Why losing these customers matters

### Evidence, with the right limits

EY's 2025 global wealth research reports that **29% planned to change their primary wealth provider within three years**. This is stated intention in a mixed wealth-client sample, not realised churn and not an RBI mass-affluent forecast. [EY release](https://www.ey.com/en_au/newsroom/2025/05/new-ey-survey-2025).

Bain described **hidden defection** as taking an additional product from another provider while remaining with the main bank. Its 2019 article identifies investments among the categories exposed. Use this as an established mechanism; its older numerical results are not a current benchmark. [Bain analysis](https://www.bain.com/insights/reimagining-the-digital-branch-of-the-future-lets-get-practical/).

The FCA's UK Financial Lives 2024 survey found **8.6% of adults** had received financial advice on investments, pensions or retirement planning in the prior 12 months. This illustrates limited advice reach; it does not mean the other 91.4% needed advice or that the same rate applies in CEE. [FCA findings](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/more-people-have-bank-accounts-one-ten-have-no-cash-savings).

### Business implications — our analysis

A customer can keep receiving salary into the bank but build wealth elsewhere. The bank then loses potential investment revenue and relevance without recording a closed account. That matters before outright switching happens.

| Risk | What might happen | What we would measure |
|---|---|---|
| Investments move elsewhere | Salary account retained, new portfolio opened with competitor | Customer-reported provider use; net flows; known investment share |
| Deposits leave | Money transfers to another savings/investment provider | Persistent outflows and reasons; distinguish spending and life events |
| Future relationship lost | A growing saver develops trust with another adviser | Engagement and retention over time; documented future needs |
| Trust damaged | Fees surprise, confusing recommendation or unavailable help | Complaints, comprehension, repeat contacts and reasons for leaving |
| Advice unused | Customer qualifies but cannot see its value | Advice awareness, invitations accepted, completed reviews |
| Household relationship missed | Decisions happen with partner or family elsewhere | Household involvement when wanted; referral and consent arrangements |

Do not interpret every transfer as defection, or every withdrawal during a market fall as irrational. The customer may have a legitimate liquidity need or changed objective. Likewise, retention through suitable service can benefit both sides; pressure to keep assets should not override customer interests.

### Illustrative economics, not a forecast

Assume 1,000 customers each have €50,000 in relevant investments. If 10% move all of those balances, €5m leaves. At an **assumed net annual investment-revenue rate of 0.5%**, that is €25,000 annual revenue at risk before changes in costs. Neither 10% nor 0.5% is a measured RBI value. Do not apply this investment formula directly to deposits: deposit contribution needs funding and interest-margin assumptions.

For an improvement, estimate incremental net contribution from retained/new assets, less adviser time, operations, technology and other costs. Show retention and conversion separately, avoid double counting, and test whether the service is useful even when the appropriate outcome is no investment.

## 8. Where AI could help

AI may explain approved material, prepare a draft meeting summary or route a question. A rules-based workflow or a better appointment process may also solve the problem. “Robo-advice” can use controlled algorithms and does not necessarily mean generative AI.

A live personal recommendation needs the appropriate regulated service and accountable process. ESMA's suitability guidelines address clear information, sustainability preferences and testing/supervision of automated algorithms. Reducing friction means making the assessment understandable, not deleting required information. [ESMA guidelines](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-04/ESMA35-43-3172_Guidelines_on_certain_aspects_of_the_MiFID_II_suitability_requirements.pdf).

For our classroom prototypes, use fictional customers and clearly labelled mock recommendations. If a later concept uses real transaction data, determine the permitted use and access with the bank's responsible teams. An account surplus is not automatically disposable money; age or occupation cannot replace an actual understanding of goals and financial circumstances.

## 9. What to establish in Budapest

1. Choose a market and target customer group. Who is eligible for which service today?
2. Observe or describe a real recent journey. Where does the customer's intended outcome fail?
3. Check what the existing investment app and human channel already do.
4. Obtain a few customer accounts alongside banker/expert evidence.
5. Choose one moment to improve, create competing concepts, and test an uncertain assumption.
6. Present customer value, operating implications, evidence and remaining unknowns together.

This is a stronger basis for a pitch than a claim that all mass-affluent customers have no advice and need a new app.
