## Our challenge

> “How are mass affluent clients served with wealth management advice and where are the gaps? How can we find more ways to offer more value to customers during the process?”

In everyday language: **How can we help customers with meaningful savings or income make better financial decisions, complete the actions and receive useful support afterwards, at a cost the bank can sustain?**

The challenge covers the customer experience. We will use Design Thinking to understand people, choose a problem, explore ideas, test something small and explain a business case. A final solution is not expected before Budapest.

## The most important distinction

**Mass affluent describes a customer group; Premium describes the service a bank offers.** They overlap. The question is not automatically “What do Premium customers have that affluent customers do not?” Instead ask: “Which mass-affluent customers have access to which services, and where do those services still leave needs unmet?”

Avoid memorising one global wealth threshold. Our team still needs to agree on a market, its actual segment definition and the people we will investigate.

## What wealth advice is about

A customer may ask: “What should I do with my savings?”, “How much cash should I keep?”, “Can I afford this goal?”, “What happens if I lose money?” or “Am I still on track?”

A fund is a product. Advice is help deciding whether a particular choice fits the person. Planning connects the choice with other priorities. Ongoing support checks what has changed. A trading app, a personal banker and a suitable investment recommendation provide different kinds of value; none automatically covers everything.

Know these three concepts particularly well: **investment horizon** is when money is needed; **risk tolerance** is comfort with uncertainty; **capacity for loss** is the financial ability to absorb a loss. These can point in different directions for the same person.

## What happens today, based on our evidence

Our internal transcript reports limited branch time, paperwork, product-led interactions, confusing tools and customers needing education and assistance. Its operational examples mainly reflect Serbia and must not be presented as measured RBI-wide facts.

Public sources show services already exist:

- **Serbia:** Premium includes a personal banker and video contact; planning material covers several financial needs. [Premium services](https://www.raiffeisenbank.rs/sr/premium.html).
- **Slovakia:** Tatra offers several Premium care models, including a shared team and named bankers. [Care models](https://www.tatrabanka.sk/sk/premium-banking/).
- **Czech Republic:** a Raiffeisen disclosure describes robo-advice with requested employee involvement, but a limited one-off scope. [Advice disclosure](https://www.rb.cz/attachments/mifid/informace-poskytovani-zavisleho-finporadenstvi.pdf).

Therefore, **investigate the gap between available service and the customer's actual experience**. Do not claim RBI has no digital advice, or Premium customers only receive card perks.

## Why the bank should care

A customer can keep the salary account while investing elsewhere. The bank may lose future investment business and the role of trusted adviser before the account closes. Bain calls this kind of additional-product leakage hidden defection. This is a mechanism to investigate, not a measured rate for our team case. [Bain explanation](https://www.bain.com/insights/reimagining-the-digital-branch-of-the-future-lets-get-practical/).

Our research identifies potential loss of investment balances, deposits, future relationship value and trust. We have no internal retention or loss figures. The proposed service must create customer value; retaining money is not a sufficient reason to recommend an investment.

## Three ideas to carry as possibilities

1. **Explain the existing proposal better:** a one-page summary linking the choice to the goal, costs, risks and alternatives.
2. **Make human help easy:** a handover that carries the customer's context so they can continue without starting over.
3. **Support the next decision:** a short health check or goal review that gives useful actions even when no product is sold.

These are starting points for discussion. An app or chatbot may help deliver one, but the evidence should determine the approach.
