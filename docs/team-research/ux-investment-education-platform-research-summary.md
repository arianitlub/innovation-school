# Investment Education Platform: UX Interview Research Summary

## 1. Source and scope

Source: Investment-Education-Platform- UX Test Results (1) (1).pptx.

This document summarizes the research findings reported in the supplied presentation, not raw interview transcripts. Source references below use slide numbers so the Markdown remains usable outside this conversation.

Two evidence sets must be kept separate:
- **Serbian UX study:** eight participants; methodology and results reported on slides 7–19.
- **Cross-country synthesis:** findings from Serbia, Romania, Albania and Hungary, reported on slides 20–46. The total cross-country sample size is not specified in the extracted presentation text.

The newly collected expert interviews and untranscribed audio recordings are not included in this summary. Embedded presentation-template instructions were ignored.

## 2. Research purpose and context

The project explored investment education within mobile banking for customers new to investing, across four markets. The project overview gives November 2024–July 2025 as the overall period; this is not an exact interview fieldwork date. The testing phase is labeled April–July 2025, while the cross-country synthesis is labeled June 2025. [Slides 1–6, 20]

The research sought to understand:
- Which prototype better helps users feel confident about starting to invest.
- Which content users find helpful and engaging.
- What encourages action beyond learning. [Slide 6]

Two prototypes were tested:
- **Prototype A:** flexible learning experience.
- **Prototype B:** structured learning journey, described in the results as the quiz version.

Both included stories, articles, simulators, a glossary, courses and a quiz. [Slide 6]

## 3. Serbian study: methodology and sample

The study used **qualitative interviews with eight participants**, rather than a quantitative survey. [Slide 8]

| Participant segment | Reported count |
|---|---:|
| Trustful Digital Adopter (TDA) | 5 |
| Digital First Financial Novice (DFFN) | 3 |
| Total | 8 |

The presentation describes all participants as having low-to-medium investing confidence and medium-to-high digital literacy. Participants saved regularly but were not yet investing. [Slide 8]

The source does not establish that these eight participants were all mass affluent customers. It does not provide participant-level transcripts, recruitment criteria, interview duration or a complete response dataset in the extracted text.

## 4. Serbian study: prototype preference

Prototype B was the overall preferred option. The presentation reports:
- All five TDA participants preferred Prototype B.
- Two of the three DFFN participants preferred Prototype B; one preferred Prototype A.
- The chart reports 87.5% for Prototype B and 12.5% for Prototype A. [Slide 10]

These results describe this small qualitative sample, not a population-wide preference estimate.

## 5. Serbian study: reported needs and pain points

### 5.1 Understanding investment outcomes

The simulator did not clearly distinguish invested principal from gains and lacked yearly breakdowns. This hindered understanding of the displayed outcomes. [Slide 17]

### 5.2 Understanding available funds

Participants needed more detail about risk levels, returns and withdrawal processes. The presentation also reports a missing overview of available funds. [Slide 17]

### 5.3 Clearer educational content

Text-heavy content was perceived as boring. Some users viewed quiz hints as spam. Interactive simulators and quizzes were reported as popular content types. [Slides 13, 17]

### 5.4 Navigation and discoverability

Banners and progress-tracking features had low visibility. Chat and glossary features often went unnoticed. [Slide 17]

### 5.5 Risk and security explanations

The research reports a common need for clear explanations of investment risks and security. [Slide 13]

## 6. Serbian study: investment readiness

### Reported motivating factors
- Learning that smaller amounts can be sufficient to start investing.
- Interactive and personalized learning experiences.
- Clear explanations of security and risk. [Slide 18]

### Reported barriers
- Lack of financial education.
- Perceived complexity.
- Concerns about risk and security.
- Insufficient explanation of investment processes and the funds offered. [Slide 18]

The presentation discusses readiness and willingness. It does not provide observed investment transactions or measured conversion after testing.

## 7. Cross-country findings

The following findings belong to the four-country synthesis and must not be attributed exclusively to the eight Serbian participants.

### 7.1 Prototype preference

The synthesis lists Prototype B as the most preferred prototype in each of the four countries. It does not provide country-level participant counts or exact preference percentages for the other three markets. [Slide 28]

### 7.2 Content and interaction findings

| Content or feature | Reported cross-country findings |
|---|---|
| Quizzes | Appreciated as personal and interactive; some users did not understand that the quiz was a learning activity rather than a formal test. Hints contained too much text. |
| Courses | Valued for structure; content could be too long, while some advanced users wanted greater depth. |
| Stories | Some were too fast to read or contained too many slides. Visuals did not always match the text, and scrolling requirements were not always clear. |
| Simulator | Mixed feedback: useful for exploring outcomes, but complexity and unclear interfaces were recurring issues. |
| Glossary | Needed when unfamiliar terms arose; the synthesis favored access within the content rather than a separate section. |
| Progress tracking | Confusing formats and unclear progress were reported. Findings were mixed rather than uniformly positive. |

[Slides 27, 29–30, 35–46]

### 7.3 Supporting customer themes

The cross-country synthesis reports themes of control, relevant learning, clear explanations, practical planning and access to human support. These are summarized research themes, not independently verified statements about all customers. [Slides 23–28]

## 9. Recommendations reported by the researchers

These are the presentation authors’ proposed improvements, not direct participant statements or proven outcomes:
- Clearly distinguish invested money from gains and add yearly breakdowns.
- Improve fund information, including risk, returns and withdrawal processes.
- Make educational content more concise and interactive.
- Improve navigation and visibility of important information. [Slide 19]
- Clarify the purpose of quizzes and simplify hints.
- Make glossary explanations available within relevant content.
- Simplify simulators and improve risk labels and mobile usability. [Slides 29–30, 36, 38, 45]

The presentation proposes keeping and improving Prototype B. It places simulator and progress-tracking improvements in a post-MVP category. [Slide 32]

## 10. Evidence limitations and inconsistencies

1. **Sample scope:** eight is the Serbian sample, not a verified four-country total.
2. **Research type:** qualitative interviews and prototype testing, not a representative quantitative survey.
3. **Target segment:** the source does not establish that the participants represent the mass affluent segment.
4. **Source granularity:** this summary uses a presentation; full transcripts and participant-level responses are not available in the extracted text.
5. **Behavior versus intention:** reported willingness to invest is not evidence of completed investment activity.
6. **Mixed findings:** broad statements about quizzes and progress tracking coexist with more critical country-specific feedback. Those differences should be retained rather than reduced to a single universal preference. [Slides 27, 35, 46]
7. **Internal inconsistency:** the stories conclusion describes effectiveness for one audience while also saying that audience prefers fact-based formats; detailed country feedback is mixed. Treat that conclusion cautiously. [Slides 39–40]
8. **No invented totals:** participant counts for Romania, Albania and Hungary are not supplied in the extracted text.

## 11. Implications for the current research: hypotheses only

The following are proposed interpretations to test with the new customer interviews:
- Customers may need explanations at the exact point of uncertainty, rather than more standalone content.
- Customers may want independent exploration combined with accessible human support.
- Unclear risk, withdrawal information or investment outcomes may obstruct an informed decision.
- Personalized learning may be useful, but relevance and clarity may matter more than a particular interface format.

These hypotheses should not be presented as validated mass affluent findings.

## 12. Candidate problem and How Might We question

**Candidate problem:** Customers may struggle to make an informed investment decision when important questions about options, risks and access to their money remain unanswered.

**How Might We question:**

> How might we help customers resolve their investment questions at the moment they need to make an informed decision?

For the current project, “customers” can be narrowed to “mass affluent customers” once the new research establishes the relevant segment and need.
