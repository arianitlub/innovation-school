# Meeting preparation — first AI Practice Session (Individual Homework)

*16.10.2026. Part 1 is the RBI Prompt 6.1.1 Meeting Preparation Canvas (skill `innovation-meeting-prep`): direct feedback, no agenda by design. Part 2 is my own suggested plan, beyond the RBI prompt, for me to change. Goal context: [02-learning-transfer-goal.md](02-learning-transfer-goal.md).*

## Is the RBI skill enough?

No. The skill only pressure-tests my preparation and lists methods. It never designs the session. So: canvas and feedback from the RBI skill (Part 1), design from me with the suggestions in Part 2, and the Goal v3 as the frame. ## Part 1 — Canvas and RBI feedback

### 1. First impulse
- **What do you want to do?** Raise the AI maturity of my team, so that engineers use AI in a repeatable, high-quality way.
- **What created that urge?** Teams use AI mostly ad hoc. Some colleagues already work at a high level, but most do not yet get the full value from reusable skills, workflows and orchestration.

### 2. Big picture
- **What is the underlying problem?** AI maturity and practices differ a lot between people and projects. Know-how stays with a few individuals.
- **What are you trying to achieve?** Move from individual AI use to repeatable AI-assisted engineering on the RBI AI maturity levels (SDLC/PDLC), with advanced users coaching others.
- **What is different afterwards?** People know where they are and what blocks them. They have a buddy, a shared starter kit and one experiment to try.

### 3. Visualize the process
- **Step by step:** Where are we? → What already works? → Try it together → What stops us? → Choose experiments → Owners → Review after 2–4 weeks.
- **Where do you need co-creation?** Real examples, blockers, experiment design, and what should become a shared standard.

### 4. What should the session be about?
- **Diverge or converge?** Diverge first (examples, blockers), then converge (choose).
- **Collaborate or decide?** Collaborate first, decide at the end.
- **Type of meeting:** a hands-on working session, not a presentation.

### 5. Co-creation
- **Goal of the meeting:** a shared picture of where we are, 2–3 experiments (owner, date, success measure) and at least 2 buddy pairs.
- **How?** Advanced users show real work → groups of 3 solve a real ticket with AI → blockers → vote on experiments and pairs.
- **Is everything in place?** Not yet: the official RBI levels, 3 advanced users who agreed to show an example, real tickets, Miro, and a co-facilitator.

### 6. Stakeholders
- **Invite:** my team (about 20 engineers and testers), the advanced AI users as coaches, AI champions. Optional: Architecture and Security/Compliance.
- **Why:** engineers bring reality, advanced users bring practice, leads prioritise, experts remove technical and governance blockers.
- **Inform:** department manager, other project leads, AI/platform owners.

### 7. Invite
- **Invitation:** "Let's get more value from AI together: the people who are far ahead will show how, and we choose a few experiments we can try."
- **Prepare:** one AI success, one pain point, one real use case.
- **Clarify beforehand:** the maturity levels, scope, goal, what we may decide, and that this is not an assessment.

### 8. Flow and coaching approach
- **Important elements:** safe start → real examples → hands-on trying → honest blockers → clear decision → ownership.
- **Atmosphere:** open, practical, curious, no blame. There is no "right" level.
- **How should people feel?** Heard, helped, safe to admit gaps and motivated to try one small thing.
- **Coaching:** advanced users show "what I do", not "what you should do". A champion co-facilitates and challenges me.

### 9. Methods and agenda
- **Timeframe:** 2 hours (matches the homework).
- **Work formats:** individual → groups of 3 → whole team → vote.
- **Methods:** lightning show-and-tell, Troika (hands-on), 1-2-4-All, dot voting, pre-mortem, experiment card.
- **Materials:** Miro or whiteboard, the maturity levels, 3 real tickets, experiment cards.
- **Agenda:** see Part 2.

### 10. Documentation
- **What:** a one-page summary the same day: current picture, blockers, good practices, experiments, buddy pairs, owners, dates.
- **Where:** Confluence or Miro, with actions in Jira.
- **Who gets it:** participants, team leads, department manager, AI/platform stakeholders.

### 11. Action
- **Tracking:** every experiment has one owner, one deadline and one success measure. Every buddy pair has a start date.
- **Follow-up:** review after 2–4 weeks.
- **At the follow-up:** decide for each experiment: continue, improve, scale or stop. Reflect: what worked, what blocked us, what we learned, did maturity improve, what next.

**Strongest element:** one success, one pain point and one real use case per person. It puts the session on real work.

**Risks and questions**
1. **Coaches as a hierarchy.** If the "advanced" people are seen as the experts, the others may feel behind and stay quiet. *Will the advanced people present as "this is what I do" or "this is what you should do"?*
2. **Shared level definitions.** A map only works if all read the levels the same way. *Which official RBI levels do we use, and how do we check a level with an example, not only a self-rating?*
3. **Experiment size vs. level 4.** Experiments of 2–4 weeks may not move a level. *What is one visible change in how a person works after 4 weeks that I would call success?*

**Methods (RBI list, I choose and combine)**

| Method | Produces | When | Time | Group | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spectrogram (line from level 1 to 4) | Visible spread of current maturity | Early | 10 min | 8–20 | People fear standing in front of their lead |
| Lightning show-and-tell (3 min each) | Concrete practices others can copy | Middle | 20 min | 6–20 | Only champions show and the rest feel behind |
| Troika Consulting | Peer coaching on one blocker each | Middle | 20 min | Groups of 3 | Mixed seniority makes people hold back |
| 15% Solutions | The step each person can take alone | Middle to late | 15 min | Any | Everyone writes "wait for the platform team" |
| 1-2-4-All | Ideas from silent thinking to group. Also the reset when energy drops | Any time | 12 min | Any | Prompts too vague |
| Impact/effort matrix | Ranked experiments | Late | 15 min | Any | Effort unclear |
| Pre-mortem | Risks to the chosen experiments | Late | 10–15 min | Any | The group is tired and polite |

## Part 2 — My suggested plan (to change as I like)

### Before the session
1. Get the **official RBI maturity levels** and use them word for word.
2. Talk 1:1 to the 3–4 advanced users: will they coach, how much time, what would they show?
3. Talk 1:1 to 3–4 colleagues who are less advanced: what stops them? (Empathize.)
4. Tell the department manager what the team may try.
5. Send the invitation with the three preparation items. Make clear it is not an assessment and not a presentation.
6. Prepare Miro: a level line, a blocker board, an experiment template (owner, deadline, success measure).

### Session (2 hours, "learn by doing")

| Min | What | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Purpose, rules ("no blame, no right level"), check-in | One-word round |
| 10–30 | Three advanced users show one real example each | Lightning show-and-tell |
| 30–60 | Groups of 3 (one advanced, two others) solve a real ticket with AI | Troika, hands-on |
| 60–70 | Break | |
| 70–90 | What worked, what stopped us | 1-2-4-All |
| 90–105 | Choose experiments and buddy pairs | Dot vote |
| 105–120 | What could go wrong, then owners and dates | Pre-mortem, experiment card |

If energy drops, use 1-2-4-All as a reset. For a 90-minute version, shorten the hands-on block to 20 minutes and drop the pre-mortem.

**Story in one line:** Where are we? → What already works? → Let's try it together → What stops us? → What do we try? → Who owns it? → Did it work?

### After the session
- **Same day:** a one-page summary (current picture, blockers, examples, experiments, owners). Confluence or Miro, actions in Jira.
- **After 2–4 weeks:** review each experiment: continue, improve, scale or stop.
- **For the homework (by 04.11):** write a reflection (what worked, what surprised me, what I would change) and prepare 0.5 hours for the line manager.

### Success for this first session
- Every experiment has an owner and a date.
- At least 2 buddy pairs agreed.
- People said something honest about gaps (watch for this).
- I can describe a visible change to check at the follow-up.
