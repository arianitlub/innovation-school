# PROMPT 2.2.2: Real-Time Interview Facilitation Tips

Use Case: Guidance for conducting effective empathy interviews in real-time

```text
I will be conducting empathy interviews with customers about [your challenge area]. I need practical tips for facilitating these interviews effectively in real-time.

Provide comprehensive facilitation guidance:

1. BUILDING RAPPORT QUICKLY
   - First 2 minutes: how to make them comfortable?
   - Conversation starters that work
   - How to show empathy and active listening
   - Body language tips (video/in-person)
   - Tone and pacing for phone interviews

2. ASKING QUESTIONS EFFECTIVELY
   - How to ask open-ended questions that evoke stories
   - How to avoid leading questions
   - Examples of good vs. bad question phrasing:
     * Good: "Tell me about the last time you [experienced this situation]"
     * Bad: "Do you like [the solution/product]?"
   - When to use silence strategically
   - How to follow interesting threads vs. staying on script

3. PROBING DEEPER - THE "5 WHYS" TECHNIQUE
   - How to ask "why" multiple times without sounding repetitive
   - Alternative ways to probe:
     * "Tell me more about that"
     * "What do you mean by [term they used]?"
     * "Can you give me a specific example?"
     * "How did that make you feel?"
     * "What did you do next?"
   - When to keep probing vs. when to move on

4. HANDLING DIFFICULT INTERVIEW SITUATIONS

   Participant is giving short answers:
   - How to encourage elaboration
   - Questions to revive the conversation
   - When to accept they're just not talkative

   Participant goes off-topic:
   - How to redirect gracefully
   - How to determine if it's actually relevant
   - Phrases to use: "That's interesting, and let me bring us back to..."

   Participant is overly negative/venting:
   - How to empathize without getting stuck
   - How to channel frustration into constructive insights
   - When to acknowledge and move forward

   Participant is saying what they think you want to hear:
   - How to detect this
   - How to reassure them you want honesty
   - How to ask for contrary examples

   Participant doesn't remember/know:
   - How to help them recall
   - When to accept lack of memory and move on
   - Alternative questions to try

5. OBSERVING NON-VERBAL CUES
   - What to watch for:
     * Hesitation (might indicate uncertainty or concern)
     * Lighting up (indicates excitement or strong preference)
     * Frustration (sighing, eye-rolling, tense body language)
     * Confusion (when explaining something)
   - How to probe when you notice these cues
   - How to note these for later

6. MANAGING YOUR OWN BIASES
   - Don't defend or explain your product
   - Don't correct their misunderstandings during interview (note for later)
   - Don't show judgment (positive or negative) to their opinions
   - Don't lead them to your solution
   - Stay genuinely curious even if you've heard similar things before

7. TIME MANAGEMENT DURING INTERVIEW
   - How to pace through script while being flexible
   - What to do if running out of time (what to prioritize)
   - What to do if finishing early (what to explore deeper)
   - Smooth transitions between sections

8. ACTIVE LISTENING TECHNIQUES
   - Paraphrasing to confirm understanding: "So what I'm hearing is..."
   - Mirroring (repeating their last few words as a question)
   - Summarizing to check alignment
   - Using minimal encouragers ("Mm-hmm", "I see", "Go on")

9. HANDLING SENSITIVE TOPICS
   - Money/financial struggles
   - Feeling embarrassed about lack of technical skill
   - Privacy concerns
   - Complaints about company employees or service
   - How to create safe space for honest sharing

10. CLOSING STRONG
    - Summary of key points heard
    - "What haven't I asked that I should have asked?"
    - Thank you with genuine appreciation
    - Next steps and follow-up process
    - Permission to contact again if needed

11. IMMEDIATELY AFTER INTERVIEW (Within 15 min)
    - Debrief with note-taker or team
    - Expand abbreviated notes while fresh
    - Record immediate reflections
    - Note any technical issues or interview quality concerns

12. RED FLAGS & QUALITY CHECKS
    When to be concerned about interview data quality:
    - Participant seemed guarded or dishonest
    - You accidentally led them to answers
    - They didn't have relevant experience after all
    - Interview was repeatedly interrupted
    - How to flag low-confidence data

13. COMMON INTERVIEWER MISTAKES TO AVOID
    - Talking too much (aim for 20% you, 80% them)
    - Jumping to solutions during interview
    - Asking compound questions (multiple questions at once)
    - Interrupting their stories
    - Rushing through due to time pressure
    - Leading with your hypothesis

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Structured facilitation guide with:
- Do's and Don'ts lists
- Example phrases to use
- Troubleshooting guide for common problems
- Quality checklist for self-assessment after each interview
```
