# PROMPT 2.1.2: Interview Guide Development

Use Case: Creating comprehensive interview scripts for empathy interviews

```text
I am preparing empathy interviews about: "[INSERT YOUR CHALLENGE]"

My target interviewees are:
[DESCRIBE YOUR TARGET INTERVIEWEES - Examples:]
- "Current customers who experience this process"
- "Employees who perform this task daily"
- "Users of our system/service"
- "Stakeholders affected by this challenge"
- "Mix of satisfied and dissatisfied users"
- "Various experience levels (new users vs. experienced)"

My top mission-critical hypotheses to validate:
1. [HYPOTHESIS 1 - e.g., "Users abandon step 3 because instructions are unclear"]
2. [HYPOTHESIS 2 - e.g., "Experienced users have created workarounds that bypass the official process"]
3. [HYPOTHESIS 3 - e.g., "Users don't trust the accuracy of system outputs"]
4. [HYPOTHESIS 4]
5. [HYPOTHESIS 5]

Create a comprehensive interview guide:

PART 1: INTRODUCTION (2-3 minutes)
- How should I introduce myself and the purpose?
- How do I build rapport without biasing them?
- How do I get consent for recording/notes?
- How do I set expectations for honesty?

PART 2: BACKGROUND & CONTEXT (5-7 minutes)
- What questions help me understand their role, experience level, and context?
- How do I learn about their familiarity and comfort with this area?
- What background do I need about their current situation?
- Questions should be neutral and open-ended

PART 3: WARM-UP - GENERAL EXPERIENCE (5-10 minutes)
- What questions ease them into discussing this area without leading?
- How do I understand their typical patterns or approach?
- How do I get them comfortable sharing frustrations?
- Sample questions that evoke stories rather than opinions

PART 4: SPECIFIC/DEEP DIVE QUESTIONS (25-35 minutes)
For each of my mission-critical hypotheses, suggest:

- 2-3 open-ended questions that probe this area WITHOUT stating the hypothesis
- Follow-up "why" questions to dig deeper
- Probes to get to emotional responses and real stories
- Questions like "Tell me about the last time you..." to evoke concrete examples
- How to explore alternative explanations

Example structure for each hypothesis:
- Main question (indirect, story-evoking)
- Follow-up probes (why, how did that make you feel, what did you do next)
- Listening checklist (what to pay attention to in their answer)

PART 5: PAIN POINTS EXPLORATION (10-15 minutes)
- How do I systematically uncover all major friction points?
- Questions about moments of frustration, confusion, or giving up
- How to understand workarounds they've created
- How to prioritize which pains matter most to them

PART 6: IDEAL FUTURE STATE (5-10 minutes)
- How do I ask about their ideal solution without pitching my ideas?
- What questions reveal their priorities and values?
- How to understand what would make them enthusiastic or satisfied?
- Questions about experiences from other contexts or industries they wish we would learn from

PART 7: COMPARISONS & EXPECTATIONS (5 minutes)
- What alternatives or other approaches have they tried?
- What do they think about how competitors/others handle this?
- What experiences from other contexts set their expectations?

PART 8: CLOSING (2-3 minutes)
- How do I wrap up and thank them?
- What final open question ensures I didn't miss anything?
- How to ask for permission to follow up?

INTERVIEW PRINCIPLES TO APPLY:
- All questions must be open-ended (not yes/no)
- Questions should evoke specific stories and examples, not general opinions
- Use "Tell me about..." "Walk me through..." "How did you..." not "Do you..." or "Would you..."
- Ask "Why" or "Tell me more about that" at least 3-5 times per topic
- Aim for 80% listening, 20% talking
- Do not introduce bias by describing our solution ideas
- Stay curious and follow interesting threads even if not in the script
- Watch for emotional responses and probe deeper there

PROBES & FOLLOW-UPS TO PREPARE:
- Standard probes for any answer ("Tell me more", "Why is that?", "How did that make you feel?", "What did you do next?", "Can you give me a specific example?")
- Silence (how to use pauses to let them think and elaborate)
- Mirroring (repeating their last few words as a question)

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Complete interview script with:
- All questions written out verbatim
- Suggested probes and follow-ups
- Time allocations for each section
- Reminders about interview principles
- What to listen for in responses
```
