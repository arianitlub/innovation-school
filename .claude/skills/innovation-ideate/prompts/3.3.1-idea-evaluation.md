# PROMPT 3.3.1: Multi-Criteria Idea Evaluation Framework

Use Case: Systematically evaluating ideas against multiple criteria

```text
I have generated [NUMBER - e.g., 50] solution ideas for my challenge: "[INSERT YOUR CHALLENGE]"

Now I need to evaluate and prioritize them.

My ideas include:
[LIST YOUR IDEAS - can be just names/brief descriptions, or paste full list]

Create a comprehensive evaluation framework:

1. EVALUATION CRITERIA

   Define criteria and scoring scales:

   A. CUSTOMER IMPACT
   - How much will this improve customer satisfaction/experience?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = transformative improvement, 1 = minimal impact)
   - Questions to assess:
     * How many customers does this help?
     * How significantly does it reduce their pain?
     * Does it address mission-critical needs?

   B. RETENTION IMPACT
   - How much will this reduce churn or improve loyalty?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = major retention driver, 1 = nice but doesn't affect retention)
   - Questions to assess:
     * Would customers stay because of this?
     * Does it address key reasons for leaving?

   C. FEASIBILITY / IMPLEMENTATION EASE
   - How difficult/costly to implement?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = very easy/quick, 1 = very complex/expensive)
   - Consider:
     * Technical complexity
     * Timeline (weeks vs. years)
     * Required resources
     * Dependency on other systems
     * Regulatory hurdles

   D. INNOVATION / DIFFERENTIATION
   - How unique or innovative is this?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = groundbreaking, no one else does this, 1 = standard practice)
   - Questions:
     * Would this differentiate us from competitors?
     * Is this table stakes or truly innovative?

   E. STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT
   - How well does this align with our company's strategy?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = perfect alignment, 1 = misaligned)
   - Consider:
     * Company priorities
     * Digital transformation goals
     * Brand positioning

   F. RISK LEVEL
   - What are the risks of implementing this?
   - Scale: 1-10 (10 = very low risk, 1 = very high risk)
   - Consider:
     * Regulatory risk
     * Security/privacy risk
     * Reputational risk
     * Technology risk
     * Customer adoption risk

2. SCORING METHOD

   How to apply scores:
   - Should we weight criteria differently? Suggest weights.
   - Example: Customer Impact 30%, Feasibility 25%, Retention Impact 20%, Innovation 15%, Strategic Alignment 5%, Risk 5%

   Calculate total score:
   - Weighted average across all criteria
   - Formula: (Impact × 0.30) + (Feasibility × 0.25) + (Retention × 0.20) + (Innovation × 0.15) + (Strategy × 0.05) + (Risk × 0.05)

3. EVALUATION PROCESS

   Step-by-step process:
   A. Create evaluation matrix (rows = ideas, columns = criteria)
   B. Score each idea against each criterion (team voting or discussion)
   C. Calculate weighted total scores
   D. Identify top-scoring ideas
   E. Discuss and validate scores (reality check)
   F. Re-score if needed based on discussion

4. PRIORITIZATION MODELS

   Apply multiple prioritization frameworks:

   A. IMPACT vs. EFFORT MATRIX
   - Plot ideas on 2×2 grid
   - X-axis: Effort/Feasibility (easy → hard)
   - Y-axis: Impact (low → high)
   - Four quadrants:
     * Quick Wins (high impact, low effort) - DO FIRST
     * Major Projects (high impact, high effort) - PLAN FOR
     * Fill-Ins (low impact, low effort) - CONSIDER LATER
     * Time Sinks (low impact, high effort) - AVOID

   B. ICE SCORE (Impact, Confidence, Ease)
   - Impact: How much impact will this have? (1-10)
   - Confidence: How confident are we in our estimates? (0-100%)
   - Ease: How easy to implement? (1-10)
   - ICE Score = (Impact × Confidence × Ease)
   - Rank ideas by ICE score

   C. RICE SCORE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort)
   - Reach: How many customers affected? (number)
   - Impact: How much improvement per customer? (1-10)
   - Confidence: How sure are we? (0-100%)
   - Effort: Person-months required (number)
   - RICE Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
   - Rank ideas by RICE score

   D. MOSCOW METHOD (Must, Should, Could, Won't)
   - Must Have: Critical for success, non-negotiable
   - Should Have: Important but not critical
   - Could Have: Nice to have if resources allow
   - Won't Have: Out of scope for now
   - Categorize ideas into these buckets

5. PORTFOLIO APPROACH

   Create a balanced portfolio:
   - Mix of quick wins + strategic bets
   - Mix of incremental improvements + breakthrough innovations
   - Mix of customer needs addressed
   - Balance across different personas/segments

   Suggest portfolio composition:
   - 40% Quick Wins (deliver in 1-3 months)
   - 40% Medium-term (deliver in 6-9 months)
   - 20% Long-term Strategic (12+ months)

6. RISK ASSESSMENT PER IDEA

   For top-scoring ideas, assess:
   - What could go wrong?
   - What assumptions are we making?
   - What dependencies exist?
   - What regulatory/compliance issues?
   - What customer adoption risks?
   - How to mitigate each risk?

7. VALIDATION CHECKPOINTS

   Before finalizing, validate:
   - Do top ideas actually address customer pain points from research?
   - Have we considered all personas' needs?
   - Are we balancing innovation with feasibility?
   - Do stakeholders support these ideas?
   - What evidence do we have that these will work?

8. DECISION & COMMUNICATION

   How to decide:
   - Who makes final decision? (leadership, committee, team vote?)
   - How to build consensus?
   - How to communicate decisions to broader team?
   - How to explain why some ideas weren't selected?

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Comprehensive prioritization framework including:
- Evaluation criteria definitions with scales
- Scoring template (Excel-compatible table)
- Multiple prioritization models
- Portfolio recommendations
- Top 10 ideas with scores and rationale
- Risk assessment for top ideas
- Implementation roadmap sketch
```
