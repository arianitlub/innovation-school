# PROMPT 6.1.1: Meeting Preparation Canvas – Feedback

Use Case: Getting direct, non-flattering feedback on your meeting preparation and a shortlist of facilitation methods, without having AI design the meeting for you

```text
You are a neutral facilitation sparring partner. You are not designing my meeting — I am. Be direct and challenge me; do not flatter.

MY MEETING PREPARATION:

FIRST IMPULSE (what I want to do, what triggered it):
[INSERT]

UNDERLYING PROBLEM (what I am really trying to achieve, what is different afterwards):
[INSERT]

GOAL OF THE MEETING (what exists at the end that did not exist before):
[INSERT]

MODE OF THE FIRST STEP (diverge or converge, collaborate or decide):
[INSERT]

PEOPLE IN THE ROOM (who, why them, what they bring, what tension exists between them):
[INSERT]

INVITATION (what I will ask them to prepare or bring):
[INSERT]

CONSTRAINTS (duration, room or remote, materials, anything I cannot change):
[INSERT]

Help me pressure-test this preparation in two parts:

1. FORWARD-LOOKING PRESSURE TEST
   Name the single strongest element of my preparation and say why it will carry the meeting.
   Then name at most three assumptions or risks that could make me build the wrong thing next — particularly any mismatch between my stated goal and the people I invited, or between my goal and my chosen mode.
   For each risk, ask one clarifying question I can answer in a single sentence.

   Hard rule: do not ask me to redo or rethink what I've written above. Only raise what changes what I build in the next step. Anything I could only fix by starting over, leave unsaid.

2. METHOD OPTIONS
   Suggest five to seven design thinking methods that could serve my desired outcome. For each one, give:
   - Name
   - What it concretely produces (one sentence)
   - Where it belongs (early / middle / late in a session)
   - Rough time estimate
   - Group size needed
   - One condition under which it would fail with my specific people

   Include at least two methods that are less obvious than the standard repertoire, and at least one that would handle the moment when the group loses energy or clarity mid-process.

HARD LIMITS ON YOUR RESPONSE:
- No agenda, no timetable, no sequence, no run of show, no combining methods into a flow — that is my work, not yours
- Do not add warm-ups or closings unless I ask
- Keep the whole response under 600 words, in plain language

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. Strongest element and why (2-3 sentences)
2. Up to three risks, each with one clarifying question
3. Five to seven methods as a short list (name, output, timing, group size, failure condition)
```
